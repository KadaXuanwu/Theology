# Description
A comparative study of religious traits across 33 hunter-gatherer societies, coding which beliefs are present in each and reconstructing which came first.

# Shows
- Reported frequencies: animism 100%, afterlife belief 79%, shamanism 79%, ancestor worship 45%, high gods 39%, high gods active in human affairs 15%.
- "High gods" is coded as a single all-powerful creator, so roughly six in ten of the sampled societies had no creator concept at all.
- On the authors' reconstruction animism is the base state and every other trait is layered on later.

# Limits
- Ethnographic coding of 33 recent societies, not a reconstruction of prehistory. These are contemporary or recently documented foragers, all of whom have had some contact with outside cultures.
- The paper itself reports high gods as the worst-fitting trait on its phylogeny and highly changeable, so the 39% is the least stable number in the set.
- "High god" is a coding category. Societies with a vague distant creator figure may be counted either way depending on the ethnographer.
- Matthew Braddock, [Natural Nonbelief in God](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRANNI), argues that what prehistoric humans believed is still an open empirical question.

# Source
Hervey C. Peoples, Pavel Duda and Frank W. Marlowe, [Hunter-Gatherers and the Origins of Religion](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12110-016-9260-0.pdf), *Human Nature* 27 (2016), 261–282.
