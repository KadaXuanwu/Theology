# Description
Two small rolled sheets of silver, found in 1979 in chamber 25 of burial cave 24 at Ketef Hinnom, on the shoulder of the Hinnom Valley in Jerusalem, in an excavation led by Gabriel Barkay. Both were pierced to be worn. Unrolled, each carries a scratched Hebrew inscription that includes wording close to the Priestly Blessing of [Numbers 6:24–26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%206:24-26&version=NIV), and both write the divine name. Held by the Israel Museum, Jerusalem.

# Shows
- The wording of the Priestly Blessing was in circulation in Jerusalem before the Babylonian exile, on the standard dating.
- It is the oldest surviving object carrying text that also stands in the Hebrew Bible.
- The objects are amulets. They quote a formula; they are not copies of a biblical book.

# Limits
- Silver carries no radiocarbon. The date rests on the script and on pottery from a chamber that was reused over generations, so the deposit is not sealed.
- Johannes Renz and Wolfgang Röllig argued the script was too worn to date and that a 3rd or 2nd century BC date could not be excluded. The 2004 re-edition with new imaging is the answer to that, and it is the reading most now follow, but the dispute happened and belongs in the note.
- A blessing formula in use says nothing about when the book of Numbers reached the shape we have.
