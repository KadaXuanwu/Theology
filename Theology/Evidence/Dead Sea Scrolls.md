# Description
About 15,000 fragments from roughly 900 to 1000 separate manuscripts, found between 1947 and 1956 in eleven caves near Qumran on the north west shore of the Dead Sea, with related finds from other Judean Desert sites. Around 200 to 230 of the manuscripts are copies of books of the Hebrew Bible, covering every book except Esther. The copies run from about 250 BC to 68 AD. The earliest biblical fragments are a Samuel manuscript, 4QSam-b, and an Exodus and Leviticus manuscript, 4Q17, both placed around 250 BC. Most of the material is held by the Israel Antiquities Authority, with the display pieces in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem.

# Shows
- Copies of the Hebrew Bible a thousand years older than the medieval manuscripts the printed Hebrew Bible was based on.
- Several textual forms circulating at the same time. Emanuel Tov's classification puts roughly 60 percent close to the later Masoretic text, about 5 percent close to the Samaritan Pentateuch, about 5 percent close to the Hebrew behind the Greek translation, and about 10 percent aligned with none of them.
- A library of non biblical Jewish writing from the same period, which is where [[4Q521 Messianic Apocalypse]] and [[4Q246 Son of God Text]] come from.

# Limits
- Most of the biblical material is fragmentary. [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] is the exception, not the rule, and a book being represented does not mean a particular verse survives.
- Dates rest on handwriting plus radiocarbon. A 2025 study by Mladen Popović and colleagues, combining fresh radiocarbon samples with a trained handwriting model, put a number of manuscripts 50 to 100 years earlier than the standard palaeographic dates. It is recent and not yet absorbed, so any date quoted from it needs saying as such.
- One community's library in one place. It is not a random sample of what Jews elsewhere were reading.
