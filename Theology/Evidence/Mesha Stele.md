# Description
A basalt stele set up by King Mesha of Moab, c. 840 BCE, found at Dhiban in 1868 and now in the Louvre (AO 5066). Line 7 reads "and Israel has perished; it has perished forever."

# Shows
- The line is false as a report. Assyria ended the northern kingdom in 722 BCE, roughly 120 years later.
- The same "destroyed forever" formula appears in a neighbouring kingdom, in the same script family, about a state that carried on. The Egyptian version of it is on the [[Merneptah Stele]].
- Moabite and Hebrew are near-identical languages, and the inscription is a direct contemporary of the biblical monarchy.

# Limits
- It establishes that the convention existed. It does not show which other texts use it.
- The stele was smashed after discovery and is partly reconstructed from a squeeze taken beforehand. Line 7 is not among the heavily disputed readings, but some other lines are.
- It is a victory report, written after the fact. It carries nothing about commands given beforehand.
