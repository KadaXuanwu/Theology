# Description
A basalt stele set up by King Mesha of Moab, c. 840 BCE, found at Dhiban in 1868 and now in the Louvre (AO 5066). Line 7 reads "and Israel has perished; it has perished forever."

Written roughly 120 years before Assyria actually ended the northern kingdom in 722 BCE. The claim is a boast, not a body count.

# Shows
- The same "destroyed forever" formula appears in a neighbouring kingdom, in the same script family, about a state that demonstrably carried on. Supports [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]].
- This is the closer parallel to the biblical wording than [[Merneptah Stele]], because Moabite and Hebrew are near-identical languages and the inscription is a direct contemporary of the biblical monarchy.

# Limits
- Like Merneptah, it establishes the convention exists. It does not prove any particular biblical passage is using it.
- The stele was smashed after discovery and is partly reconstructed from a squeeze taken beforehand. Line 7 is not among the heavily disputed readings, but some other lines are.
- It works on victory reports. It does not touch commands, which is the sharper problem noted in [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]].
