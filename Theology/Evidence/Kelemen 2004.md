# Description
Kelemen reviews her own and others' work showing children default to purpose-based explanations of natural things: rocks are pointy so animals won't sit on them, rain exists so plants can grow. She calls this promiscuous teleology. The paper asks whether this makes children "intuitive theists", meaning whether the bias sets them up to infer a designer, and argues the developmental evidence points that way.

Later work by the same group found the bias is suppressed rather than removed. Professional physical scientists fall back on purpose-based answers when put under time pressure.

# Shows
- The instinct to see purpose behind nature is real and early, not learned from religious teaching. This is a genuine point for [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]] and conceding it is better than fighting it.
- It is also the mechanism behind [[God of the Gaps]]: the reach for an agent happens before any evidence arrives.
- What it does not show is which conclusion the instinct lands on. Purpose is not a creator. See [[Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016]] for what the instinct actually produces at population level.

# Limits
- The samples are heavily Western. Rottman et al. (2017) replicated the bias in China, which helps, but the base is still narrow relative to the size of the claim.
- A belief being natural says nothing about whether it is true. Justin Barrett, who ran the largest project in this field, said so directly.
- "Intuitive theist" is Kelemen's framing and it does more work than the data. Children preferring purpose is not children inferring a single God.

# Source
Deborah Kelemen, [Are Children "Intuitive Theists"? Reasoning About Purpose and Design in Nature](https://www.bu.edu/cdl/files/2013/08/2004_Kelemen_IntuitiveTheist.pdf), *Psychological Science* 15 (2004), 295–301.

Follow-ups: Kelemen, Rottman and Seston, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 142 (2013), on scientists under time pressure. Rottman et al., *Religion, Brain & Behavior* 7 (2017), on the China replication.
