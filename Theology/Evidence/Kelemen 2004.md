# Description
Kelemen reviews her own and others' work showing children default to purpose-based explanations of natural things: rocks are pointy so animals won't sit on them, rain exists so plants can grow. She calls this promiscuous teleology. The paper asks whether this makes children "intuitive theists", meaning whether the bias sets them up to infer a designer, and argues the developmental evidence points that way.

# Shows
- The preference for purpose shows up early in childhood and is not traceable to religious teaching.
- It is suppressed rather than removed. Professional physical scientists fall back on purpose-based answers when put under time pressure.
- What was measured is the preference for purpose-based explanation. Belief in a creator was not measured. For what the same instinct produces at population level, see [[Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016]].

# Limits
- The samples are heavily Western. Rottman and colleagues replicated the bias in China, which helps, but the base is still narrow relative to the size of the claim.[^rottman-2017]
- "Intuitive theist" is Kelemen's framing for the question the paper asks. It is not one of the measurements.
