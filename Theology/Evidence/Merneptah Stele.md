# Description
A granite victory stele of Pharaoh Merneptah, c. 1208 BCE, found at Thebes in 1896 and now in the Egyptian Museum, Cairo (JE 31408). Line 27 reads "Israel is laid waste, its seed is no more." It is the earliest known mention of Israel outside the Bible.

The line is false as a report. Israel is still there afterwards, for centuries.

# Shows
- Total-annihilation wording was normal victory language in the ancient Near East, written by kings whose enemies plainly survived. Supports [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]].
- Separately, and pointing the other way, it places a people called Israel in Canaan by around 1208 BCE.

# Limits
- Egyptian royal propaganda is a genre with its own conventions. Showing that Egypt overstated does not by itself show that Joshua uses the same convention, only that the convention existed in the same region and period.
- The reading of the name as "Israel" rests on the determinative for a people rather than a land, which is standard but has been questioned.
- It says nothing about who Israel was or how they got there, so it does not settle anything in [[Joshua's Conquest Lacks Archaeological Evidence]].
