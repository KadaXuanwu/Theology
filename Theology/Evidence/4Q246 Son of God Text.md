# Description
An Aramaic manuscript of two columns from Cave 4 at Qumran, acquired in 1958, written in an early Herodian hand usually placed in the last third of the 1st century BC. It is not a biblical text. Column ii reads that a figure "will be called son of God, and they will call him son of the Most High", and that his kingdom will be an everlasting kingdom. Formally edited by Émile Puech in Discoveries in the Judaean Desert XXII (1996). [[Joseph Fitzmyer]] published an early study of it.

# Shows
- The titles "son of God" and "son of the Most High" were in Jewish use in Aramaic before the New Testament was written.
- Both titles stand together in [Luke 1:32–35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201:32-35&version=NIV), in the same order, in the annunciation.

# Limits
- Who the figure is stays disputed. Józef Milik read him as a Seleucid king, that is a boast by an enemy; Fitzmyer read him as a coming Jewish ruler; John Collins reads him as the messiah. The text does not settle it, and a negative reading would remove the parallel.
- Two columns of a damaged scroll, and the first column is the harder half.
- Shared vocabulary is not shared meaning. It shows the words were available, not what anyone meant by them.
