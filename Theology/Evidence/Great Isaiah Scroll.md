# Description
A parchment scroll of seventeen sheets sewn together, about 7.3 m long, carrying the complete book of Isaiah in Hebrew across 54 columns. Found in 1947 in Cave 1 at Qumran. Catalogued 1QIsa-a and held in the Shrine of the Book at the Israel Museum, Jerusalem. Frank Moore Cross dated the hand to about 125 BC; radiocarbon tests in 1991 and 1995 returned ranges falling in the 3rd to 1st centuries BC. It carries [Isaiah 7:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%207:14&version=NIV) and [Isaiah 52:13–53:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2052:13-53:12&version=NIV) in full.

# Shows
- The text of Isaiah, including both passages, was fixed in substance more than a century before Jesus was born.
- Compared with the medieval Masoretic Isaiah, the differences are overwhelmingly spelling and word forms rather than content.
- Isaiah 7:14 reads almah here, as the Masoretic text does. The Greek parthenos that Matthew quotes is a translation choice, not a variant in the Hebrew.

# Limits
- One manuscript of one book. It dates the text of Isaiah, nothing else. See [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] for the rest.
- The 2025 radiocarbon and handwriting study in the Dead Sea Scrolls note bears on scroll dates generally and needs checking against this one specifically.
- It says what Isaiah said. It says nothing about who Isaiah meant.
