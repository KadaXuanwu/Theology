# Description
A Greek parchment scroll of the Twelve Minor Prophets, in a revision of the Septuagint pulled back toward the Hebrew, the recension usually called kaige. Fragments were sold by Bedouin in 1952 and more were excavated in 1961 by Yohanan Aharoni's expedition in the Cave of Horror at Nahal Hever in the Judean Desert. Written by two hands. The divine name is written in palaeo Hebrew letters inside the Greek text. Parts of Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah and Zechariah are identified. Catalogued 8HevXIIgr and held in the Rockefeller Museum, Jerusalem. Dominique Barthélemy dated it to the end of the 1st century AD; Colin Roberts preferred a window from 50 BC to 50 AD, which is the range usually quoted.

# Shows
- A Greek text of the Minor Prophets close to the Hebrew was in Jewish hands in Judea around the turn of the era.
- Jewish copyists of Greek scripture did not write the divine name as kyrios, which is what Christian manuscripts do.

# Limits
- The date is a range of about a century, and the two ends belong to different editors. Quoting the earliest end as the date is a choice, not a finding.
- Which verses of Micah survive is the whole question for the Bethlehem passage, and the published fragment lists do not obviously include Micah 5:2. This has to be checked against Emanuel Tov's edition before the node claims the verse.
- It is a revision of a Greek translation, so it is a witness to the Hebrew at one remove.
