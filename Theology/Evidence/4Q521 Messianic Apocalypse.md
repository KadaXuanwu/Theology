# Description
A fragmentary Hebrew manuscript from Cave 4 at Qumran, in a Hasmonean hand placed by its editor Émile Puech in the first quarter of the 1st century BC. It is not a biblical text. Fragment 2, column ii, opens with heaven and earth obeying "his messiah" and goes on: the Lord "will heal the badly wounded and will make the dead live, he will proclaim good news to the poor". The wording draws on [Isaiah 61:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2061:1&version=NIV) and [Psalm 146:7–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%20146:7-8&version=NIV). Published by Puech in *Revue de Qumrân* 15 (1992) and in Discoveries in the Judaean Desert XXV (1998).

# Shows
- Some Jews before Jesus expected an anointed figure whose arrival was marked by healing, raising the dead and good news to the poor.
- That list of signs is close to the answer Jesus gives John's disciples in [Luke 7:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%207:22&version=NIV) and [Matthew 11:4–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2011:4-5&version=NIV).

# Limits
- Who does the healing is grammatically open. The subject of the verbs in line 12 is most naturally God, not the messiah named in line 1, and scholars are split on it.
- The manuscript is damaged and the text is reconstructed in places. See [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] for how much of the corpus that applies to.
- One text from one library. It shows that this expectation existed, not that it was widespread.
