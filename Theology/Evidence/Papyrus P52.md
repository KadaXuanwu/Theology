# Description
A scrap of papyrus about 9 by 6 cm, written on both sides, so it comes from a codex rather than a scroll. The front carries parts of [John 18:31–33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2018:31-33&version=NIV) in Greek, the back parts of [John 18:37–38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2018:37-38&version=NIV). Bought in Egypt in 1920 by Bernard Grenfell and identified in 1934 by Colin Roberts. Held by the John Rylands Library, Manchester, as Greek Papyrus 457. Dated by handwriting alone: Roberts and the Alands put it around 125 AD, Pasquale Orsini and Willy Clarysse at 125 to 175 AD.

# Shows
- A copy of John's gospel was circulating in Egypt within roughly a century of the traditional date of composition.
- Christians were using the codex format very early.

# Limits
- Brent Nongbri showed in 2005 that the letter forms used to argue for an early 2nd century date also occur in manuscripts of the late 2nd and early 3rd centuries, so the window is wider than the popular figure of 125 AD suggests. No dated documentary parallel fixes it.
- Handwriting dating on a scrap this small carries a margin of decades in either direction.
- About 110 legible letters. It confirms the wording of a few lines and nothing more.
