# Description
A fragmentary Hebrew Psalms scroll from the Cave of Letters at Nahal Hever in the Judean Desert, catalogued 5/6HevPs and dated to about 50 to 68 AD. At [Psalm 22:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2022:16&version=NIV), verse 17 in the Hebrew numbering, it writes the disputed word with a final waw rather than the yod of the Masoretic text. The Masoretic reading is ka-ari, "like a lion". The scroll's ka-aru is read by its editors as a verb from the root meaning to dig or bore. Published by Peter Flint in Discoveries in the Judaean Desert XXXVIII (2000).

# Shows
- A Hebrew manuscript from the 1st century AD supports a verbal reading at Psalm 22:16 rather than the noun in the Masoretic text.
- The Greek Septuagint had already read a verb there, oryxan, centuries earlier, so the scroll shows a Hebrew text behind that Greek reading.

# Limits
- One letter on one manuscript. Several scholars who have worked from the published photographs say the ink at that point is too faded for the waw to be certain.
- Even read as a verb, the root means to dig or bore. "Pierced" is a translation choice made downstream.
- The scroll is later than Jesus, so it cannot show what the text read before the crucifixion. The Septuagint is the pre Christian witness here, not this.
