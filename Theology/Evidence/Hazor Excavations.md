# Description
Tel Hazor in Upper Galilee is the largest archaeological site in the southern Levant, with an area of 84 hectares.[^runjajic-2023] It has a tell of about 30 acres, rising about 130 feet above the plain, and a rectangular lower city of about 175 acres to its north.[^yadin-gibson-2007] The lower city was first settled in the mid 18th century BCE, in the [[Middle Bronze Age]] II.[^yadin-gibson-2007] Yadin counted 21 occupation levels on the tell and five in the lower city.[^yadin-gibson-2007]

The British archaeologist John Garstang dug soundings in 1928.[^hazor-reports] He found no Mycenaean pottery and dated the final destruction to about 1400 BCE, the date he gave to Joshua's conquest.[^yadin-gibson-2007] Yigael Yadin of the Hebrew University led four seasons from 1955 to 1958 and a fifth in 1968.[^yadin-gibson-2007] His excavation found Mycenaean and local pottery of the 13th century.[^yadin-gibson-2007] [[Amnon Ben-Tor]] renewed the excavation in 1990, and by 2013 he and [[Sharon Zuckerman]] directed it.[^hazor-2013] An expedition led by Yosef Garfinkel of the Hebrew University re-excavated part of the lower city gate, Area K, in August 2019.[^runjajic-2023] The Hebrew University project is still in the field.[^hazor-site]

The reports are *Hazor I* (1958), *Hazor II* (1960), *Hazor III–IV* (plates 1961, text 1989) and *Hazor V* (1997, the 1968 season).[^hazor-bibliography] The renewed excavations published *Hazor VI* (2012, the [[Iron Age]]) and *Hazor VII* (2017, the Bronze Age).[^hazor-bibliography] Preliminary reports stand on the project's own pages and in *Hadashot Arkheologiyot*.[^hazor-reports][^hazor-2013]

The reports describe fire at four levels. Yadin's entry in the *Encyclopaedia Judaica*, updated by Shimon Gibson, says lower city Stratum 3, from the end of the Middle Bronze Age II, was "razed by fire".[^yadin-gibson-2007] It also reports a destruction layer between the Middle Bronze city and the [[Late Bronze Age|Late Bronze]] city on the tell.[^yadin-gibson-2007] In Area M on the tell, an early Late Bronze phase ended in a conflagration that the 2001 report places in the 15th century.[^hazor-2001] The entry says Stratum XIV on the tell "was destroyed in a fire", at the end of the 14th century.[^yadin-gibson-2007] The last Late Bronze city, Stratum XIII on the tell and Stratum 1A in the lower city, ended in a conflagration in the 13th century.[^yadin-gibson-2007][^hazor-2012]

The excavators describe ash and charred wood in places 3 feet deep across the last city, with vitrified mudbrick and cracked basalt slabs in the palace.[^ben-tor-rubiato-1999] A basalt statue, the largest Canaanite statue of human form found in Israel, lay smashed into nearly a hundred pieces.[^ben-tor-rubiato-1999] Its head and hands, like those of several others, were missing.[^ben-tor-rubiato-1999] Five statues of Egyptian kings had been found by 1998, and the 1997 report says the newest "suffered intentional mutilation in antiquity", like the earlier ones.[^hazor-1997][^hazor-1998] A small fragment of an Egyptian inscription, apparently from a stele or statue, came from Area M in 1999.[^hazor-1999] [[Kenneth Kitchen]] published an Egyptian inscribed fragment from Late Bronze Hazor in 2003.[^kitchen-2003]

Needs sources: Garstang's own report of the 1928 soundings, cited by Zuckerman as Garstang 1931, *Joshua Judges* (London), at p. 198.

# Shows
Hazor's last Canaanite city burned in the 13th century BCE, on the tell and in the lower city.[^yadin-gibson-2007][^hazor-2012] Fire is reported at three earlier levels, and each rests on different dating.

- End of the Middle Bronze Age. A fire destroyed at least one building in Area C at the end of Stratum 3. Yadin read the ash layer as the end of the whole stratum.[^yadin-gibson-2007][^bimson-2025] John J. Bimson, who argues for an Israelite assault at that time, reports a different reading. Shlomit Bechar, a co-director of the current excavations, finds one building burned in Area C, not the stratum and not the lower city.[^bimson-2025][^bechar-2022] Bimson adds that no hiatus of the kind Yadin claimed has been found on the tell.[^bimson-2025] The 2019 gate trench found no destruction between Strata 3 and 2.[^runjajic-2023] The entry gives Stratum 3 as the 17th to 16th centuries and no closer date for its end.[^yadin-gibson-2007]
- Late Bronze I. A fire in Area M is dated to the 15th century by a small pottery group.[^hazor-2001] The 2001 report calls it "most probably" contemporary with the end of Stratum 2 in the lower city. That end "may have been the result of the military campaign led by Thutmosis III".[^hazor-2001] Bechar, as Bimson reports her, reads the Late Bronze I city as a time of decline, with reduced use of space.[^bimson-2025]
- End of the 14th century. Stratum XIV on the tell burned, and it matches Stratum 1B in the lower city.[^zuckerman-2007-dating] The entry says the fire "may have been at the time of Seti I".[^yadin-gibson-2007] Yadin wrote that Stratum 1B "suffered destruction in undetermined circumstances".[^yadin-gibson-2007] A 2023 study of the Area K gate reports that Yadin put a destruction by Seti I at the beginning of the 13th century, in Stratum 1B.[^runjajic-2023] The entry gives no basis for the Seti I link, and the sources place the fire at the end of one century or the start of the next.
- 13th century. The 2012 report says the 13th century dating of the Area M structure's final demise was established.[^hazor-2012] It adds that the structure burned with other public buildings on the tell and throughout the lower city.[^hazor-2012] Within the century, Zuckerman writes that the Mycenaean pottery "seems to support" the first half.[^zuckerman-2007-dating] Yadin gave about 1230 BCE, and Kitchen prefers 1220 BCE or later.[^zuckerman-2007-dating][^petrovich-2008] Bechar and others added radiocarbon dating in 2021.[^bechar-2021]

The mutilation of statues in the last city was deliberate. The 1997 report says so of an Egyptian king's statue.[^hazor-1997] Zuckerman calls the treatment of the cult objects "a systematic annihilation campaign, against the very physical symbols of the royal ideology".[^petrovich-2008] Douglas Petrovich, who argues for a 15th century exodus, quotes her 2007 paper. Kitchen, as Petrovich reports him, notes that the vizier Prahotep set up a monument at Hazor in the decade after years 40 to 45 of Ramesses II.[^petrovich-2008]

# Limits
- Pottery and objects date most of these fires. The Late Bronze I fire rests on a "meagre" pottery group.[^hazor-2001] The 1997 report says a more precise date within the Late Bronze Age awaited further study, and it notes the scarcity of Cypriote and Mycenaean imports.[^hazor-1997] Yadin dated the last fire to about 1230 BCE from late Mycenaean sherds in Stratum 1A.[^zuckerman-2007-dating] Zuckerman writes that the pottery scheme behind that date no longer fits the accepted range, and that Mycenaean pottery alone cannot date the Late Bronze levels.[^zuckerman-2007-dating]
- The abstract of Bechar and others 2021 says the authors "offer two different possible scenarios" for the date and the processes of the destruction. It does not say which they prefer.[^bechar-2021] A 2023 study of the Area K gate reports two readings in that paper. In one, all of Hazor burned in the last stratum, 1A. In the other, the demise came at the end of 1B, with decline and reuse of temples in 1A.[^runjajic-2023] Bimson reports the same two readings and writes that current evidence does not resolve the difficulty.[^bimson-2025] The study's table, adapted from the 2021 paper, gives lower city Stratum 1B as about 1457 or 1456 to 1300 BCE. It gives Stratum 1A as 1250 to 1230 or 1200, while its text puts the demise at the end of 1B.[^runjajic-2023]
- The match between the tell's strata and the lower city's is open. Zuckerman pairs Stratum XIV with 1B and XIII with 1A.[^zuckerman-2007-dating] Bimson reports a reading that pairs Stratum XIII with 1B.[^bimson-2025] [[Israel Finkelstein]] reassessed Yadin's documentation and, with earlier excavators of the area, put the Area K gate's destruction layer in Stratum 1B.[^runjajic-2023] Ben-Tor and Zuckerman's 2008 article says in its abstract that his reading of the Late Bronze strata fits neither Yadin's data nor the renewed excavations.[^ben-tor-zuckerman-2008] The 2019 gate trench found that the gate "had been destroyed only once by fire", with no datable material for the fire.[^runjajic-2023] A fall in 1B would leave the lower city standing unfortified in 1A, and a fall in 1A would mean an abrupt end.[^runjajic-2023]
- The finds do not name who set the last fire. Yadin ascribed it to the Israelite tribes of Joshua.[^yadin-gibson-2007] Ben-Tor held the same reading in 1998, 1999 and 2013.[^rabinovich-silberman-1998][^bas-2012] He rules out the Egyptians because statues at Hazor, those of Egyptian kings in particular, were smashed, and rival Canaanite cities because none nearby was strong enough.[^rabinovich-silberman-1998] He rules out the [[Sea Peoples]] because no sherd of their decorated pottery has been found in the city, as *Archaeology* reports him.[^rabinovich-silberman-1998] Ben-Tor and a co-author name the Israel of the [[Merneptah Stele]] as the likeliest destroyer.[^bas-2012] Kitchen holds, as Petrovich quotes him, that neither the Egyptians, Canaanites nor Sea Peoples destroyed the city and that "the early Hebrews remain a feasible option".[^petrovich-2008] Zuckerman excludes the Sea Peoples, the Egyptians and rival Canaanite cities too.[^zuckerman-2006] She writes that more recent dating "places it too early for the Israelites", and she holds that an internal revolt burned the city.[^zuckerman-2006] The abstract of her 2007 paper stresses internal socio-economic and ideological factors rather than external agents.[^zuckerman-2007-anatomy] The abstract of Bechar and others 2021 names no destroyer.[^bechar-2021]
- No weapons or human victims are reported from the excavated areas, in Zuckerman's words as Petrovich quotes them.[^petrovich-2008] Only part of the site has been excavated. A royal archive might pinpoint the date of the destruction, *Archaeology* wrote in 1998.[^rabinovich-silberman-1998]
- Fragments of 19 Egyptian statues have been found at Hazor, most in the Late Bronze destruction level, as Bimson reports.[^bimson-2025] All but two date to the Middle Kingdom, centuries before the fire.[^bimson-2025] The 2013 report says basalt vessels in the western hall of the Area M structure and some clay vessels "seem to have been intentionally smashed prior to the final destruction".[^hazor-2013]
- Kitchen's fragment dates a monument. It dates the fire only if it lay in the destruction layer, and the BCE years for Prahotep's decade are Petrovich's conversion of regnal years. Kitchen's own earlier point, as Petrovich quotes it, is that Yadin's 1230 date rested on a 1940s pottery scheme that used high Egyptian dates "not usable today".[^petrovich-2008]
- Petrovich and [[Bryant G. Wood]] read the Late Bronze I fire as the destruction of [Joshua 11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011&version=NIV), at about 1400 BCE, and the 13th century fire as that of [Judges 4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%204&version=NIV).[^petrovich-2008][^wood-2005]
- Bimson reads Yadin's ash as a limited, perhaps token, burning in an Israelite assault on the Middle Bronze city, and Bechar's Late Bronze I decline as its result.[^bimson-2025] He quotes Bechar that finding no destruction level in the strata discussed does not imply Hazor did not witness a crisis.[^bimson-2025]

Needs sources: from the full text of Bechar and others 2021, the calibrated ranges, the two scenarios in the authors' words and the preferred scenario. From Zuckerman 2007, the layers and buildings her argument rests on. From Kitchen 2003, the fragment, its find spot and its date. From Hazor VII, the Stratum XIV fire and the Late Bronze I fire in Area M. The share of the 84 hectares excavated. Whether a royal archive of the last city has been found.
