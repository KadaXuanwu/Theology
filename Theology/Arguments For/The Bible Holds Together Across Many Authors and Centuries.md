# Description
The Bible was assembled over centuries by dozens of writers in three languages and on three continents, most of whom never met, and it still reads as one connected story with one moral direction, with the later parts answering the earlier ones. A library that accumulated by accident would not do that. The Old Testament in particular keeps describing a figure the Gospels then claim arrived, on counts that run into the hundreds of passages.

# Based On
- [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] and [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] show the text held its shape across a thousand years of copying, which is the transmission half of the argument.
- [[Ketef Hinnom Silver Scrolls]] push written scripture back to before the exile.
- [[The Bible Only Speaks Truth]] is the internal version of the same claim and is the weaker support, since it assumes what is being asked.
- Needs a claim node for the exactness of Jewish scribal and oral transmission. [[Richard Bauckham]] argues in *Jesus and the Eyewitnesses* that the Gospel tradition ran through named witnesses rather than anonymous drift, which is the New Testament end of it.
- Passage counts: J. Barton Payne's *Encyclopedia of Biblical Prophecy* (1973) is the usual scholarly source, and his own figure for passages with a personal reference to Christ is closer to 190 than to 300.

# Countered By
Needs nodes. Unity is what an edited and curated collection looks like, and the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament were both selected and arranged by communities with a view of how they fit together. The later writers had the earlier ones in front of them, which is the ordinary explanation for a text that answers itself.

# Limits
- The crux: the argument needs the authors to be independent, and many of them demonstrably are not. [Daniel 9:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Daniel%209:2&version=NIV) reads Jeremiah. Chronicles rewrites Samuel and Kings. [Luke 1:1–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201:1-4&version=NIV) says openly that earlier accounts were used. Paul reports meeting Peter and James in [Galatians 1:18–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians%201:18-19&version=NIV). What would settle it is a case of agreement between writers who provably had no access to each other's work.
- The span of 1500 years assumes Moses wrote around 1400 BC. On the dating most scholars use, the oldest written material to the latest New Testament book is nearer a thousand years. It is still centuries, but the figure is a position, not a measurement.
- Counts of messianic passages are not fixed. Estimates range from about 100 to about 400 depending on what is allowed to count, so "over 300" is a choice among those.
- The Dead Sea Scrolls cut both ways here. They show remarkable stability, and they also show several textual forms circulating side by side, with roughly a tenth of the biblical manuscripts matching none of the known traditions. Careful copying is not the same as a single unchanged text.
- Unity of theme is judged by readers inside the tradition. The Jewish canon reads the same Old Testament as a complete story without the Gospels, and the argument has to say why that reading is worse.
