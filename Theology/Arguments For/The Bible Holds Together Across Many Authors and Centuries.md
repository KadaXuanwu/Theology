# Description
The coherence and textual stability of the Bible count in favour of one mind guiding its writing, gathering and copying, over ordinary collection and editing. Its books were written and edited by many hands over several centuries, and later writers read and reworked earlier ones.[^breed-2022][^schniedewind-2010] From the second century Christian writers read the Law and the Prophets as speaking of Christ.[^eusebius-he4][^irenaeus] By the count of the United Bible Societies' Greek New Testament, the New Testament quotes the prophetic books 84 times, and Matthew marks at least ten passages as fulfilled.[^moyise-2022][^viljoen-2007] The core books were in use early, while the edges of the [[Canon|canon]] were settled over centuries and still differ between churches.[^josephus-apion][^irenaeus][^eusebius-he3][^breed-2022] Copies of one line of the Hebrew text, the forerunner of the [[Masoretic Text]], survive from about 50 BC on.[^tov-2017-essence] The line was copied with precision, down to correction dots never meant to be copied.[^tov-2017-essence][^tov-2017-marks] Before the first century AD other text forms circulated beside it.[^tov-2017-essence] If one mind guided the process, a collection whose parts answer one another and a carefully kept text are what one would expect. Without that guidance, many hands over centuries would be unlikely to produce this coherence and this stability.

# Based On
- **The popular form.** [[Josh McDowell]]'s *Evidence That Demands a Verdict* says the Bible was "Written over a 1,500 year span" and "by over 40 authors".[^mcdowell-1979] One heading reads "The Old Testament contains over 300 references to the Messiah that were fulfilled in Jesus".[^mcdowell-1979] Of the continuity list he adds that it "does not prove the Bible is the Word of God".[^mcdowell-1979] Tradition dated the earliest biblical writing to Moses, about 1500 to 1200 BC.[^breed-2022] A span of 1,500 years needs the early end of that range. Many scholars now date the earliest biblical writing to the 8th or 7th century BC.[^breed-2022] Writing and editing ran over about a thousand years.[^breed-2022]
- **Older and scholarly forms.** The Westminster Confession is a confession of faith that the Orthodox Presbyterian Church keeps among its standards.[^westminster-confession] It counts "the consent of all the parts" among the arguments by which Scripture shows itself to be the Word of God. Full persuasion, it adds, comes "from the inward work of the Holy Spirit".[^westminster-confession] [[John Calvin]] wrote that belief is "confirmed" by considering "how beautifully it harmonises in all its parts", as a help after the Spirit's witness.[^calvin-institutes] [[Richard B. Hays|Richard Hays]] holds that the Bible has "a deep and subtle narrative unity", "not because unity has been superimposed by ecclesial fiat or by some clever editorial design". The emergence of its writings, he says, "is itself part of God's mysterious 'authorial' action".[^hays-2016]
- **The pointers.** No academic count of 300 was found. Some passages the New Testament applies to Jesus are not predictions in their own setting: [Hosea 11:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hosea%2011:1&version=NIV), applied in [Matthew 2:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%202:15&version=NIV), looks back to the exodus.[^moyise-2022] [[The Messianic Prophecies Were Written Before Jesus]] takes up the count.
- **Later writers knew earlier ones.** In [Daniel 9:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Daniel%209:2&version=NIV) Daniel reads Jeremiah's seventy years ([Jeremiah 29:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2029:10&version=NIV)).[^athas-2009] Most New Testament scholars hold that Mark came first and that Matthew and Luke used it.[^goodacre-2001] [[Later Biblical Writers Drew on Earlier Ones]] sets out the cases and how far they reach.
- **Core and edges of the canon.** [[The Bible's Core Was in Use Early and Its Edges Stayed Open for Centuries]] traces when the core books came into use and how the edges were settled.
- **The text in one line.** Twenty-five biblical texts from Judean Desert sites outside Qumran, copied from about 50 BC to AD 115, agree almost completely in their consonants with the [[Leningrad Codex]].[^tov-2017-essence] [[Emanuel Tov]] estimates the agreement at "roughly 98%", with differences in spelling, small linguistic points and "minute content differences".[^tov-2017-outside] All Jewish streams accepted this consonantal text probably in the first century AD; before then only the Pharisaic stream held it.[^tov-2017-essence] [[Jews Used Several Forms of the Hebrew Bible Text Before One Remained]] sets out the other forms and the dispute over them.
- **How it held.** Rabbinic traditions describe a master copy of the Torah in the Temple Court and the correction of scrolls against it.[^tov-2017-essence] The other text forms dropped out of Jewish use after AD 70. Tov holds this was likely accidental: the Qumran group disappeared and the Samaritans and Christians left Judaism, so one text remained.[^tov-2017-socio] He reports that "many, if not most, scholars" see instead "a conscious and organized effort to stabilize the text of Scripture".[^tov-2017-socio]

# Countered By
- **Dependence plus a believing reading is the strong counter.** On this view the coherence is what dependent writers, editing scribes and a community reading backward from Christ would produce. It then calls for no single guiding author. [[Karel van der Toorn]] holds that the scribes who made the Hebrew Bible were not mere copyists but invented, compiled, expanded and adapted texts.[^schniedewind-2010] [[Bernard M. Levinson|Bernard Levinson]] argues that the same literature forbids alteration ([Deuteronomy 12:32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2012:32&version=NIV)) while revising earlier law. Its texts "resist any simple notion of canonical authority or of Scripture as one-sidedly divine".[^levinson-2007] Hays holds that discerning Christ in the Old Testament is "necessarily retrospective rather than prospective".[^hays-2014] It "would be a hermeneutical blunder", he writes, "to read the Law and the Prophets as deliberately *predicting* events in the life of Jesus".[^hays-2014] His point is about when the pattern can be seen: he holds it is real and God's work.[^hays-2016]
- **Unity from authorship.** [[Alvin Plantinga]] describes traditional biblical commentary as taking God to be the principal author of the whole Bible. That, he writes, "impels us to treat the whole more like a unified communication than a miscellany".[^plantinga-wcb] Unity read from that premise cannot also serve as evidence for it.
- [[Scripture Can Be God's Word Without Reporting God's Speech]] holds further cases of later biblical writers revising earlier ones.

# Limits
- The argument stands or falls on whether unguided writers, editors and copyists over centuries would be unlikely to produce this coherence and a text kept this exactly. Later writers read earlier ones.[^athas-2009][^goodacre-2001] Churches settled the canon's edges over centuries.[^eusebius-he3][^trent-1546] Christians read the Old Testament as about Christ looking back.[^hays-2014] One copying line became the text of all Jewish groups, by accident or by deliberate effort.[^tov-2017-socio] Each of these ordinary processes could produce part of the coherence and the stability. No study was found that compares the Bible's coherence and stability with those of other many-authored scriptures, to their own readers and to outsiders. That comparison is what would settle it.
- [[Elliott Sober]] argues that a design hypothesis gives an observation a probability only when it is supplemented with assumptions about the designer's goals and abilities.[^sober-2005] That needs "information about the putative designer(s) that is independently attested".[^sober-2002] A guiding author aiming at one unchanged text is contradicted by the plural text before the first century AD. One aiming at a coherent Scripture needs that aim attested from outside the Bible. Sober's cases are organisms and the cosmos, not texts, so applying his rule to scripture extends it.
- Where later writers drew on earlier texts, their agreement is not that of independent witnesses, the strongest form of the argument. [[Later Biblical Writers Drew on Earlier Ones]] sets out how far that reaches.
- Careful copying shows how well scribes copied, not who authored what they copied. Only "possibly five percent" of the forerunner of the Masoretic Text survives from the Judean Desert.[^tov-2017-outside] Because the surviving texts are "virtually identical" with the medieval text, Tov infers that the lost portions matched it too.[^tov-2017-outside]
- On Tov's account the evidence that one exact text held comes from the scrolls outside Qumran.[^tov-2017-outside][^tov-2017-socio] The [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] from Qumran "display broad textual diversity".[^tov-2017-outside] The [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] "generally conforms" to the Masoretic Isaiah, yet has "well over 2,600" variants, from one letter to whole verses.[^israel-museum-isaiah] The Israel Museum, which holds the scroll, says it confirms "to a remarkable degree" the wording and spelling of medieval manuscripts.[^israel-museum-gac] [[Jews Used Several Forms of the Hebrew Bible Text Before One Remained]] gives the other forms.
- The record shows care for the written scroll, not exactness as a general rule of Jewish culture. [[Josephus]] says no one dared "to add any thing to them, to take any thing from them". He says it in *Against Apion*, a defence of Jewish records against their detractors.[^josephus-apion][^dunkelgrun-2016] The Talmud credits the early scribes with counting every letter of the Torah, but a later teacher there says "we are not experts" in its spellings.[^kiddushin-30a] A rabbinic commentary on Deuteronomy reports three Temple scrolls that differed, settled by the reading two of them shared.[^sifre-356] By 1999, on one survey, most scholars no longer held that rabbinic sayings reproduce the sages' own words, though Scripture was memorised as a fixed text.[^jaffee-1999]
- At most the record favours guidance over its rival. Whether that makes guidance more probable than not depends on how likely it was before the evidence, which the record does not settle. Plantinga holds that belief in the Bible's inspiration is typically [[Warrant|warranted]] by testimony or the work of the Holy Spirit, not by historical inference.[^plantinga-wcb]
- No academic defence of the step from the Bible's unity to a single author was found. The step is stated in confessional writing: the Westminster Confession, Calvin and an interview with Hays.[^westminster-confession][^calvin-institutes][^hays-2016] The German school of whole-Bible theology reads the Testaments together on theological grounds and, in the texts reached, does not argue from coherence to a divine author.[^roloff-2000][^bormann-2024]
