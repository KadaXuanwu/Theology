# Description
Judgment in the Bible arrives after long warning and is called off when people change. God is described as preferring repentance over punishment, which makes the harsh outcomes late rather than quick or impulsive.

# Based On
- [Genesis 15:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2015:16&version=NIV) God waits four generations because the Amorites' sin isn't full yet.
- [Ezekiel 33:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ezekiel%2033:11&version=NIV) he'd rather people turn around than die.
- [Jonah 3:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jonah%203:10&version=NIV) Nineveh is warned, changes, and is spared.
- [Exodus 34:6–7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2034:6-7&version=NIV) merciful and slow to anger, but not letting guilt slide.
- [2 Chronicles 36:15–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%2036:15-16&version=NIV) warnings sent again and again before the exile.
- [Romans 2:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202:4&version=NIV) and [2 Peter 3:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Peter%203:9&version=NIV) the delay is described as patience, not indifference.

# Countered By
- [[God Is Brutal and Not Merciful]] is what this argument answers. It answers the timing, not the content.

# Limits
- Delay isn't the same as a fair chance. Children in [1 Samuel 15:2–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2015:2-3&version=NIV) had no four generations of warning, whatever their ancestors did.
- Nineveh is the one clear case in the text where a warned city repents and survives. Building a pattern on one example is thin.
- A delayed order to kill children is still an order to kill children. This argument shifts the objection from impulsiveness to justice; it does not remove it.
