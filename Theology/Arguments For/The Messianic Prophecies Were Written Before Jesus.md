# Description
The passages the New Testament applies to Jesus were written, copied and kept by Jews before he was born, so none was composed or added afterward to fit him. [[Justin Martyr]] rested his proof on testimonies "published before He came".[^justin-apology] [[Robert C. Newman]] and two co-authors make proper chronology a condition of any argument from prophecy. They count it met by the [[Septuagint]] and the [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] for any outcome after 150 BC.[^newman-2003]

The scrolls from Qumran include about 230 biblical manuscripts, dated from the third century BC to AD 70, with every book of the Hebrew Bible except Esther.[^iaa-intro] [[John J. Collins]] places the visions of Daniel, probably the latest of these books, in the persecution of 168 to 164 BC.[^collins-2018]

Six passages are tested here: [Isaiah 7:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%207:14&version=NIV), [Isaiah 52:13–53:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2052:13-53:12&version=NIV), [Psalm 22:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2022:16&version=NIV), [Micah 5:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Micah%205:2&version=NIV), [Daniel 9:24–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Daniel%209:24-27&version=NIV) and [Zechariah 9:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Zechariah%209:9&version=NIV). The [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] holds all 66 chapters of Isaiah, and every range printed from two [[Radiocarbon dating|radiocarbon]] runs on it ends before 100 BC.[^israel-museum-isaiah][^bonani-1992][^jull-1995] Micah 5:2 stands in a Greek scroll that the Göttingen catalogue dates to the first century BC.[^albrecht-2024]

For [[Augustine]] the Jews who hold these books are, by their own scriptures, "a testimony to us that we have not forged the prophecies about Christ".[^augustine-city] Of the early copies listed under Based On, none that reaches one of the six passages leaves it out.

The language of expectation was older too. The Psalms of Solomon, Jewish poems that reflect Pompey's siege of Jerusalem in 63 BC, ask God to raise up "their king, the son of Dauid".[^nets-2007][^collins-2011] The Qumran text [[4Q521 Messianic Apocalypse|4Q521]] speaks of "His [[Messiah]]", of the dead raised and of good news for the poor.[^wise-1992][^philonenko-2002] In [[4Q246 Son of God Text|4Q246]] a figure "will be called Son of God" and "Son of the Most High".[^tantlevskij-2021]

# Based On
- **Isaiah.** The Israel Museum dates the scroll "ca. 125 BCE".[^israel-museum-isaiah] The ranges printed by the Zurich and Arizona laboratories all fall between 356 and 103 BC.[^bonani-1992][^jull-1995] In the museum's translation the scroll reads "the young woman" at 7:14. At 53:8–12 its servant is "cut off from the land of the living", is given a grave and has "poured out his life to death".[^flint-ulrich]
- **Micah.** The [[Nahal Hever Greek Minor Prophets Scroll]] holds Micah 5:2–7. The Göttingen catalogue dates it to the first century BC, and a 2004 list of early Greek manuscripts puts it at the turn of the era.[^albrecht-2024][^kraft-2004] A Hebrew fragment from Qumran, dated about 50 BC, keeps nine letters of this verse and the next, and its identification is disputed.[^guillaume-2007][^tigchelaar-2020] Neither is among the samples of the three radiocarbon studies read for this node.[^bonani-1992][^jull-1995][^popovic-2025] Both dates rest on script.[^guillaume-2007][^kraft-2004]
- **Where no early copy reaches.** Of Daniel 9, the eight [[Qumran Daniel Manuscripts]] keep only verses 12–17.[^nel-2006] The Greek scroll breaks off at Zechariah 9:5.[^albrecht-2024] No early Hebrew copy of Zechariah 9:9 was found for this node. The one Judaean Desert scroll in which the disputed word of Psalm 22:16 is recorded is the [[Nahal Hever Psalms Scroll]], radiocarbon dated to AD 10 to 205.[^swenson-2004][^popovic-2025] The 2004 list of early Greek manuscripts has no copy of Isaiah or the Psalms older than Jesus.[^kraft-2004] The Greek Isaiah is generally dated near 150 BC, and the dates proposed for the Greek Psalms in the same survey fall in the second or first century BC.[^vorm-croughs-2014] For these verses the evidence of age is the date of the book or of its Greek translation, not a copy older than Jesus.
- **The older language.** The script of 4Q521 is dated to 100 to 80 BC.[^jull-1995] A 2025 radiocarbon run gave 355 to 285 or 230 to 100 BC, and its authors reject the older range.[^popovic-2025] [Luke 7:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%207:22&version=NIV) sets the dead raised beside good news for the poor, as 4Q521 does. The titles of 4Q246 recur in [Luke 1:32–35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201:32-35&version=NIV), and most scholars accept a date in the last third of the first century BC for its script.[^krajnc-2025]

# Countered By
- **Reading afterward is the strong counter.** [[David Friedrich Strauss]] stated the reasoning: "Such and such things must have happened to the Messiah; Jesus was the Messiah; therefore such and such things happened to him".[^strauss-1892] [John 12:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2012:16&version=NIV) says the disciples saw the link to Zechariah only after Jesus was glorified. [[John Dominic Crossan]]'s name for the strong form, applied to the passion narratives, is "prophecy historicized", set against "history remembered". He holds that those stories "are so linked to prophetic fulfillment that the removal of such fulfillment leaves nothing but the barest facts".[^goodacre-2006] [[Mark Goodacre]] calls that view "the one now common" and the choice "simply too stark". On his account the traditions "generated Scriptural reflection, which in turn influenced the way the traditions were recast".[^goodacre-2006] [[Richard B. Hays|Richard Hays]] holds that seeing Christ in the Old Testament is "necessarily retrospective rather than prospective".[^hays-2014] Each account needs the texts to be older than the events, so their age cannot choose between fulfilment and later fitting.
- **The writers told events in scripture's words.** The New Testament writers knew these texts and framed events around them, and the studies read here show it in detail for Matthew. A 2007 survey reports that at the entry into Jerusalem Matthew's quotation and its context "have mutually influenced each other".[^viljoen-2007] Only Matthew has two animals, and a 2012 study judges that Zechariah's doubled wording produced the second: "history conformed to Scripture".[^coppins-2012] A 2008 study finds that the passages Matthew cites as fulfilled are "not bound to prophetic predictions".[^kirk-2008] [[Later Biblical Writers Drew on Earlier Ones]] holds that where one book took material from another, the two are not independent witnesses for that material.

# Limits
- The argument stands or falls on whether any match between a passage and the life of Jesus is independent of the passage. Beyond chronology, Newman and his co-authors require a clear prediction, a documented outcome and odds too small for luck.[^gauch-2022] [[J. L. Mackie]] adds that the accuracy must not be explainable "causally (for example, on the ground that it brought about its own fulfilment) or as accidental".[^mackie-1982] By the notes below, five of the six passages fail one of those tests, and the death in Isaiah 52:13–53:12 is the one match with an outside witness. Newman and his co-authors judge otherwise. They hold that Jesus "was cut off in just the period designated by Daniel", and write that many features of Isaiah 53 "cannot be independently verified in human history".[^newman-2003] Evidence for a matched event from a source that does not know the passage would settle it, given a fit closer than a later search would find.
- No find can show whom a passage means: a manuscript's date says when the copy was made. That the passages refer to Jesus is interpretation, and Jewish readings differ. The [[Targum]] renders Isaiah 52:13 "my servant the messiah" and reads Micah 5:2 of the messiah.[^targum-jonathan] The [[Talmud]] reads Zechariah 9:9 and Isaiah 53:4 so.[^sanhedrin-98] Sefaria dates the Talmud tractate to AD 450 to 550 and the Targum anywhere from 150 BC to AD 350.[^targum-jonathan][^sanhedrin-98] In the places cited neither applies its passage to Jesus.
- **Isaiah 7:14.** The Hebrew word is almah, "young woman". The Greek rendered it parthenos, "virgin", and [Matthew 1:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%201:23&version=NIV) quotes the Greek form.[^vorm-croughs-2014][^viljoen-2007] [Isaiah 7:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%207:16&version=NIV) gives the sign a time limit in Ahaz's own crisis: before the child can choose good, the land of the two kings Ahaz dreads will be laid waste. A 1970 defence argues that the sign looks beyond Ahaz and that parthenos is a fair rendering.[^motyer-1970]
- **Isaiah 52:13–53:12.** No academic source read ranks it the strongest case. [[Tacitus]] reports the execution, and the scroll has the poem's death wording.[^tacitus-annals][^flint-ulrich] Yet the Gospels quote the poem for healings ([Matthew 8:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%208:17&version=NIV)), for unbelief ([John 12:38](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2012:38&version=NIV)) and at the Last Supper ([Luke 22:37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2022:37&version=NIV)), never where Jesus dies.[^horrell-2014] The exception, [Mark 15:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2015:28&version=NIV), is an addition found in some ancient copies only. [[Martin Hengel]] concluded that "we are not entirely without grounds for the hypothesis" of suffering messianic figures in Judaism before Jesus.[^hengel-2004] Collins finds "certainly no hint" of a dying messiah in the Jewish literature surviving from before Bar Kokhba's defeat in the second century AD.[^collins-2011] Isaiah names Israel as the servant at [41:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2041:8&version=NIV), [44:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2044:1&version=NIV) and [49:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2049:3&version=NIV), and at [49:5–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2049:5-6&version=NIV) gives the servant a mission to Israel. The earliest report found of this poem read of Israel dates probably from the late 240s. In it a Jewish opponent applies the poem to "the whole people, regarded as one individual".[^origen-celsus][^paget-2017]
- **Psalm 22:16** (verse 17 in Hebrew). The [[Masoretic Text]] reads "like a lion" and has no verb of its own, where the Septuagint has a verb meaning dug or gouged.[^nets-2007][^swenson-2004] A 1997 monograph on the Psalms scrolls records a verb in the Nahal Hever scroll, in a text one study of its photograph calls "nearly impossible to read". Whether that verb can mean "pierced" is disputed.[^swenson-2004] No New Testament writer quotes the line, and Justin is the first found to apply its hands and feet to the crucifixion.[^peake-1904][^justin-dialogue]
- **Micah 5:2** (5:1 in Hebrew). The verse promises a ruler and has no word for an anointed one. The birth at Bethlehem is told only in [Matthew 2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%202&version=NIV) and [Luke 2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%202&version=NIV), and the accounts differ. In Matthew the child is found in a house in Bethlehem, and the family settles in Nazareth only after a flight to Egypt. In Luke it comes from Nazareth for a registration and goes home.
- **Daniel 9:24–27.** Newman and his co-authors count sixty-nine seven-year cycles from Artaxerxes's commission and reach AD 28 to 35.[^newman-2003] The scholarly consensus puts the vision of Daniel 11 before the death of Antiochus IV in 164 BC.[^segal-2021] Scholars refer the anointed one cut off in 9:26 to the high priest Onias III, murdered about 171 or 170 BC.[^segal-2021][^athas-2009] A 2021 study judges that the chapter's prophecy was most likely composed after the events it describes.[^segal-2021] A 2008 commentary from Concordia Publishing House argues for the traditional earlier date.[^steinmann-2008] One Qumran text, copied about 75 to 50 BC, calls a herald the anointed of the spirit. The line breaks off after the first letters of Daniel's name, and a 2021 study prints a restored quotation of Daniel 9:25 there.[^rainbow-1997][^tantlevskij-2021]
- **Zechariah 9:9.** In [Mark 11:1–7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2011:1-7&version=NIV) Jesus sends for the animal himself, so on Mark's own telling the ride was arranged. Neither Mark nor Luke quotes Zechariah. Strauss listed three natural accounts: Jesus acted on the text, a chance ride was later read by it, or the story was composed from it.[^strauss-1892]
- The older language does not show fulfilment by itself. A 2025 study judges that Luke and 4Q246 "share the same theological tradition".[^krajnc-2025] The hope the Psalms of Solomon voice is for a conquering king, who is to "shatter in pieces unrighteous rulers".[^nets-2007] A 1956 notice of 4Q521 ascribed its saving acts directly to God, and its 1995 radiocarbon range runs to AD 80.[^philonenko-2002][^jull-1995] On 4Q246 there "remains no consensus on whether the Son of God plays a positive or negative role". The word messiah does not occur in what survives of it, and [[Joseph Fitzmyer]] denied that its figure was the messiah.[^krajnc-2025]
- "Not added afterward" holds for the six passages. Justin charged Jews with cutting "from the wood" out of Psalm 96:10, words the Hebrew lacks and whose origin is unsettled. Two other sayings he cites are in no known biblical manuscript, and most commentators treat them as Christian compositions.[^justin-dialogue][^kraft-1978]
- In print the step from older to foretold is argued by Newman and his co-authors.[^gauch-2022] No study in a peer reviewed journal outside confessional venues was found that weighs these passages as predictions against later fitting. Crossan's own books and the standard Daniel commentaries were not reachable in full.
