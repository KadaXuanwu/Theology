# Description
The Old Testament passages the New Testament applies to Jesus were already written, copied and circulating before he was born, so none of them can have been composed after the fact to fit him. Manuscripts settle this rather than tradition: Isaiah, the Psalms, the Twelve and Daniel are all attested in copies made before the 1st century AD. Alongside them, Jewish texts from the same caves describe a coming anointed figure in language the Gospels later use.

# Based On
- [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] for [Isaiah 7:14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%207:14&version=NIV) and [Isaiah 52:13–53:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2052:13-53:12&version=NIV), around 125 BC.
- [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] for the corpus generally, including the Psalms and Daniel.
- [[Nahal Hever Greek Minor Prophets Scroll]] for [Micah 5:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Micah%205:2&version=NIV), subject to the verse question flagged in that note.
- [[Nahal Hever Psalms Scroll]] for the disputed reading at [Psalm 22:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2022:16&version=NIV).
- [[4Q521 Messianic Apocalypse]] and [[4Q246 Son of God Text]] for the pre Christian expectation.
- [[Ketef Hinnom Silver Scrolls]] for how far back written scripture reaches, though it carries no messianic text.

# Countered By
Needs nodes. The strong counter is not about dates at all: a writer who knows a text can build a narrative around it, so the Gospel accounts of the donkey, the casting of lots and the birthplace are exactly what either a fulfilment or a well read author would produce. The second counter is that the Jewish reading of the same passages runs elsewhere, above all on the servant of Isaiah 53, read as Israel on the strength of [Isaiah 41:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2041:8&version=NIV), [44:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2044:1&version=NIV) and [49:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2049:3&version=NIV), where the text names the servant.

# Limits
- The crux: dating shows priority, not reference. Every manuscript in Based On establishes that a text existed before Jesus and nothing at all about who it is about. What would settle it: a pre Christian Jewish source applying a given passage to a coming messiah in the specific way the Gospels do. 4Q521 comes closest and is not tied to any named figure.
- Isaiah 7:14 has almah, a young woman of marriageable age, not betulah, the word for a virgin. The Greek Septuagint chose parthenos, and Matthew quotes the Greek. [Isaiah 7:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%207:16&version=NIV) sets the sign inside the reign of Ahaz.
- Micah 5:2 has no surviving copy older than the 1st century BC, and the claim that Jews already read it messianically before Jesus rests on [Matthew 2:4–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%202:4-6&version=NIV) and [John 7:42](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%207:42&version=NIV), which are Christian sources. The Bethlehem birth itself appears only in Matthew and Luke, and their accounts do not agree.
- Psalm 22:16 turns on one letter on one manuscript from after the crucifixion. See the Nahal Hever note.
- Daniel is dated by most scholars to around 165 BC, and [Daniel 9:24–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Daniel%209:24-27&version=NIV) is commonly referred to the murder of the high priest Onias III in 171 BC. Those particular verses are not preserved in any Qumran fragment, though other parts of Daniel are.
- [Zechariah 9:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Zechariah%209:9&version=NIV) is the clearest case of a prophecy a person could choose to act out.
