# Description
Texts written outside the Bible name people, peoples and places the Bible names, with matching titles and roughly matching dates, so its history was not invented from nothing. The matches cover the frame of Kings from the ninth century BC on, with one notice from the tenth.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007][^frevel-2023] They reach names, order, rough dates and public acts such as tribute and sieges.[^frevel-2023][^lemche-2001] To confirm means here that an outside text names the same person with a matching office and period, or reports the same act. Texts from Egypt, Moab, Aram, Assyria and Babylon name Israel, the house of David and kings from Omri to Jehoiachin.[^frevel-2023] Two Roman inscriptions name Pilate and Gallio.[^alfoldy-1999][^plassart-1967] Israel's neighbours and enemies wrote the first group for their own purposes, most of it more than a century before Kings took its final form.[^frevel-2023] Kings ends with [Jehoiachin's release](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2025:27-30&version=NIV) under a Babylonian king who reigned from 562 to 560 BC.[^frevel-2023] Yet it gives these kings in the order the dated inscriptions give, with tribute, sieges and successions those texts also record.[^lemche-2001][^frevel-2023] [[Amihai Mazar]] concludes that its ninth century framework "was based on reliable knowledge of the historical outline of that century".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] [[Christian Frevel]] judges that its king lists "are not completely constructed or fabricated" and go back in part to authentic material.[^frevel-2023] Writers working from older records explain this better than invention does.

# Based On
- Sennacherib's annals and the caption of his Lachish relief. Clay prisms in several copies and a palace wall slab, on the campaign of 701 BC.[^grayson-novotny-2012][^grayson-novotny-2014] The two dated prisms (691 and 689 BC) were purchased; their edition calls them certainly from Nineveh.[^grayson-novotny-2012] For [2 Kings 18:13–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2018:13-17&version=NIV) they confirm Hezekiah of Judah, Jerusalem as his royal city, the loss of his fortified towns and a payment with 30 talents of gold.[^grayson-novotny-2012] The relief's caption has the booty of Lachish pass before the seated king, and verse 14 has Hezekiah send word to him at Lachish.[^grayson-novotny-2014] The prisms give 800 talents of silver where the verse has 300, and they never claim that Jerusalem fell.[^grayson-novotny-2012] Esarhaddon's prism adds "Manasseh, king of Judah" ([2 Kings 21:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2021:1&version=NIV)).[^leichty-2011]
- Tiglath-pileser III's inscriptions. Stone slabs and a clay tablet from Nimrud, some lines broken.[^tadmor-yamada-2011] They confirm Menahem of Samaria paying Assyria ([2 Kings 15:19–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2015:19-20&version=NIV)), Hoshea replacing Pekah ([15:30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2015:30&version=NIV)) and Ahaz of Judah paying under his full name, Jehoahaz ([16:7–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2016:7-8&version=NIV)).[^tadmor-yamada-2011][^frevel-2023] Pekah's twenty years in [15:27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2015:27&version=NIV) do not fit the Assyrian dates.[^frevel-2023]
- Shalmaneser III's Black Obelisk and Kurkh Monolith. The obelisk was excavated at Nimrud in 1846, and the monolith was found at Kurkh in 1861.[^grayson-1996] They confirm Ahab "the Israelite" in 853 BC ([1 Kings 16:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2016:29&version=NIV)) and Jehu ruling Israel in 841 BC ([2 Kings 9–10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%209-10&version=NIV)).[^grayson-1996][^frevel-2023] Kings tells neither the battle nor Jehu's tribute.[^frevel-2023][^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- Two Babylonian texts. Ration lists from a palace archive excavated at Babylon allot oil to "Jehoiachin, the king of Judah" and to the king's five sons.[^alstola-2020][^pedersen-2009] That confirms the king of [2 Kings 24:12–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2024:12-15&version=NIV) by name and title. The one dated list is from 592 or 591 BC.[^frevel-2023][^alstola-2020] Babylonian Chronicle 5 is a British Museum tablet whose CDLI record gives no find spot.[^cdli-p555785] For [2 Kings 24:10–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2024:10-17&version=NIV) it confirms the siege of "the city of Judah", its capture in 597 BC, the king's capture and a successor chosen by Babylon.[^livius-abc5][^frevel-2023] It names neither king and puts the capture in Nebuchadnezzar's seventh year, where [24:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2024:12&version=NIV) has the eighth.[^frevel-2023]
- Sheshonq I's list on a standing temple wall at Karnak. It confirms the Shishak of [1 Kings 14:25–26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2014:25-26&version=NIV) and a campaign in Palestine in the later tenth century BC.[^frevel-2023] Jerusalem is not in the list.[^frevel-2023] Mazar holds that the writer of Kings "must have had records of some sort".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- [[Mesha Stele]], the lines on Omri. It came to notice at Dhiban in 1868, was broken up, and was rebuilt from its pieces and a paper impression taken first.[^louvre-ao5066][^langlois-2019] Frevel dates it to the middle or second half of the ninth century BC, the Louvre to shortly after 842 BC or about 830 to 805.[^frevel-2023][^louvre-ao5066] It confirms Mesha as king of Moab, Israel's rule over Moab and its end ([2 Kings 3:4–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%203:4-5&version=NIV)), and Omri, the first king of the northern kingdom named outside the Bible.[^frevel-2023] The campaign of [2 Kings 3:6–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%203:6-27&version=NIV) is not in it, and the two texts differ on timing.[^lemche-2001]
- [[Tel Dan Stele]]. Three fragments excavated at Tel Dan in 1993 and 1994, reused in a wall and a pavement, and dated by their editors to the ninth century BC.[^hagelia-2009] "King of Israel" stands in line 8 and "house of David" in line 9, whole and written as one word.[^hagelia-2009] That matches the name Kings gives Judah's dynasty ([1 Kings 12:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2012:19&version=NIV)) and a war between Aram and Israel ([2 Kings 8:28–29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%208:28-29&version=NIV)).[^frevel-2023] Frevel writes that this "is not proof of David's historical existence" but "makes it very probable", and that it fixes neither his date nor his realm.[^frevel-2023] The kings' names are restored, and on that restoration the author claims the killings [2 Kings 9:24–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%209:24-27&version=NIV) gives to Jehu.[^frevel-2023] A small minority reads a place name or a god's name.[^hagelia-2009][^frevel-2023]
- [[Merneptah Stele]]. Excavated at Thebes in 1896, with the find spot recorded.[^petrie-1897] It matches no single passage, and its excavator noted that Judges records no Egyptian invasion.[^petrie-1897] It names Israel, a reading Frevel calls certain, with a sign that suggests a people, and he dates it to probably 1208 BC.[^frevel-2023] It does not show what that group was, how large it was or where exactly it lived.[^frevel-2023][^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- [[Pilate Stone]]. Excavated at Caesarea in 1961, reused as a step in the theatre.[^alfoldy-1999][^barron-ciip] "Pilatus" is complete. "Pontius" and "praefectus" are restored from their last letters, and no one disputes them.[^alfoldy-1999] It confirms the governor of [Luke 3:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%203:1&version=NIV) and [Matthew 27:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2027:2&version=NIV) by name, office and province.[^barron-ciip] It says nothing of a trial, and lines 1 and 4 are disputed.[^alfoldy-1999] The Gospels' word for governor is a general one, and Greek has separate words for [[Prefect|prefect]] and [[Procurator|procurator]].[^lsj-1940] [[Josephus]] and [[Tacitus]] had already named him.[^josephus-antiquities][^tacitus-annals]
- Claudius's letter at Delphi. Nine fragments from the French excavation, partly restored.[^plassart-1967] It names Gallio as the emperor's friend and [[Proconsul|proconsul]] and is dated by Claudius's titles to AD 52.[^plassart-1967][^oliver-1971] That matches [Acts 18:12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2018:12&version=NIV) on the name and the office, and [Acts 18:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2018:2&version=NIV) on the reign. Paul and the hearing are not in it.[^plassart-1967]
- Kings cites the annals of the kings of Israel and of Judah as its sources ([1 Kings 14:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2014:19&version=NIV), [14:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2014:29&version=NIV)). Research generally assumes its editors had list material from both kingdoms, and Frevel reckons with none from before the ninth century BC.[^frevel-2023] Frevel also lists the option that the references to the annals are "standardized fictitious references".[^frevel-2023] For the late monarchy Mazar adds seals bearing "names of certain people mentioned in the Bible".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]

# Countered By
- The strong counter is that confirmation follows the writers' records and stops where they stop. Mazar, [[Israel Finkelstein]] and [[Niels Peter Lemche]] all observe that outside texts name Israel's kings only from the ninth century BC on.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007][^lemche-2001] Mazar pictures a telescope: the closer the events, the sharper the picture.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Frevel reports a relative consensus that Kings was first composed before the exile.[^frevel-2023] No outside text names Israel between the Merneptah Stele and the ninth century, about 350 years.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] No source of their own time names the patriarchs, and none outside the Bible proves that Solomon existed, which Frevel says does not make him unhistorical.[^frevel-2023] For the patriarchs, the exodus, the conquest and the scale of David and Solomon's kingdom, the same archaeology is silent or counts against the text.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Finkelstein finds no mention of an exodus in any Egyptian source of the period, and Mazar no remains of it in Sinai.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Mazar calls the [[United monarchy|united monarchy]] "far from the rich and widely expanding state portrayed in the biblical narrative".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] [[Joshua's Conquest Lacks Archaeological Evidence]] carries the conquest. Mazar defends the tenth century only: less than a century separates David and Solomon from Omri, and no outside power was then writing inscriptions, Sheshonq's list excepted.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] A confirmed name also confirms only the name. Lemche writes that the Mesha inscription "does not make 2 Kings 3 a reliable historical source".[^lemche-2001] Finkelstein and two colleagues hold that a late author used real places and names to give the Balaam story "a sense of authenticity".[^finkelstein-naaman-romer-2019] Finkelstein, on the early narratives, still calls it "inconceivable that the authors invented stories", and Lemche writes that the names of Israel and Judah "are not invented".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007][^lemche-2001]
- Mazar writes that the inscriptions "may reveal the incompleteness of the biblical narrative and some distortions".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Frevel judges the attack on Jerusalem in 1 Kings 14 "not historical in its narrated form".[^frevel-2023] He calls the Dan inscription and the Jehu story in Kings alike "biased literature".[^frevel-2023]
- On the usual reading Luke dates Jesus's birth to Herod's reign, which ended about 4 BC, and to the census of [Luke 2:1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%202:1-2&version=NIV), which Josephus puts in AD 6.[^armitage-2018] The study reporting this proposes a reading that removes the clash, and calls the Theudas of [Acts 5:36–37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%205:36-37&version=NIV) "chronologically incompatible" with the one in Josephus.[^armitage-2018][^josephus-antiquities]

# Limits
- The argument stands or falls on one step: that Kings matches the dated inscriptions on names, order and public acts because its writers had older records. The step reaches statements of that kind from the ninth century BC on, and before that only the notice of Shishak's campaign. It gives no measured support to the prophetic stories and miracles told in the same chapters. A source study of Kings that separates entries drawn from royal records from the stories set around them would fix where the step ends. One study does this for a single notice, the fall of Samaria, and traces it to a probable official document.[^levin-2019]
- Confirming the setting is not confirming the miracles. Mazar writes that fixing the site of the sea crossing "would only corroborate the geographic and environmental background to the story, but it cannot verify its historicity".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Lemche says 2 Kings 3 "remains as miraculous and fictional as ever" beside the Mesha inscription.[^lemche-2001] Nor does an inscription's silence count against them. Sennacherib's annals are silent on the angel of [2 Kings 19:35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2019:35&version=NIV), but an editor of those annals notes that Assyrian kings "generally avoided referring to unfinished business".[^novotny-2019]
- The dates match only roughly. Frevel counts "only a few absolute dates", such as Qarqar in 853 BC, that tie the Bible's figures to the ancient Near Eastern and Egyptian king lists.[^frevel-2023]
- [[Caiaphas Ossuary]] does not meet the test used here, because the box carries no title.[^iip-talpiyot] It comes from a tomb excavated at Jerusalem in 1990 and reads Joseph son of Qayafa, or Qafa, in two spellings.[^iip-talpiyot][^cargill-2011] The New Testament never calls Caiaphas Joseph, a name that comes from Josephus.[^josephus-antiquities][^zissu-goren-2011] Scholars agree that the tomb belonged to a family of that name and disagree on whether the high priest lay in it.[^cargill-2011][^zissu-goren-2011]
- This argument leaves out finds of doubtful reading or origin. The "house of David" in line 31 of the Mesha Stele restores a damaged line, and Frevel calls that reading and its rival both uncertain.[^langlois-2019][^finkelstein-naaman-romer-2019][^frevel-2023] A seal impression commonly linked with Jeremiah's scribe Baruch came from the antiquities trade, and Frevel reports it as a forgery.[^frevel-2023] The James [[Ossuary|ossuary]], inscribed "James, son of Joseph, brother of Jesus", belongs to a private collector.[^shanks-2012] A court in 2012 cleared him of forgery without ruling the inscription genuine.[^shanks-2012] One published list of New Testament figures records no inscribed monument or coin for Jesus, John the Baptist or James.[^mykytiuk-2017]
