# Description
Religious experience is evidence that God exists. [[Richard Swinburne]] argues from what he calls the principle of credulity: if it seems to someone that something is present, that seeming is itself reason to think it probably is present unless there is some specific reason to doubt it, the way an ordinary perceptual report is trusted until something specific counts against it. He argues experiences that seem to be of God should be treated the same way.[^swinburne-1979][^sep-religious-experience] William Alston, the philosopher behind *Perceiving God: The Epistemology of Religious Experience* (1991), argues the beliefs Christian religious practice produces are justified on the same footing as beliefs from sense perception: neither practice can be shown reliable except by already trusting some belief forming practice or other, so ruling out the religious one on that ground rules out the perceptual one too.[^alston-1991][^sep-religious-experience] [[William James]] had already supplied the descriptive half, cataloguing what conversion and mystical experience look like across the people who report them.[^james-varieties]

Scripture frames the experience as something to go find rather than take on report. [Psalm 34:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2034:8&version=NIV) says to taste and see that the Lord is good, [Jeremiah 29:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2029:13&version=NIV) promises that whoever seeks God with their whole heart will find him, and [John 7:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%207:17&version=NIV) makes doing God's will the condition for finding out whether the teaching is from him. [[Obedience Comes Before Evidence]] holds the same order as a claim of its own: confirmation follows the step, not the other way round.

# Countered By
- [[Experiential Confirmation Doesn't Distinguish Religions]] and [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] together are the strongest counter. The same procedure, commit first and look for confirmation after, produces confident confirmation of incompatible religions, and a documented bias toward finding agents and purpose once someone is looking supplies the mechanism that would produce this result whether or not anything is actually there.
- [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]]. The direct kind of evidence scripture reports giving Thomas, Paul and Elijah is not what is on offer now, so the evidence stays out of reach of anyone who has not already made the leap.
- [[Creation Alone Doesn't Point to God]] presses the same primed to find agents mechanism against the natural theology case, and reaches its conclusion from a different direction: an instinct reliably producing an interpretation does not make the interpretation true.

# Limits
- The leap has no stated content and no stated failure condition. Nothing in the invitation to come and see says what trying it consists of or what a negative result would look like, and a report that can never come back negative is not behaving like the perceptual report Swinburne's principle needs it to be. [[Obedience Comes Before Evidence]] and [[No Outcome Counts Against It]] hold the same problem for the step itself. What would settle it: a stated, checkable criterion for what a failed attempt looks like.
- Evidence for God is not yet evidence for Christianity. If Swinburne's and Alston's arguments hold, they support belief that some experience answering reality exists. They do not by themselves say which tradition's account of it, if any, is right.
