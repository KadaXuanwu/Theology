# Description
The wording of the New Testament is better attested than that of any work of Greek or Latin literature. It survives in more than 5,300 handwritten copies, more than any such work has.[^holmes-2012][^ehrman-2016] [[Bart D. Ehrman|Bart Ehrman]] writes that "the more manuscripts you have, the more likely it is that you can figure out what the authors originally said".[^ehrman-2016] The oldest fragments are dated to the second century and the oldest complete copy to the middle of the fourth.[^intf-liste][^sinaiticus-project] The *Annals* of [[Tacitus]] rest on two manuscripts, one of the ninth century and one of the eleventh.[^malloch-2013][^woodman-2017]

No classical scholar, [[F. F. Bruce]] wrote, would call the authenticity of Herodotus or Thucydides doubtful because their earliest useful manuscripts are "over 1,300 years later than the originals". For Bruce "the same standards must be applied to both".[^bruce-1960] On this argument, whoever trusts the wording of Tacitus on one late copy has no ground in the copying for doubting the wording of the New Testament.

# Based On
- **The count.** [[New Testament Manuscript Count]]: the register kept by the Institute for New Testament Textual Research at Münster held 6,199 entries for Greek manuscripts on 26 November 2026.[^intf-liste]
- **The comparison.** [[Tacitus Annals Manuscripts]]: books 11 to 16, with *Histories* 1 to 5, depend on one manuscript written at Monte Cassino in the eleventh century. Books 1 to 6 depend on one of the ninth.[^malloch-2013][^woodman-2017] The oldest witness of Caesar's *Gallic War* is of the ninth century.[^garcia-villena-2006] [[Iliad Manuscript Count]]: the oldest complete *Iliad* is of the tenth century AD, and before it stand papyrus fragments that reach back to the third century BC.[^due-2009]
- **The dates.** The register dates [[Papyrus P52]], a scrap of John, to 125 to 175. It dates P46 (letters of Paul), P66 (John) and P75 (Luke and John) to 200 to 225.[^intf-liste][^michigan-p46][^bodmer-p66][^vatican-p75] [[Codex Sinaiticus]], written in the middle of the fourth century, holds "the earliest complete copy of the Christian New Testament".[^sinaiticus-project] [[Michael W. Holmes|Michael Holmes]] writes that very little of the evidence can be securely dated before the end of the second century. He quotes [[Eldon Jay Epp|Eldon Epp]]'s judgement that it still gives a "close continuity with the remote past" that "is unusual in ancient text transmission".[^holmes-2012]
- **The Old Testament counterpart, which is one of date.** The main medieval copies of the [[Masoretic Text]] are the Aleppo Codex of about 925 and the [[Leningrad Codex]] of 1009.[^tov-2017-essence] The [[Great Isaiah Scroll]] was found among the [[Dead Sea Scrolls]] in 1947. It is dated by handwriting to about 125 BC, and the [[Radiocarbon dating|radiocarbon]] ranges printed by the Zurich and Arizona laboratories all fall between 356 and 103 BC.[^israel-museum-isaiah][^bonani-1992][^jull-1995] The Israel Museum calls it "some one thousand years older than the oldest manuscripts of the Hebrew Bible known to us before the scrolls' discovery".[^israel-museum-isaiah]

# Countered By
The strong counter is that the copies reach a text current about AD 200 and no further. [[David C. Parker|David Parker]] writes that the best an editor can do is recreate "forms of text that were current" between 200 and 300.[^parker-2007] He speaks of "a period for which we have no manuscript attestation, and in which we know the greatest amount of change and variation to have arisen".[^parker-2007] [[William L. Petersen|William Petersen]] dates the text of the critical editions to "about 180 c.e., at the earliest".[^petersen-2002] [[Helmut Koester]] wrote in 1989 that no external evidence confirms that the forms of the Synoptic Gospels fixed about 200 match what the authors wrote.[^koester-1989] The Münster institute calls what it reconstructs the [[Initial text|initial text]], which "is not necessarily identical to the text of the author".[^intf-initial] Nor does a large base guarantee that the right reading survived in it.[^wettlaufer-2012] At [2 Peter 3:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Peter%203:10&version=NIV) the Münster editors found no reading in the Greek witnesses that met their test, and printed a conjecture.[^holmes-2012] Ehrman's verdict is that the better attestation "cannot provide us with assurance that we know what the authors originally wrote".[^ehrman-2017]

"If nothing is known about the early text of the Gospels, then how can it be determined that they do not match our critical texts?", Holmes asks of Petersen's conclusion.[^holmes-2012] Koester's later work, he adds, gives up the claim for Matthew. He proposes stability from the paragraph up and fluidity from the sentence down. How far behind the late second century the text can be recovered he leaves open.[^holmes-2012] The institute's working hypothesis is that the initial text "corresponds largely to the text of the author".[^intf-initial] [[Larry W. Hurtado|Larry Hurtado]] argues from the agreement of P75 with the fourth century Codex Vaticanus in Luke and John. Careful copying of that type of text, he concludes, "goes back at least into the late second century". He takes P75 to be of about 200.[^hurtado-2012] A 2016 study argues that P75 fits a fourth century date.[^nongbri-2016]

Bruce wrote of people who "regard a 'sacred book' as ipso facto under suspicion".[^bruce-1960] [[A. N. Sherwin-White]] found it "astonishing" that the study of the Gospel narratives had taken "so gloomy a turn" while Graeco-Roman historians grew in confidence.[^sherwin-white-1963] His subject was the narratives and not their wording. [[M. I. Finley]] wrote in 1964, quoting him without his name, that the Gospel writers "bore witness, an activity of an altogether different order" from inquiry. The historian of Christian origins, he wrote, must work with the Christian writings "with no external controls".[^finley-1964] Bruce did "not quarrel with those who want more evidence for the New Testament than for other writings". He held that "there is much more evidence for the New Testament than for other ancient writings of comparable date".[^bruce-1960]

For the Old Testament counterpart, [[Emanuel Tov]] counts about 200 fragmentary biblical scrolls from Qumran.[^tov-2008-texts] [[Jews Used Several Forms of the Hebrew Bible Text Before One Remained]] draws on the same finds for the claim that the text circulated in several forms.

# Limits
- The crux is what "attested" means. Copies show what a text said, here back to a form current about AD 200.[^parker-2007] They do not show that what it says happened. That step needs evidence from outside the manuscripts. Tacitus on a trial of AD 20 can be set against the senate's own decree, which survives on bronze.[^cooley-2023][^flower-1997]
- [[James B. Prothro|James Prothro]], who defends the comparison, says more manuscripts mean only that "we have more to work with". He adds that a perfect record of an ancient historian's words would not make him believe all of them.[^prothro-2020]
- Editors weigh manuscripts and do not count them. A 1913 handbook of classical editing states the rule, and a 2012 study of New Testament editing restates it.[^hall-1913][^wettlaufer-2012] Holmes calls the New Testament tradition "cross-pollinated", so that the traditional family tree of manuscripts cannot be drawn for it.[^holmes-2012]
- The comparison is between copies of literary works. It fails for the sources of the Roman empire at large. The largest database of Latin inscriptions held 541,333 entries in 2025.[^kolb-2025]
- The lead is clear only in total copies. A list of 2001 numbers the ancient manuscripts of the *Iliad*, all fragmentary, up to 1569.[^bird-2010][^due-2009] The register has 920 dated New Testament entries from before AD 1000, 325 of them lectionaries.[^intf-liste]
- About 85 percent of the copies were made in the eleventh century or later, and very few of the rest are older than the third.[^holmes-2012] By one count about 90 percent of Byzantine manuscripts dating from the ninth to the twelfth century are religious books.[^shawcross-2018] The count also records what medieval scribes copied.
- The early dates rest on handwriting. The holding library now puts P52 in the middle to late second century, and published dates for P66 run from the early second century to the fourth.[^rylands-p52][^bodmer-p66] For much of the Gospels' text the earliest witnesses are, in Parker's words, "parchment codices copied at least two and a half centuries after the composition of the Gospels".[^parker-2011] On the register's dates 2 Timothy, 2 John and 3 John have no copy before the fourth century.[^intf-liste]
- The totals are counted on different bases. The register counts entries, and split manuscripts and supplements have entries of their own.[^intf-liste] Prothro says that the usual tables give classical works "a functional, whittled-down number" and not the copies that exist.[^prothro-2020] The New Testament is also a collection set beside single works: 57 Greek register entries are coded as holding all its parts.[^intf-liste]
- More copies mean more variants: about 500,000 in the Greek manuscripts, spelling aside. The estimate's author reads the number as a sign of how often the text was copied, more than of how badly.[^gurry-2016]
- Finley's answer to Sherwin-White appeared in a review for general readers. No reply in an academic journal is cited here because none was found.
