# Description
God is supposed to be loving, but he sends people to eternal death for getting one thing wrong: not accepting Jesus as the Son of God. This seems deeply unfair. You could be kind and honest your whole life, but if you don't believe, you're condemned. And for a lot of people, that disbelief isn't stubbornness. It's an honest conclusion based on what they see. Even the Bible itself gives reasons to doubt, like the killing in Joshua's conquest. Punishing someone forever for an honest mistake isn't justice.

# Based On
- [John 3:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:18&version=NIV) whoever does not believe is condemned already.
- [John 14:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2014:6&version=NIV) no one comes to the Father except through Jesus.
- [Mark 16:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016:16&version=NIV) whoever does not believe will be condemned.
- [[God Is Brutal and Not Merciful]] supplies the "the Bible itself gives reasons to doubt" step in the Description.
- [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]] supplies the "honest conclusion" step. The disbelief is honest because the evidence is thin.

# Countered By
- [[God Can't Accept Sin]] the separation follows from God's nature rather than from a verdict on one intellectual error, so the framing of a single mistake being punished is wrong.
- [[Hell as Self-Chosen Separation]] no one is sent anywhere. The outcome is the life without God that the person chose.

# Limits
- [Mark 16:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016:16&version=NIV) sits in the longer ending of Mark, which is missing from the earliest manuscripts. It is weak ground to build on and the argument does not need it.
- "One honest mistake" is the argument's own framing, not the tradition's. On most Christian readings the issue is a whole orientation, not a single belief. The argument is stronger if it engages that version rather than the thin one.
- It runs together two different complaints: that the penalty is disproportionate, and that the evidence was insufficient. The second is [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]]. Worth keeping them separate.
