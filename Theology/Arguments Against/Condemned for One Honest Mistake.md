# Description
Someone hears the Christian claim, looks into it, and is not convinced. [John 3:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:18&version=NIV) says whoever does not believe stands condemned already. Taken at face value, that lands on a person whose only failing was reaching an honest conclusion, and an honest conclusion cannot deserve a penalty without end. The argument runs against that reading of the verse. Westminster Confession 10.4 takes the same hard line, denying salvation to non-Christians "be they never so diligent" in following the light of nature.[^schaff-creeds]

The verse gives its own ground, and the ground is the not-believing itself: "because he has not believed in the name of God's one and only Son". It stands in the same perfect tense as the judgment it explains.[^bernard-1928] Nothing further is pending. [[J. H. Bernard|Bernard]] reads the perfect as a judgment already settled in this life, which the last day publishes rather than pronounces.[^bernard-1928] The United Bible Societies handbook for translators reads it as a present state.[^newman-nida-1980]

Catholic teaching has an answer for the person who never heard the claim, and an answer for the person who heard it and refused. [[Invincible Ignorance Excuses Unbelief]] traces both, and the ignorance that excuses reaches past the unevangelised. What it turns on is the quality of the presentation rather than the honesty of the hearer. An adequate presentation obliges assent under pain of mortal sin, and an inadequate one leaves the ignorance standing. The hearer who got an adequate presentation and was honestly not convinced has no place in that structure.[^bullivant-2011]

Nobody decides to find an argument convincing the way they decide to take an action.[^schellenberg-2015-philosophy] That holds for direct control, and [[Belief Isn't Directly Chosen]] sets out the indirect influence the field grants past it.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism] The duties attach there, to gathering evidence and thinking about it, and the duty to gather grows with how much the question matters.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism] [[Rik Peels]], who denies that belief is chosen, makes responsible belief turn on having had control over what influenced it and having broken no intellectual obligation.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism] The argument's step is that a person who investigated as hard as the stakes demanded has nothing left to answer for.

The sizes do not match either. One wrong conclusion, drawn on limited evidence, is answered with a penalty that never ends.

John's gospel itself ties guilt to what a person was given. Without the message they would not be guilty, and the blind would not be guilty. The guilt falls on those who claimed they could see, and on those who saw the works and hated anyway. Both sayings are addressed to people who had been given the most, so the principle is drawn out rather than stated.

Philosophers call this case [[Non-resistant non-belief|non-resistant non-belief]]. On the face-value reading the verse penalises a state the person reached by doing the one thing he owed, without end and without grading.

# Based On
- [John 3:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:18&version=NIV) the verb rendered "stands condemned already" is κέκριται, a perfect read as a passive. The ground follows in a ὅτι clause with its verb in the same perfect, ὅτι μὴ πεπίστευκεν εἰς τὸ ὄνομα.[^bernard-1928]
- [John 9:41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%209:41&version=NIV) and [John 15:22,24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2015:22,24&version=NIV) guilt tracks what a person was given, drawn out of the sayings rather than stated in them.
- [[Invincible Ignorance Excuses Unbelief]] carries the tradition's side, from Aquinas on [[Invincible ignorance|invincible ignorance]] through the magisterial texts to Vitoria's rule that a bare announcement without proofs does not oblige belief. *Lumen Gentium* and the Catechism at §847 and §1260 run against §846, which conditions condemnation on knowing and refusing.[^lumen-gentium][^catechism]
- [[No Finite Life Deserves Infinite Punishment]] carries the size half: [[Marilyn McCord Adams|Adams]] on why guilt tracks the offender rather than the one offended, the second prong that turns retributivism against itself, and the defenders of the Anselmian premise still in print.
- [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]] carries the honesty half, including where [[J. L. Schellenberg|Schellenberg]]'s term actually comes from.
- [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]] supplies what the person was working with, and [[God Is Brutal and Not Merciful]] one route to an honest no.

# Countered By
- [[God Can't Accept Sin]] and [[Hell Is Self-Chosen Separation]] deny the trigger. What is judged is a settled orientation of the will, not a verdict on a question. This is the strong counter, and the version that bites is textual. The object of believing through the passage is a person rather than a claim, and the handbook takes "the name" in verse 18 as a Semitism for the person.[^newman-nida-1980] [[Teresa Morgan]] and [[Chris Seglenieks]] argue at book length that Johannine believing names a relationship of trust rather than assent to a proposition.[^morgan-2015][^seglenieks-2020] [John 3:19–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:19-20&version=NIV) backs it from the node's own proof text, grounding the condemnation in loving darkness. Bernard takes verse 19 to introduce an explanation rather than a new topic, and writes that John "traces unbelief to moral causes".[^bernard-1928] No commentary opened for this node reads verse 19 as replacing the ground verse 18 gives.
- The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* names [[C. S. Lewis|Lewis]], [[Jerry Walls|Walls]], [[Richard Swinburne|Swinburne]] and [[R. Zachary Manis|Manis]] holding hell to be a freely embraced condition rather than an imposed punishment.[^talbott-sep]
- [Romans 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:20&version=NIV) "without excuse" denies this defence, see [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]]. The Greek attaches the phrase by a bare pronoun to the people just described, and the ICC commentary takes it of the Gentile in view.[^sanday-headlam-1902] [[Alvin Plantinga]] argues the deliverances of the sense of God can be "suppressed and impeded".[^plantinga-wcb] [[The Suppression Thesis Rests on Possibility]] sets out what he does and does not claim with it. [[Lari Launonen]], who accepts part of that, calls the universal suppression thesis "hopeless" while arguing suppression is common and non-resistant non-belief rare.[^launonen-2025]
- [[Stephen Napier]] argues the putative cases are either resistant or not of the kind a loving God would rule out.[^napier-2023] [[Douglas V. Henry]] put the denial earlier, in two papers asking whether reasonable non-belief exists.[^henry-2001][^henry-2008] [[Andrew Blanton]] answered that line in 2026, arguing the class is relatively common.[^blanton-2026]
- The objection the argument cannot answer is [[Michael Rea]]'s. He grants that the honest unbeliever exists and denies the inference: on his account our concepts of God's attributes, love included, apply only analogically.[^rea-2018]
- Walls argues that nobody is damned who has not rejected God decisively and with adequate knowledge, since the opportunity is not closed at death.[^walls-1992] The move grants that the verse read at face value would be unjust.
- [[Stephen Bullivant]] argues that in a plural society many people, perhaps a majority, are never "existentially confronted" with the gospel in [[Karl Rahner|Rahner]]'s sense.[^bullivant-2011] That would put this person inside the ignorance category rather than outside it.
- On the size, [[Anselm]] holds no sin against God is small, since one may not act against God's will even to save the whole creation.[^anselm-cdh] Adams reads that as guilt tracking the dignity of the one offended, and answers at p. 443 that we judge a nobody striking [[Albert Schweitzer|Schweitzer]] less guilty than the reverse.[^adams-1975] Its current defenders are at [[No Finite Life Deserves Infinite Punishment]].

# Limits
- The argument turns on what the not-believing in John 3:18 is. Whether the verse's own stated ground reaches a person who was not convinced, or only a person who refused, decides the case. The next sentence gives the judgment as people loving darkness, and the object of the verb through the passage is a person. The one commentary opened on the point traces the unbelief to moral causes, which runs against the argument, and it dates from 1928.[^bernard-1928] A current lexicon on πιστεύω εἰς and a modern commentary on John 3:18–21 would settle it. Neither was opened for this node, and every commentary that was dates from between 1880 and 1953.
- Whether such people exist in any number is contested and the node asserts it. What is measured, what is not, and who denies it are set out at [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]]. No survey instrument reached on this run separates a person who examined the claim and was not convinced from one who did not want it to be true.
- The step from not under direct control to not blameworthy is the weakest joint. Peels states his condition for believing something responsibly, not for responsibly failing to believe it.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism] The field holds live positions on which the step fails. [[Richard Feldman]] ties the duty to getting the belief right rather than to the effort spent,[^mccormick-2013] and [[Lindsay Rettler]] grounds blame in indirect control.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism]
- "Looked into it" is a stipulation with no stated standard, and what counts as enough scales with the stakes.[^sep-doxastic-voluntarism] Schellenberg's own account of resistance covers careless investigation and mental drifting, so the term does not stretch to everyone who looked and was not persuaded.[^sep-hiddenness]
- No defender of the face-value reading was found at academic register. Every academic counter reached works by declining that reading, and its live holders publish confessionally. Westminster 10.4 denies salvation first to the non-elect who are called by the ministry of the Word, and only then to non-Christians.[^schaff-creeds] It calls the contrary opinion "very pernicious, and to be detested".[^schaff-creeds] [[Denny Burk]] defends eternal conscious torment in *Four Views on Hell*.[^burk-2016] The Reformed tradition has no category answering to invincible ignorance, and covers whoever it covers by election.[^schaff-creeds]
- "One honest mistake" is the argument's own framing, not the tradition's. It joins two complaints, the penalty's size and the evidence's sufficiency, which is the shape [[Theodore Drange|Drange]]'s argument from nonbelief already had in 1993.[^drange-1993] The second complaint is [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]]. The Greek verb in John 3:18 is κρίνω, to judge, and "condemned" is the NIV's rendering.
- [Mark 16:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016:16&version=NIV) is usually stacked with John 3:18 and left out here. It sits in the longer ending of Mark, missing from the earliest complete Greek manuscripts.[^metzger-1994] The Editio Critica Maior of Mark, published in 2021, prints the gospel ending at 16:8.[^head-mark][^ecm-mark-2021]
- The argument has no answer to Rea, and no published reply to him was found on this run.
- Bullivant's conclusion that most modern unbelievers are inculpably ignorant runs on a sociological premise its own field has contested for fifty years, set out at [[Invincible Ignorance Excuses Unbelief]].
