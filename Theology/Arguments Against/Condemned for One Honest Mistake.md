# Description
The case is narrower than the title. Set two people aside: one never heard the Christian claim, the other heard it and turned it down. This node is about the person in between, who heard it, looked into it, and came out unconvinced. Their character is not the issue. Whether they were resisting is, and this one was not.

Christianity has answers for the two set aside, and they differ. Aquinas treats the unbelief of someone who has heard nothing as a punishment rather than a sin, while still holding that such people are damned for their other sins. Vatican II and the Catechism go further: those who through no fault of their own do not know the gospel can be saved, if they seek God sincerely and follow their conscience. Where a person understood and refused, the refusal is what gets judged. Neither route reaches the person in between. They heard, so ignorance does not cover them. They did not refuse, so refusal does not describe them. What is left is an eternal outcome attached to a conclusion someone reached in good faith.

Two things go wrong at once. The first is the trigger: the outcome follows from what the person ended up persuaded of, and nobody picks what to be persuaded of the way they pick what to do. The second is the size. A finite person, working on partial information in a situation nobody calls clear, gets an outcome with no end to it. Nothing about the failure scales to the response.

The gospel carrying the sharpest verse for the condemnation also carries the principle that cuts against it. Twice in that book guilt is made conditional on what a person was handed. Without the message they would not be guilty, and the blind would not be guilty. The guilt lands on those who claimed they could see, and on those who saw the works and hated anyway.

Philosophy has a name for this case, non-resistant non-belief, meaning non-belief that does not come from resisting God. The argument stands or falls on whether such people exist in any number.

# Based On
- [John 3:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:18&version=NIV) whoever does not believe stands condemned already, in a perfect passive, so the verdict is already in place.
- [John 9:41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%209:41&version=NIV) and [John 15:22,24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2015:22,24&version=NIV) guilt tracks what a person was given.
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* II-II q.10 a.1, on unbelief in "those who have heard nothing about the faith". *Lumen Gentium* 16 (1964) and Catechism §847 and §1260, against §846, which conditions condemnation on knowing and refusing.
- [[No Finite Life Deserves Infinite Punishment]] carries the size half, with Adams on why guilt tracks the offender rather than the one offended.
- [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]] carries the honesty half, including where Schellenberg's term actually comes from.
- [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]] supplies what the person was working with, and [[God Is Brutal and Not Merciful]] one route to an honest no.

# Countered By
- [[God Can't Accept Sin]] and [[Hell as Self-Chosen Separation]] deny the trigger. What is judged is a settled orientation of the will, not a verdict on a question. This is the strong counter. The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* names Lewis, Walls, Swinburne and VanArragon holding hell to be a freely embraced condition rather than an imposed punishment. [John 3:19–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203:19-20&version=NIV) backs it from the node's own proof text, grounding the condemnation in loving darkness.
- [Romans 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:20&version=NIV) "without excuse" denies this defence, see [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]]. Alvin Plantinga argues the deliverances of the sense of God can be "suppressed and impeded" (*Warranted Christian Belief*, 2000). Lari Launonen, who accepts part of that, calls the universal suppression thesis "hopeless" while arguing suppression is common and non-resistant non-belief rare (*Religious Studies* 61, 2025).
- Stephen Napier argues the putative cases are either resistant or not of the kind a loving God would rule out ("Is there non-resistant non-belief?", *Religious Studies* 59:4, 2023).
- On the size, Anselm holds no sin against God is small, since one may not act against God's will even to save the whole creation (*Cur Deus Homo* I.21). Adams reads that as guilt tracking the dignity of the one offended, and answers that we judge a nobody striking Schweitzer less guilty than the reverse (*Religious Studies* 11:4, 1975, p. 443).

# Limits
- Whether such people exist in any number is contested and the node asserts it. What is measured, what is not, and who denies it are set out at [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]].
- The target is not one doctrine. Westminster Confession 10.4 denies salvation to non-Christians, "be they never so diligent" in following the light of nature, against Catechism §1260.
- [Mark 16:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016:16&version=NIV) is usually stacked with John 3:18 and left out here, since it sits in the longer ending of Mark, which is missing from the earliest manuscripts (Metzger, *A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament*, 1994).
- "One honest mistake" is the argument's own framing, not the tradition's. On most Christian readings the issue is a whole orientation, not a single belief. It also mixes two complaints, the penalty's size and the evidence's sufficiency. The second is [[Demanding Belief Without the Proof]].
