# Description
The Book of Joshua describes Israel destroying great fortified Canaanite cities. If this happened in the Late Bronze Age (~13th century BCE), the archaeology should show it, but it largely doesn't. Excavations repeatedly find the named cities were small unfortified villages or empty ruins at that time. Jericho's famous walls date to the Middle Bronze Age, centuries too early, and the site was minor or empty at the conquest date. Ai (et-Tell) was destroyed around 2400 BCE and lay abandoned for about 1,200 years, until roughly 1200 BCE. The Amarna Letters show Canaan was already an Egyptian province run through vassal city-rulers, not independent super-kingdoms, and Egypt never records losing it to invaders. Defenders like [[Titus Kennedy]] and [[David Falk]] propose alternative site identifications and higher population estimates, but reworking the data that heavily suggests it doesn't fit the text. Hazor is the one real exception, a large destruction layer in roughly the right period, but experts dispute the cause (Israelites, rival Canaanites, or internal upheaval), and one burned city isn't a conquest of the land. The most economical conclusion: the narrative was shaped later, most plausibly in the Iron Age, as a founding story projecting a unified origin onto a past that looked nothing like it.

# Based On
- [Joshua 6:20–21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%206:20-21&version=NIV) Jericho's walls fall, city destroyed.
- [Joshua 8:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%208:28&version=NIV) Ai burned and made a ruin.
- [Joshua 11:10–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011:10-13&version=NIV) Hazor burned (the one disputed destruction layer).
- The excavation data in the Description is currently unsourced. Kenyon on Jericho, Callaway on et-Tell and Ben-Tor on Hazor are the primary references and should be pulled into Evidence notes rather than left as prose here.
- [[Merneptah Stele]] places a people called Israel in Canaan by c. 1208 BCE. This cuts against the argument rather than for it, and is listed here because the argument has to account for it.

# Countered By
- [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]] if the destruction language is genre rather than report, the missing layers stop being a problem. This is the main answer and it is a strong one.
- [[The Bible Only Speaks Truth]] is what the argument is ultimately aimed at, since a failed historical claim is the clearest test of inerrancy available.

# Limits
- Absence of evidence in Levantine archaeology is weaker than it sounds. Small, brief settlements and mudbrick destruction leave little, and the sites are dug in small fractions.
- It cuts both ways with [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]]. Accepting the hyperbole reading removes the archaeological problem, but shrinks the historical conquest to something far smaller than the text describes. The two arguments cannot both be pressed at full strength.
- The dating depends on which exodus chronology is assumed. A 15th century date changes which layers are the relevant ones, and defenders use exactly this move.
- It does not touch [[God Is Brutal and Not Merciful]]. If anything it makes that argument harder, because the commands then look composed rather than recorded.
