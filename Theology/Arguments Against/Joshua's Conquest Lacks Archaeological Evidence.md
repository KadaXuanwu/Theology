# Description
At no date yet proposed does the excavated record, as its current excavators read it, show Jericho, Ai and Hazor all burned within one lifetime. So the record does not bear out [Joshua 6–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%206-11&version=NIV) as a report of the campaign it narrates, and at the 13th century date it contradicts it. The book may still keep older memories of Canaan. Joshua narrates the burning of these three cities and of no other. If the account reports events, towns stood at Jericho and Ai to be taken, and all three burned within Joshua's lifetime. Jericho's walled [[Middle Bronze Age|Middle Bronze]] city burned about 1550 to 1525 BCE.[^nigro-yasine-2024] Et-Tell, the usual site of Ai, lay in ruins for over a thousand years before an [[Iron Age|Iron I]] village rose on it.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Hazor's last Canaanite city burned in the 13th century.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] The early Iron Age hill villages grew from people already in the land, and the book took its present form centuries later.[^frevel-2023]

# Based On
- [Joshua 6:24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%206:24&version=NIV), [8:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%208:28&version=NIV) and [11:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011:11&version=NIV) have Israel burn Jericho, Ai and Hazor, and [11:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011:13&version=NIV) says that of the northern cities standing on their mounds Israel burned Hazor alone. The cities taken in [10:28–39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2010:28-39&version=NIV) are put to the sword, most of them under the [[Herem|ban]], and none is burned; the Hebrew keeps capture, ban and burning as three verbs.[^bdb]
- A town lived in for years leaves pottery that excavation recovers.[^wallach-2019] A study of the method rates settlement archaeology a favourable case for inference from absence, but holds each inference to the area dug.[^wallach-2019] The same study reports that total removal after the fact, offered for Ai and Jericho, "was generally regarded as ad hoc and unconvincing".[^wallach-2019] [[Bryant G. Wood|Bryant Wood]], who defends the conquest, notes that on an eroding mound pottery washes to the base rather than vanishing.[^wood-2005]
- Jericho's walled Middle Bronze city ended in fire. Short lived grain dates the fire to the late 17th or the 16th century BCE, most likely the 16th.[^bruins-vanderplicht-1995] The dating paper rules out Wood's date of about 1400 BCE for this city.[^bruins-vanderplicht-1995] New samples from the current excavation give 1550 to 1525 BCE.[^nigro-yasine-2024] A [[Late Bronze Age|Late Bronze]] building above it is dated by its pottery to the 15th century and one of its walls to Late Bronze II. The report gives no date for its end.[^nigro-yasine-2024] The 1950s excavator wrote in 1957 that no fragment of the Late Bronze town wall survives, and a 1986 study read a small unwalled settlement.[^kenyon-1957][^bienkowski-1986] The current team found scanty remains of a Late Bronze I mudbrick wall on the old rampart. It cites earlier excavators for the same wall in places, that excavator's 1981 final report among them.[^nigro-yasine-2024] Wood reports that four expeditions found no 13th century pottery on the mound or in its tombs.[^wood-2005]
- Et-Tell, the usual identification of Ai, was a walled Early Bronze city destroyed over a thousand years before an Iron I village was built on its ruins.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Its excavator in the 1930s found no Middle or Late Bronze remains and judged [Joshua 7–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%207-8&version=NIV) a legend founded on a real site.[^marquet-krause-1935]
- Khirbet el-Maqatir, about a kilometre west, is the site some defenders propose instead.[^kennedy-2011] Its excavators report a small Middle Bronze and Late Bronze I fortress, poorly preserved and abandoned until Iron I.[^stripling-hassler-2023] Wood, its director, reports a fire about 1400 BCE from reddened bedrock, calcined stones, ash and refired pottery.[^wood-2012] [[Titus Kennedy|Kennedy]], who also dug there, describes that evidence as "a few small patches of ash" with refired pottery.[^kennedy-2011] He finds no well preserved burn layer, which he says should not be expected at so shallow a site.[^kennedy-2011] A 2025 study that places the conquest at the end of the Middle Bronze Age quotes an archaeologist who reads the fortress pottery Wood published as late Middle Bronze.[^bimson-2025] The report on its Bronze Age strata was still forthcoming in 2023.[^stripling-hassler-2023]
- Hazor's last Canaanite city fell to "a tremendous fire" in the 13th century, in [[Amihai Mazar]]'s words.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Statues there, Egyptian kings' among them, were deliberately mutilated.[^hazor-reports][^rabinovich-silberman-1998] The 1950s excavations found the Middle Bronze lower city "razed by fire", and another fire ended a later city at the end of the 14th century, on that entry's dating.[^yadin-gibson-2007] The same 2025 study reports that a current director of the excavation finds that Middle Bronze fire in one building only, with no destruction of the city.[^bimson-2025] A 15th century fire, dated by a small pottery group, may have come from an Egyptian campaign, its excavators suggest.[^hazor-reports]
- Apart from Hazor's fire, what the record matches in Joshua is setting. Mazar reads the thirty-one kings of [Joshua 12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2012&version=NIV) as a land of city states "hardly known" under the monarchy.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] He finds Canaanite city life continuing through Iron I in many of the cities that [Joshua 17:11–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2017:11-13&version=NIV) and [Judges 1:27–35](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%201:27-35&version=NIV) list as not taken.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] He also notes that Joshua never mentions the three centuries in which Egypt ruled Canaan, a rule that lasted into the 12th century.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007][^frevel-2023]
- [[Israel Finkelstein]] counts over 250 Iron I sites in the central hills against about thirty Late Bronze ones, with "no hint of large-scale migrations".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] [[Christian Frevel]] calls it "now a consensus" that the Iron I findings contradict an extensive population exchange.[^frevel-2023]
- Frevel reports that the conquest account is usually dated to the late 7th century BCE.[^frevel-2023] Finkelstein holds it "inconceivable" that the late writers invented the conquest stories outright.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- These are statements by named scholars, not a survey. Mazar calls it "now accepted by all" that archaeology contradicts the conquest as one event under one leader.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Wood writes that scholars abandoned the 13th century model because the evidence does not support it.[^wood-2007] [[James K. Hoffmeier|James Hoffmeier]], who defends the exodus tradition, writes that the real debate is whether the books from Exodus to Judges hold any history at all.[^hoffmeier-2007]

# Countered By
- The strong counter is Hazor, the one place where the account may report an act. Mazar reports that its excavator [[Amnon Ben-Tor]] found no destroyers more fitting than the Israelites or "proto-Israelites".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Ben-Tor excluded Egypt, Canaanite rivals and the [[Sea Peoples]].[^zuckerman-2006] A magazine report gives his reasons: the smashed statues of Egyptian kings, no rival city strong enough to attack, and no Sea Peoples pottery.[^rabinovich-silberman-1998] Hoffmeier ties the mutilated statues to [Deuteronomy 7:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%207:5&version=NIV).[^hoffmeier-2007] Mazar reads [Joshua 11:10–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011:10-13&version=NIV) as "a reflection of historical memories", since a 7th century writer could not have invented Hazor's old rank.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Against it, [[Sharon Zuckerman]] wrote that newer dating "places it too early for the Israelites" and blamed an internal revolt.[^zuckerman-2006] The 2021 study of the destruction, with Ben-Tor among its authors, offers in its abstract two possible scenarios for the date and the cause.[^bechar-2021] Mazar calls the destroyers' identity "enigmatic" and writes that archaeology cannot confirm Israelite tribes were responsible.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Frevel holds that the destruction does not necessarily speak for Israelite conquerors, nor against them, and Finkelstein ties the Hazor story to late claims on the lost north.[^frevel-2023][^finkelstein-mazar-2007] Mazar does not exclude that such stories echo events "perhaps not specifically in relation to Israel as a nation or to Joshua as a military leader".[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- The other replies move the date or the site, and each date's defenders reject the others. Wood calls the 13th century model "no longer tenable", and Hoffmeier finds Wood's case for the earlier date "fraught with some serious problems".[^wood-2005][^hoffmeier-2007] [[Kenneth Kitchen|Kitchen]] holds the 13th century date and proposes that any Jericho of that time has "long, long since gone" through erosion, as Wood quotes him.[^wood-2005] Wood and Kennedy date Jericho's burned city to about 1400 BCE, and Wood sets Ai at Khirbet el-Maqatir and Hazor's fall at the same date.[^wood-2007][^kennedy-2011] Kennedy's 2023 paper, by its abstract, reassesses the pottery, scarabs, a cuneiform tablet and radiocarbon samples to date that city's fall.[^kennedy-2023] The radiocarbon dates above put that city's end more than a century earlier. A 2026 paper accepts that and moves Joshua's Jericho to the Late Bronze town above, with a conquest between 1426 and 1346 BCE by its reckoning.[^windle-2026] It reports that the current excavator dates the fall of that town's wall to the second half of the 14th century, by earthquake, and that no final report has appeared.[^windle-2026] A 2024 paper by a geochemist, in a journal of Christians in science, accepts the radiocarbon date and moves the exodus to about 1575 BCE.[^dickin-2024] It builds on a 1996 proposal that, by its own account, archaeologists have given "little credence".[^dickin-2024]
- [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]] answers the summary verses of total conquest, such as [Joshua 10:40](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2010:40&version=NIV) and [11:23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2011:23&version=NIV). The prevailing reading already takes them as an editor's idealising language, as a study that disputes that reading reports.[^clarke-2010] It does not reach the three burnings, since a named city burned is a specific act, not a claim of totality.

# Limits
- The argument stands or falls on one premise: that no date has all three cities burning within one lifetime. It holds most firmly in the 13th century, when Hazor burned but et-Tell had no Late Bronze town and Maqatir stood empty from about 1406 to 1187 BCE.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007][^stripling-hassler-2023] Around 1400 BCE it rests on absence. Hazor burned in the 15th century, dated by a small pottery group, and Maqatir about 1400 on its director's account.[^hazor-reports][^wood-2012] A 2026 paper dates a fire in Jericho's Late Bronze town, near walls found in the 1930s, to the 14th century.[^windle-2026] The current team's interim report dates no such fire.[^nigro-yasine-2024] At the end of the Middle Bronze Age Jericho burned, and Maqatir's fortress would fall in the same window if its pottery is late Middle Bronze.[^bimson-2025] There the premise rests on Hazor, where a current director finds only one building burned.[^bimson-2025] Firmer dates for these fires, the final reports on Maqatir and on Hazor's Bronze Age strata, and which site is Ai would settle it.
- If Israelites did burn Hazor in the 13th century, the book would remember one real act, but Ai would still have no town to take in that century.
- The inference is a [[Modus tollens|modus tollens]] on the account together with a date and a site, so a defender can keep the account by changing either. Mazar's verdict on Ai holds "assuming" et-Tell is Ai, which he grounds on its being the only prominent site east of Bethel.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- Against Wood's date for the burned city, the case at Jericho rests on a positive date, not on absence. Elsewhere it rests on absence. In probability terms a real search that finds nothing lowers a claim's probability.[^stephens-2011] How far depends on how likely the evidence was to survive and be found.[^wallach-2019] It weighs least where remains are thin, and the current team calls the Late Bronze remains on Jericho's rampart scanty.[^nigro-yasine-2024]
- It reaches the campaign the book narrates, not every memory in it. The classic manual of historical method tests a document one statement at a time, since any one may be false while the others are accurate.[^langlois-seignobos-1898] Frevel calls a sweeping rejection of the Bible as a source for Israel's history "foolish".[^frevel-2023]
- [[Merneptah Stele]] shows a group named Israel in Canaan in the late 13th century, but not its size or exact location.[^finkelstein-mazar-2007]
- Ancient DNA adds little. Two Iron Age people from the inland cities of Megiddo and Abel Beth Maacah carry ancestry very similar to the region's Middle and Late Bronze Age people.[^agranat-tamir-2020] The authors read this as no necessary break at every site, and warn that two inland cities may not represent the others.[^agranat-tamir-2020]
