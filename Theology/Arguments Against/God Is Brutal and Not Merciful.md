# Description
The same God worshipped today commanded Israel to exterminate the Canaanites entirely, leaving no survivors [Deuteronomy 20:16–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2020:16-18&version=NIV), and issued numerous comparable orders throughout the Old Testament. This reveals a brutal deity, not the merciful God he is claimed to be.

# Based On
- [Deuteronomy 20:16–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2020:16-18&version=NIV) order to leave no survivors.
- [1 Samuel 15:2–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2015:2-3&version=NIV) order to destroy the Amalekites, including children.

# Countered By
- [[Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole]] the wording is genre, not a body count.
- [[God Delays Judgment to Allow Repentance]] the harsh outcomes arrive late, after long warning.
- [[The Covenant Changed with Christ]] the commands are not a standing rule and no later biblical writer treats them as one.
- The harsh order sits inside a war law that normally requires mercy. [Deuteronomy 20:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2020:10&version=NIV) says offer peace before attacking a city. [Deuteronomy 20:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2020:19&version=NIV) says don't cut down fruit trees even during a siege. The same chapter treats holding back as normal.
- The reason given is what the people did, not who they were. [Leviticus 18:24–25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2018:24-25&version=NIV) and [Deuteronomy 18:9–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2018:9-12&version=NIV) list things like burning children as offerings. [Deuteronomy 9:4–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%209:4-5&version=NIV) tells Israel straight out they aren't getting the land for being good. So it's told as judgment on behaviour, not favouritism.
- The goal in the text is to push the people out, not kill them all. [Exodus 23:28–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2023:28-30&version=NIV) and [Deuteronomy 7:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%207:22&version=NIV) have the nations driven out slowly. Across the conquest texts, "drive out" wording shows up far more often than "destroy" wording.
- It isn't about race. Rahab and her family live [Joshua 6:25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%206:25&version=NIV), the Gibeonites live by asking for a deal [Joshua 9:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%209:15&version=NIV), and Ruth from Moab is welcomed. Israel gets the same warning: [Leviticus 18:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2018:28&version=NIV) says the land will spit them out too, and the exile is told as that happening.

# Limits
- The counters mostly address the victory reports. The order in [1 Samuel 15:2–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2015:2-3&version=NIV) names children specifically, and none of the above touches that. It is the sharpest point in the argument and should be kept front and centre rather than buried in the general case.
- The argument is about the text, so it holds even if the events never happened. [[Joshua's Conquest Lacks Archaeological Evidence]] does not help here and arguably makes it worse, since it means the commands were written down deliberately rather than recorded.
