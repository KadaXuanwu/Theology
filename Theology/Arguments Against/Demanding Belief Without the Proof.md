# Description
God stays hidden, gives no clear proof, and still holds people responsible for not believing. The unfairness is not that proof is impossible in principle. By the Bible's own account God repeatedly gives it. Thomas got to touch the wounds. Paul, who was actively hostile, got a direct appearance on the road. Elijah got fire from the sky in front of a crowd. Exodus even states the purpose out loud, that people would hear and trust.

So the standard reply, that visible proof would destroy free choice, does not hold. Those people kept their freedom. The question is why the same evidence is given to a few and withheld from everyone else, while the penalty for not believing falls on everyone.

# Based On
- The claim that God wants a freely chosen response, which is the usual reason given for staying hidden. See [[Coerced Love Isn't Love]].
- [Exodus 19:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2019:9&version=NIV) God says he will come in a dense cloud so the people will hear and always trust. Producing trust through evidence is stated as the goal.
- [1 Kings 18:38–39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2018:38-39&version=NIV) fire falls in front of a crowd and the people respond.
- [John 20:27–29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2020:27-29&version=NIV) Thomas is given physical proof on request.
- [Acts 9:3–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%209:3-6&version=NIV) Paul is given a direct appearance while actively persecuting Christians.
- [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]] is the premise this rests on: people not resisting God who still do not believe. Note the sharp version: Paul was resisting and got evidence anyway.

# Countered By
- [[Obedience Comes Before Evidence]] argues the sequence is deliberate, evidence after the step rather than before it.
- [[Personal Relationship with God]] answers that the evidence is available but only from inside, after the leap.
- [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]] denies the premise. The evidence is already there in creation.
- [[Hell as Self-Chosen Separation]] answers the penalty half rather than the evidence half.
- [John 20:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2020:29&version=NIV) blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe. The text itself treats belief without proof as the higher state, so the rarity of direct evidence may be deliberate rather than an oversight.
- Evidence does not produce trust. [James 2:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=James%202:19&version=NIV) says the demons believe and shudder. On this reading God is after allegiance, not agreement, and more proof would not deliver it.
- The biblical cases are all people with a role in the story, not ordinary believers, so they may be exceptions by design rather than a standard God is failing to meet.

# Limits
- The Elijah case cuts against the argument as much as for it. The crowd that saw fire fall went back to Baal within a chapter, which is the James 2:19 point in narrative form.
- It assumes the biblical accounts of Thomas, Paul and Elijah are accurate. Someone who doubts the evidence for God generally cannot lean too hard on those same texts as a record of what happened.
