# Description
[Romans 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:20&version=NIV) says God's power and nature have been clear since creation, so people are without excuse. [Romans 2:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202:14-15&version=NIV) extends this to people who never had the Law, so the claim covers someone who never heard of Jesus.

Paul is partly right about the instinct. Human minds do reach for agents and purpose. But the instinct does not land on a creator. Across a sample of 33 hunter-gatherer societies, animism was present in all of them and a creator deity in 39%. Theravāda Buddhism and Jainism are built without a creator at all. The one thing creation supposedly makes clear is the thing that keeps not showing up.

Two further problems. Even granting a faint pull, sensing that something is behind the world is not knowing which God or what he wants. Acting on it stays guesswork among hundreds of options, and punishing a wrong guess made on evidence that thin is not justice. Second, [Romans 1:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:18&version=NIV) says people suppress the truth, so a group reporting no sense of God counts as suppressing it. Every result confirms the claim, which means no result tests it.

Daniel Everett's fieldwork with the Pirahã is the closest anyone has come to running the test in practice, though it is one contested case and should be used as illustration, not proof.

# Based On
- [Romans 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:20&version=NIV) creation makes God's power and nature clear, so people are without excuse.
- [Romans 1:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:18&version=NIV) people suppress the truth.
- [Romans 1:21–23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:21-23&version=NIV) the target is idol worship, so Paul is arguing with religious people, not atheists.
- [Romans 2:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202:14-15&version=NIV) people without the Law still have its work on their conscience.
- [[Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016]] the forager numbers. Animism in all 33 societies, a creator in 39%, where the counted trait is a single all-powerful creator deity.
- [[Kelemen 2004]] the instinct itself is real, which is worth conceding rather than fighting. The gap between purpose-detection and a creator is where this argument lives.
- [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] the claim those two studies sit under.
- [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]] names the guesswork point: people not fighting God who still do not believe.
- Rupert Gethin, *The Foundations of Buddhism* (Oxford University Press, 1998), for Buddhism having no creator God. Marie-Hélène Gorisse, [Jaina Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaina-philosophy/), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2023), for Jain philosophers denying any such being.
- Daniel Everett went to the Amazon in 1977 to translate the Bible for the Pirahã. He asked who made the trees and who made the water, and was told nobody made them, they are just trees and water. When he pressed and spoke of a time before there were trees, they asked whether he had seen it. Asked later what colour Jesus was, he said he had never seen him, and nor had anyone still alive, so they asked why he was telling them about someone nobody had seen. His own account, from a 2009 talk printed [here](https://ffrf.org/fttoday/april-2010/articles-april-2010/the-pirahae-people-who-define-happiness-without-god/) in *Freethought Today*, April 2010, and at book length in *Don't Sleep, There Are Snakes* (Pantheon, 2008).
- Losing his faith cost Everett his marriage and years of contact with two of his three children, who got back in touch only in 2008: Patrick Barkham, [The power of speech](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2008/nov/10/daniel-everett-amazon), *The Guardian*, 10 November 2008. That rules out the reply that he took a convenient exit.

# Countered By
- [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]] is the argument this one is aimed at.
- [[Personal Relationship with God]] relocates the evidence to after the commitment rather than before it.
- [[Obedience Comes Before Evidence]] gives that relocation its scriptural case.
- [[Hell as Self-Chosen Separation]] answers the fairness half rather than the empirical half.
- Paul's claim may be about culpability, not about everyone succeeding at the inference. That blunts the "nobody found it" objection but leaves the fairness objection intact.
- The strongest counter is that Everett's cultural claims are disputed. Andrew Nevins, David Pesetsky and Cilene Rodrigues, [Pirahã Exceptionality: A Reassessment](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/94631), *Language* 85 (2009), 355–404, quote Marco Antônio Gonçalves, who spent 18 months with the Pirahã across eight years (1986–1993), on a cosmology in which a demiurge called Igagai remade the world after a cataclysm.
- Everett answered in the same issue, [*Language* 85 (2009), 405–442](https://daneverettbooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/everett_09_Pirah-Culture-.pdf), that the passage is Gonçalves' own reconstruction, not anything a Pirahã narrated, and quoted a letter from Gonçalves saying they build a cosmology without myths. Nevins and his co-authors replied in [*Language* 85 (2009), 671–681](http://lingphil.mit.edu/papers/pesetsk/Nevins_Pesetsky_Rodrigues_2_Evidence_and_Argumentation_Reply_to_Everett.pdf) that they had described it that way themselves, from Gonçalves too. What is contested is whether that cosmology counts as a creation story, not whether Igagai gets talked about.
- The Pirahã are not irreligious. Everett's own description has them talking to and imitating spirits, wearing necklaces to ward off ones they see almost daily, and once crowding the riverbank to point out a being he could not see, which he transcribes under the same name, igagaí. He says they claim immediate experience of spirits. What is missing is a creator, which is narrower than no God-belief. Daniel Everett, [Cultural Constraints on Grammar and Cognition in Pirahã](http://www.biolinguagem.com/ling_cog_cult/everett_2005_grammar_cognition_piraha.pdf), *Current Anthropology* 46 (2005), 621–646.

# Limits
- The forager evidence has its own problems, kept in [[Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016]] rather than repeated here. Short version: 33 recent societies, and high gods is one of the two traits fitting the paper's own phylogeny worst, so the 39% is among its least stable numbers.
- Matthew Braddock, [Natural Nonbelief in God](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003026419-10), in Diego Machuca (ed.), *Evolutionary Debunking Arguments* (Routledge, 2022), 160–184, argues the empirical jury is still out on what prehistoric humans believed.
- The Pirahã case is one contested ethnography. Illustration, not proof.
