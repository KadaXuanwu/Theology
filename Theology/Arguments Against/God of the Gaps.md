# Description
Where an explanation is missing, God gets put in the empty slot. When the explanation turns up, the slot is filled by something else. So the appeal was never an explanation. It marked the place where one was still needed.

The name for the move comes from inside Christianity. Henry Drummond, in 1894, wrote of "reverent minds who ceaselessly scan the fields of Nature and the books of Science in search of gaps" to fill with God, and asked "As if God lived in gaps?"[^drummond-1894] His worry was what happens next: "If God is only to be left to the gaps in our knowledge, where shall we be when these gaps are filled up?" Drummond never writes the fixed phrase in the book, and a full text search of the 1894 printing returns no hit for it.[^drummond-1894] The earliest fixed use found here is E. W. Barnes, Bishop of Birmingham, in *Scientific Theory and Religion* (1933), the printed form of his Gifford Lectures, on men who looked for God at the first appearance of life or mind: "They have made him God of the gaps in human knowledge."[^barnes-1933] Charles Coulson put the phrase into wide circulation in 1955 with "There is no 'God of the gaps'", adding that gaps of that kind have "the unpreventable habit of shrinking".[^coulson-1955] Dietrich Bonhoeffer reached the same thought in German from a prison cell in 1944, writing to Eberhard Bethge that "Gott ist kein Lückenbüßer", God is no stopgap, and that God is to be found in what we do understand rather than in what we do not.[^bonhoeffer-1944]

One worked example holds up under checking. In Query 31 of the *Opticks* Newton wrote that irregularities in the planetary system "will be apt to increase, till this System wants a Reformation".[^newton-opticks] Leibniz went straight at it in 1715: on this account "God Almighty wants to wind up his Watch from Time to Time: Otherwise it would cease to move", and a workman is the less skilful the more often he has to mend his own work.[^leibniz-clarke] Samuel Clarke, answering for Newton, footnoted the place he took Leibniz to mean, and it is the Query 31 one.[^leibniz-clarke] Laplace later closed that hole. Work presented in 1773 showed the planets' mean distances from the sun do not drift, and in 1785 he resolved the long standing mismatch between Jupiter and Saturn as a slow swing of about nine hundred years.[^laskar-2010] The famous line to Napoleon, that he had no need of that hypothesis, was denied by Hervé Faye, who prints the story and then writes "Mais Laplace n'a jamais dit cela."[^faye-1896] Faye's own reading is worth more than the quip: what Laplace rejected was Newton's occasional repair, not God. "Ce n'était pas Dieu qu'il traitait d'hypothèse, mais son intervention directe en un point déterminé."[^faye-1896]

Put in current terms by Sean Carroll: "Over the past five hundred years, the progress of science has worked to strip away God's roles in the world", and taken as an account of nature, appeal to God "is, by scientific standards, not a very successful theory".[^carroll-2012] Under the historical case sits a claim about minds. [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] holds the pull to read intent behind unexplained events, and [[Kelemen 2004]] the finding that the reach for purpose arrives before any evidence and survives training, resurfacing in professional scientists under time pressure. The conclusion drawn is that reaching for God at an unexplained point is a default setting of the mind, and a default is not a finding.

# Countered By
- The strong counter is Robert Larmer's. Gap arguments as actually made are not arguments from ignorance, because they carry a second premise, that if a natural cause could do this job there would by now be some sign of it. That makes the form modus tollens, and the live question becomes whether the premise is true. "I conclude that there is nothing wrong with the reasoning typically involved in 'God of the gaps' arguments".[^larmer-2002] He grants that reasoning "solely or simply on the basis of ignorance" would be the fallacy. Nothing answering him in print was found.
- [[God Is the Reason Anything Exists at All]] is the standard reply: the argument attacks a bad version of theism rather than the classical one, where God is the reason there is anything at all rather than the cause of particular unexplained events. That version does not shrink as science advances because it was never occupying a gap. It concedes less than it looks, since what survives covers the contingency argument rather than theism as practised.
- Kojonen answers the pedigree point. Bonhoeffer's case was pastoral, about what gap apologetics costs a church, and "these pastoral arguments, if taken in isolation, are about the practical consequences and usefulness of gaps arguments, rather than their validity and force". He concludes that the critique "is substantially weaker than is commonly assumed".[^kojonen-2016]
- Del Ratzsch and Jeffrey Koperski write in the Stanford Encyclopedia that whether there are causal gaps in nature "would seem to be a legitimate empirical question".[^sep-teleological] Ratzsch has argued elsewhere that after long enough failure a gap conclusion could in principle be warranted, and he co-authors that entry.
- Alvin Plantinga rejects the picture from within Christian philosophy, calling a God "postulated to explain what can't be explained otherwise" something "worlds apart from serious Christian theism", writing in an intelligent design journal.[^plantinga-1997]

# Limits
- A pattern of past retreats does not guarantee the next one, and it only reaches gaps resembling the ones already closed. The Stanford Encyclopedia puts the record at "at least a cautionary note",[^sep-teleological] and Kojonen adds that there is "much less inductive support" for cases strongly disanalogous to those science has solved before.[^kojonen-2016] Lightning is an event inside the universe. The origin of the universe is a different kind of question, and treating them as one case is the weakest step here.
- The Zeus comparison is weaker than it looks. Aristotle explains thunder and lightning as dry exhalation forced out of condensing cloud and gives no god a causal role, arguing against still earlier natural accounts.[^aristotle-meteor] Seneca sets a natural and an Etruscan divine reading of the same lightning side by side as two live accounts, the Etruscans referring everything to the will of God.[^seneca-nq] Schlegel's survey of encyclopedia articles finds the electrical theory displacing Aristotle, not Zeus.[^schlegel-2024] The two kinds of account ran alongside each other for centuries rather than one replacing the other.
- The appeal to progress cuts both ways, and Larmer says so: "this line of argument must grant the possibility that the progress of science has strengthened, rather than weakened, 'God of the gaps' arguments."[^larmer-2002] The cases he offers as widened gaps are a remark by the palaeontologist David Raup and work from intelligent design authors, but the structural point does not rest on them.
- The record is thinner than the argument assumes. Andrew Dickson White's account of English clergy refusing Franklin's lightning rods rests on one footnote pointing at Priestley's *History of Electricity*, and those pages carry the physics and no religious objection.[^white-1896][^priestley-1775] Colin Russell reports that White's book "is no longer regarded as even a reliable secondary source for historical study".[^russell-2002] Douglas McKie called the story of Wöhler's 1828 urea synthesis killing vitalism a legend.[^mckie-1944] No historian was found saying from period evidence that Laplace's result was taken at the time as removing a divine role. [[The Royal Touch]] is the closest thing to a clean case, an official divine mechanism for a named illness, and it does not fit the pattern either: the rite was dropped in 1732, 150 years before the bacterial cause was found.
- It pulls against the unfalsifiability complaint. A track record of retreat only exists if the claims that retreated were contentful enough to be tested and beaten, and [[No Outcome Counts Against It]] says the claims at issue rule nothing out. Both cannot hold of the same claim at the same time, so this node owes an answer on which ones it takes to have been testable. No published source was found joining the two halves, so the tension is the vault's own reading rather than a sourced objection.
- The psychology cuts against the mechanism. Cristine Legare and colleagues report natural and supernatural explanations coexisting in the same people for the same events across cultures, the supernatural ones often growing with age rather than fading.[^legare-2012] The best known experiment tying analytic thinking to disbelief failed a preregistered replication, "little to no effect" at N = 941,[^sanchez-2017] and a 13 country test led by one of its authors found analytic atheism "quite fickle cross-culturally", holding in three of them.[^gervais-2018]
