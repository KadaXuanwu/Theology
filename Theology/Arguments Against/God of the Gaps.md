# Description
Where an explanation is missing, God gets put in the empty slot. When the explanation turns up, the explanatory slot is filled by something else. The appeals were offered as explanations that could have been right, which is why losing counts against them.[^boudry-2010] Those since settled went to natural accounts that explain more, which gives good grounds to expect the same at the next gap of that kind.[^dawes-2011][^kojonen-2016] So an appeal to God there is not evidence for God. It marks the place where an explanation is still needed.

The name for the move comes from inside Christianity. [[Henry Drummond]] wrote in 1894 of "reverent minds who ceaselessly scan the fields of Nature and the books of Science in search of gaps" to fill with God. He asked "As if God lived in gaps?", and where we shall be "when these gaps are filled up". His answer was "an immanent God, which is the God of Evolution".[^drummond-1894] Drummond never writes the fixed phrase in the book.[^drummond-1894] The earliest fixed use found so far is [[E. W. Barnes]], Bishop of Birmingham, in the revised printed form of his Gifford Lectures (1933): "They have made him God of the gaps in human knowledge". In the same passage he refused God as the trigger of the cosmic beginning too.[^barnes-1933] [[C. A. Coulson|Charles Coulson]], often credited with the phrase, wrote in 1955 "There is no 'God of the gaps'", adding that gaps of that kind have "the unpreventable habit of shrinking".[^simmons-2003][^coulson-1955] [[Dietrich Bonhoeffer]] wrote to [[Eberhard Bethge]] from prison in May 1944 that God must not figure "als Lückenbüßer unserer unvollkommenen Erkenntnis", as a stopgap for our incomplete knowledge.[^bonhoeffer-1944]

In Query 31 of the *Opticks* [[Isaac Newton|Newton]] called it "unphilosophical to seek for any other Origin of the World ... by the mere Laws of Nature".[^newton-opticks] The *General Scholium* of the *Principia* argues that the planets "could by no means have at first deriv'd the regular position of the orbits themselves from those laws".[^newton-scholium] The natural answer was the [[Nebular hypothesis|nebular hypothesis]], and planetary science now reads the flat structure of the solar system as the mark of formation in one disk.[^kley-2017] After it, "it could look as if Newton's God had been made redundant".[^brooke-1991] Query 31 also says small irregularities "will be apt to increase, till this System wants a Reformation".[^newton-opticks] [[Pierre-Simon Laplace|Laplace]] showed in 1773 that the planets' mean distances from the sun do not drift. In 1785 he resolved the mismatch between Jupiter and Saturn as a slow swing of about nine hundred years. The 1773 result held only to a first approximation, and the planets' long run stability is now answered in probabilities.[^laskar-2010] In 1813 Laplace denied that the planets' mutual attraction could upset their stability, "comme Newton le suppose", as Newton supposed it could. He granted that other causes would in the long run make "une réforme" necessary. [[Final cause|Final causes]], he added, are "l'expression de l'ignorance où nous sommes, des véritables causes", the expression of our ignorance of the true causes.[^laplace-1813]

[[Gregory W. Dawes|Gregory Dawes]] states the record: theistic explanations "have repeatedly been replaced by natural explanations of greater explanatory power".[^dawes-2011] His example is [[Charles Darwin|Darwin]]. [[William Paley|Paley]] had inferred a designer from the eye's fit to the laws of optics.[^paley-1802] Darwin gave a stepwise natural account of the eye, and the Stanford Encyclopedia calls his theory "the most obvious example" of a gap that closed.[^darwin-1859][^sep-teleological] Beside the history sits an observation about minds that the argument does not need as a premise. In professional physical scientists under time pressure a bias to explain natural things by purpose returned.[^kelemen-2013] In 3,655 Irish folk cures from 1937 and 1938, diseases two doctors rated as having causes or mechanisms unclear to a patient drew more religious or supernatural cures. The link was clearest for cures with no Christian element.[^de-barra-2025] A default is not a finding: it explains why the appeal comes easily, not whether it is true.[^barrett-2007]

# Countered By
- The strong counter is that the record reaches only gaps like the ones that closed. [[Erkki V. R. Kojonen|Kojonen]] grants "strong inductive grounds" where a new case closely resembles a solved one, and "much less inductive support" where it is "strongly disanalogous" (p. 300).[^kojonen-2016] [[Miles K. Donahue|Miles Donahue]] agrees that the origin of the universe and of consciousness are "quite distinct from planetary motion" (p. 1995).[^donahue-2025] His list of closed cases includes the arrangement of the planets and the origin of species (p. 1994).[^donahue-2025] [[Robert Larmer]] presses it for origins: success with how nature works "cannot automatically be taken" as warrant about how things came to be (pp. 18–19).[^larmer-2019] He counts the origin and development of life among these questions (p. 18).[^larmer-2019] There, he holds, "any inductive argument regarding cosmological and biological origins seems to be on the side of design inferences" (p. 19).[^larmer-2019]
- Larmer argues that gap arguments as actually made are not [[Argument from ignorance|arguments from ignorance]], because they carry a second premise.[^larmer-2002] In his words, "we know enough about the operation of natural causes" to think it "unlikely, or perhaps impossible" that they account for such gaps. That makes the form [[Modus tollens|modus tollens]], his own term, and the live question becomes whether the premise is true. "I conclude that there is nothing wrong with the reasoning typically involved in 'God of the gaps' arguments".[^larmer-2002] He grants that reasoning "solely or simply on the basis of ignorance" would be the fallacy (p. 131).[^larmer-2002] No published rebuttal of the formal point was found. One later paper endorses it.[^leidenhag-2019] Another grants it for argument's sake and contests the premise for one gap, the Big Bang singularity.[^pitts-2008]
- Dawes and a co-author argued in 2018 that defending science's naturalism by pointing to superseded theistic explanations "is inconsistent with the history of the sciences".[^dawes-2018] On their account the sciences "have never invoked divine agency", so it is not the case that they "once employed theistic explanations and then abandoned them".[^dawes-2018]
- [[God Is the Reason Anything Exists at All]] is the standard reply: the argument attacks a bad version of theism rather than the classical one. On the classical view God is the reason there is anything at all, not the cause of particular unexplained events. That version does not shrink as science advances because it was never occupying a gap (pp. 299–300).[^kojonen-2016] [[Michał Heller]]'s view is reported in one Polish survey (pp. 112–113).[^oleksowicz-2014] Every gap is temporary, on that view, except three: why there is anything, why the world is intelligible, and what meaning and value it has.[^oleksowicz-2014] In that form the reply grants the argument for every gap inside nature.[^oleksowicz-2014]
- Kojonen answers the pedigree point. "Pastoral" is his label for the apologetic critique he opens with Bonhoeffer. Taken in isolation, he writes, such arguments "are about the practical consequences and usefulness of gaps arguments, rather than their validity and force" (p. 307). His overall verdict is that the critique "is substantially weaker than is commonly assumed" (p. 291).[^kojonen-2016] What came from inside Christianity, in Drummond and in an essay in the Anglican volume *Lux Mundi*, was an argument for an immanent God against an occasional one.[^drummond-1894][^moore-1890]
- [[Del Ratzsch]] and [[Jeffrey Koperski]] write in the Stanford Encyclopedia that whether there are causal gaps in nature "would seem to be a legitimate empirical question".[^sep-teleological] Ratzsch has argued elsewhere that after long enough failure a gap conclusion could in principle be warranted, and he co-authors that entry.[^kojonen-2016]
- [[Alvin Plantinga]] rejects the picture from within Christian philosophy. Gap theology, as he describes it, treats God as "a kind of large-scale hypothesis postulated to explain what cannot be explained otherwise". His verdict is that it "is worlds apart from serious Christian theism".[^plantinga-1997]

# Limits
- Everything turns on whether today's gaps, the origin of life, the beginning and fine tuning of the universe, and consciousness, are the same kind as those that closed. The Stanford Encyclopedia puts the record at "at least a cautionary note".[^sep-teleological] Kojonen sets gap arguments beside arguments from a limit science cannot cross. He says the line between them "comes quite close to the distinction between arguments we find plausible and arguments we find implausible" (p. 302).[^kojonen-2016] The Stanford Encyclopedia's entry on cosmology and theology calls whether the big bang was the beginning of the universe "an empirical, rather than a metaphysical, question".[^halvorson-2021] A review of the philosophy of cosmology argues that tested physics cannot settle it, because the answer turns on quantum gravity, and that testable physics may never settle it either.[^ellis-2007] The same review puts the question why this universe rather than another outside testable physics.[^ellis-2007] On that second question the record says nothing. A laboratory route from simple chemistry to a self copying, evolving system is still among the field's "major unresolved challenges".[^sole-2025] Such a route would put the origin of life with the closed cases. Long research in which the difficulty kept growing, the case Ratzsch describes, would count the other way.[^kojonen-2016]
- The appeal to progress cuts both ways, and Larmer says so. In his words, it "must grant the possibility that the progress of science has strengthened, rather than weakened, 'God of the gaps' arguments".[^larmer-2002] Plantinga made the same point in 1997 about the origin of life.[^plantinga-1997] Larmer's widened gaps on the origin of life are a remark by the palaeontologist [[David Raup]] and an argument from a design movement volume.[^larmer-2002] Raup in that article affirms that evolution occurred.[^raup-1979] The structural point does not rest on them.
- The record is thinner than the argument assumes. A 2025 study finds a natural philosopher who put God's direct action in a gap, the development of the embryo, a move some theologians recognised as "highly unusual".[^henry-2025] In 1804 an Edinburgh professor of natural philosophy read the proof that the planetary system is stable as "this mark of beneficent wisdom in the Contriver".[^robison-1804] Newton scholars disagree on the agent of the Query 31 "Reformation": one reads it as God, another finds that Newton never names it.[^snobelen-2012][^torrance-2023] The Stanford Encyclopedia reports that early modern natural philosophers such as Newton "regularly appealed to supernatural agents in their natural philosophy", while favouring natural explanations overall.[^de-cruz-2022]
- It pulls against the unfalsifiability complaint, and the argument takes the tested horn. A track record of retreat only exists if the claims that retreated were contentful enough to be tested and beaten. One statement of the case says so: rejecting supernatural explanations for past failure "entails that, at least in some sense, they might have succeeded".[^boudry-2010] Donahue poses the fork, asking whether such explanations are rejected "because they are non-science or merely failed science" (p. 1996). His answer is that such appeals are not science, yet the historical ones failed "because they were bad explanations more generally" (p. 1995).[^donahue-2025] [[No Outcome Counts Against It]] covers the versions that stop risking anything, not the claims that retreated.
- The psychology cuts against the mechanism. [[Cristine H. Legare|Cristine Legare]] and colleagues report natural and supernatural explanations coexisting in the same people for the same events across cultures, the supernatural ones often growing with age rather than fading.[^legare-2012] In a South African study, bewitchment explanations were "neither the result of ignorance nor replaced by biological explanations".[^legare-2008] Discussing HIV blamed on witchcraft, the authors of the Irish cures study call the two accounts "not competing explanations for the same level of causation".[^de-barra-2025] Closing a mechanism gap does not reach the question why me.
