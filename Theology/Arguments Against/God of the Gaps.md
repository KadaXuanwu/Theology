# Description
Whenever people can't explain something, they reach for God as the answer. The ancient Greeks said Zeus made lightning because they didn't understand weather. Christians say God made the universe because we don't yet have the full science. In both cases, God is just a placeholder for a gap in knowledge. As science explains more, those gaps keep shrinking, which suggests God was never the real explanation, only a stand-in for what we hadn't figured out yet.

# Based On
- [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] explains why the reach for an agent happens in the first place, which is the mechanism this argument describes.
- [[Kelemen 2004]] the specific finding: the reach for purpose comes before any evidence and persists even in trained scientists.
- Needs the historical half sourced. The phrase itself comes from Henry Drummond in the 1890s and was pressed hardest by Dietrich Bonhoeffer, both of them Christians warning other Christians off the move, which is worth noting because it means the objection is not originally an atheist one.

# Countered By
- Needs writing. The standard reply is that the argument attacks a bad version of theism rather than the classical one, where God is the reason there is anything at all rather than the cause of particular unexplained events. That version does not shrink as science advances because it was never occupying a gap.

# Limits
- A pattern of past retreats does not guarantee the next one. "Science has explained things before" is an induction, not a proof that everything currently unexplained will be explained.
- The Zeus comparison is doing rhetorical work the argument has not earned. Lightning is an event inside the universe; the origin of the universe is a different kind of question, and treating them as the same case is the argument's weakest step.
