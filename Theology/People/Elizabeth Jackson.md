# Description
Elizabeth Jackson is a philosopher at Saint Louis University working in epistemology and philosophy of religion, mainly on belief and credence, [[Blaise Pascal|Pascal]]'s wager and the nature of faith.[^slu-jackson][^cv-jackson] Her answer to [[J. L. Schellenberg]]'s hiddenness argument is that, since there is strong prudential reason to believe in God, many non-believers count as resistant, so there is no good reason to think there are very many [[Non-resistant non-belief|non-resistant non-believers]].[^jackson-2016]

- "A Permissivist Defense of Pascal's Wager", *Erkenntnis* 88/6 (2023), 2315–2340.[^jackson-2023-erkenntnis]

- "Doxastic Voluntarism", with Mark Boespflug, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2024).[^boespflug-2024]

# Stated Position
In a 2021 interview she said that in her own case the beauty of Christianity has played at least as large a role as the traditional arguments for God's existence.[^licon-2021] She said she is compelled by the beauty of the incarnation, life, atonement and resurrection of Jesus.[^licon-2021] In the same interview she said that "faith is an important part of most of my most central commitments", and she named religious commitment among them.[^licon-2021] She names no church or tradition there.
