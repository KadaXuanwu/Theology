# Description
John Stuart Mill was an English philosopher who worked for the East India Company in London from 1823 until 1858 and was MP for Westminster from 1865 to 1868.[^sep-mill][^mill-autobiography] In *On Liberty* he observes that the same causes which make a man a Churchman in London would have made him a Buddhist or a Confucian in Pekin.[^mill-on-liberty]

# Work
- *A System of Logic* (1843).[^sep-mill]
- *On Liberty* (1859).[^sep-mill]
- *Utilitarianism* (1861).[^sep-mill]
- *Three Essays on Religion* (1874, published after his death).[^mill-three-essays]

# Stated Position
In "Theism", the last of the *Three Essays on Religion*, he concludes that "the rational attitude of a thinking mind towards the supernatural, whether in natural or in revealed religion, is that of scepticism as distinguished from belief on the one hand, and from atheism on the other" (Part V).[^mill-three-essays]
