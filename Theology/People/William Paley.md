# Description
William Paley was an English clergyman and Archdeacon of Carlisle whose books argued for the existence of God from design in nature and for the truth of Christianity from its evidences.[^dnb-paley]

# Work
- *The Principles of Moral and Political Philosophy* (1785).[^dnb-paley]
- *Horae Paulinae* (1790).[^dnb-paley]
- *A View of the Evidences of Christianity* (1794).[^dnb-paley]
- *Natural Theology* (1802), his last book.[^dnb-paley] It argues from a watch's contrivance to a maker, and holds that it would not "bring any uncertainty into the argument" if there were parts whose working "we could not discover, or had not yet discovered".[^paley-1802]

# Stated Position
In the *Evidences*: "The truth of Christianity depends upon its leading facts, and upon them alone." Of the three facts he names, he writes: "These three facts, I think, are certain, and would have been nearly so, if the Gospels had never been written."[^paley-1851]
