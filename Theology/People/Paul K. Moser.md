# Description
Paul K. Moser is a philosopher who taught at Loyola University Chicago from 1983 and has been professor emeritus there since 2024, working on epistemology and the philosophy of religion.[^moser-cv] He argues that a person can be responsible, through their own stance, for lacking evidence of God that is available to them (pp. 50–51).[^moser-2013]

# Work
- *Knowledge and Evidence* (1989).[^moser-cv]
- *Divine Hiddenness: New Essays*, edited with [[Daniel Howard-Snyder]] (2002).[^moser-cv]
- *The Elusive God: Reorienting Religious Epistemology* (2008).[^moser-cv]
- *The Evidence for God: Religious Knowledge Reexamined* (2010).[^moser-cv]
- "God and Evidence: A Cooperative Approach", *European Journal for Philosophy of Religion* 5/2 (2013), 47–61.[^moser-2013]

# Stated Position
None found in print on the truth of Christianity. On evidence, his university quotes him saying that theological beliefs without evidence "are little better than wishful thinking".[^luc-moser]
