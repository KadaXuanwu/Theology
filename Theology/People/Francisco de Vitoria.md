# Description
Francisco de Vitoria was a Spanish Dominican theologian and jurist who held the senior chair of theology at Salamanca, and whose lectures on the Spanish conquest of the Americas are a founding text of the law of nations.[^britannica-vitoria] The vault cites him for the ruling that a bare announcement of the faith, without proofs or arguments, does not oblige a hearer to believe.[^bullivant-2011]

# Work
- *De Potestate Civili*, delivered 1528.[^sep-salamanca]
- *De Indis*, delivered 1539.[^sep-salamanca]
- *De Iure Belli*, delivered 1539.[^sep-salamanca]
- *Relectiones Theologicae* (Lyon, 1557), the posthumous collection in which the lectures were published.[^britannica-vitoria]

# Stated Position
On hearers who were told and not persuaded, he holds they "are not bound to believe from the first announcement of the Christian faith, in the sense of sinning mortally by not believing due to this alone: because it is merely announced and proposed to them ... without miracles or any other proofs or arguments".[^bullivant-2011]
