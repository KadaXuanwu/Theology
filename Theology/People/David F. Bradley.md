# Description
David F. Bradley is a psychologist who studies atheists and agnostics, including the reasons they give for not believing in a god or gods.[^bradley-2014][^rsp-bradley] He trained at Boston College in history and mental health counseling, and took an M.A. and a Ph.D. in psychology at Case Western Reserve University.[^rsp-bradley][^bradley-2014][^bradley-2018-dissertation]

# Work
- *The Reasons of Atheists/Agnostics for Nonbelief in God's Existence Scale: Development and Initial Validation*, MA thesis, Case Western Reserve University (2014). Its sample was 520 adults from Amazon's Mechanical Turk, and its factor analysis gave nine factors.[^bradley-2014]
- "The God of Nonbelievers: Characteristics of a Hypothetical God", *Science, Religion and Culture* 2/3 (2015), 120–130.[^bradley-2015]
- "Perceived Addiction to Internet Pornography among Religious Believers and Nonbelievers", *Sexual Addiction & Compulsivity* 23/2–3 (2016), 225–243.[^bradley-2016]
- "Relational reasons for nonbelief in the existence of gods: An important adjunct to intellectual nonbelief", *Psychology of Religion and Spirituality* 9/4 (2017), 319–327.[^bradley-2017]
- "God Belief as an Innate Aspect of Human Nature: A Response to John Shook and Questions for [[Justin Barrett]]", *Method & Theory in the Study of Religion* 29/4–5 (2017), 387–399, with Bradley as second author.[^exline-2017]
- *Sensitivity to Potential Anti-Atheist Discrimination Events: Psychological Correlates and Relationship with Psychological Well-Being*, PhD dissertation, Case Western Reserve University (2018). It builds a ten item measure of how readily atheists read hypothetical events as discrimination and tests it on 441 self identified atheist adults in the United States.[^bradley-2018-dissertation]
- "The Reasons of Atheists and Agnostics for Nonbelief in God's Existence Scale: Development and initial validation", *Psychology of Religion and Spirituality* 10/3 (2018), 263–275. It builds on the scale from his thesis and reports a second study that confirmed the nine factor structure.[^bradley-2018][^bradley-2018-preprint]
- "Agnostics Have More Spiritual Struggles Than Atheists, But More Positive Images, Emotions, and Motivations Around the Idea of God", *The International Journal for the Psychology of Religion* 36/3 (2026), 501–520, with Bradley as second author.[^exline-2026]

Julie J. Exline chaired both of his committees at Case Western Reserve.[^bradley-2014][^bradley-2018-dissertation]

The affiliation he gave was Case Western Reserve University up to his doctorate in 2018, Pittsburg State University on an author manuscript of 2020, and the Hanover Center for Cognitive Behavioral Therapies on a paper of 2026.[^bradley-2018-dissertation][^exline-2020-preprint][^exline-2026]

# Stated Position
In a 2014 essay he wrote: "This was my eighth Christmas as an atheist, after two decades of observance of the holiday as a Christian."[^bradley-2014-rsp]
