# Description
Charles Harold Dodd was a New Testament scholar and Congregational minister, and the first non-Anglican to hold a chair of divinity at Oxford or Cambridge.[^williams-2008] He held the Rylands chair at Manchester and the Norris-Hulse chair at Cambridge, sought unity in the teaching of the New Testament writings and directed the New English Bible.[^williams-2008]

# Work
- *The Authority of the Bible* (1928).[^williams-2008]
- *The Parables of the Kingdom* (1935).[^williams-2008]
- *The Apostolic Preaching and Its Developments* (1936).[^williams-2008]
- *The Interpretation of the Fourth Gospel* (1953).[^williams-2008]
- *Historical Tradition in the Fourth Gospel* (1963).[^williams-2008]

# Stated Position
In *The Authority of the Bible* he held that the Bible mediates truth through inspired individuals, through a community testing their ideas over generations and through the life of Jesus. He concluded that these "form a continuous history in which as a whole, even more clearly than in its several parts, a divine process of revelation can be discerned".[^dodd-1928]
