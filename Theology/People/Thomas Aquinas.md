# Description
Thomas Aquinas was an Italian Dominican friar who held a chair of theology at Paris and taught in Cologne, Rome and Naples. Robert Pasnau calls him the greatest figure of thirteenth century Europe in philosophy and theology.[^pasnau-sep]

# Work
- *Summa contra gentiles*, written 1259 to 1265.[^pasnau-sep]
- *Summa theologiae*, written 1267 to 1273 and left unfinished after question 90 of the third part. His disciples assembled a supplement from his earlier writing to carry it to its intended end.[^pasnau-sep]
- Commentaries on Aristotle, on Peter Lombard's *Sentences* and on books of the Bible, alongside the disputed questions of his teaching posts.[^pasnau-sep]

# Stated Position
Aquinas wrote from inside Catholic Christianity as a Dominican friar and priest, and the church later made his work its own: he was canonised on 18 July 1323, and in 1567 Pius V numbered him among the doctors of the church, a title until then kept for the ancient church fathers.[^pasnau-sep]

[^pasnau-sep]: Robert Pasnau, ["Thomas Aquinas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aquinas/), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 7 December 2022.
