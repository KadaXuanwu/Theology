# Description
Paul A. Macdonald Jr. is a professor of philosophy at the United States Air Force Academy who works on philosophy of religion, philosophical theology and the thought of [[Thomas Aquinas]].[^usafa-macdonald] His paper on the hiddenness argument says that [[J. L. Schellenberg]]'s premise that [[Non-resistant non-belief|non-resistant non-belief]] exists rests on an assumption that is no more reasonable to affirm than to withhold.[^macdonald-2021]

# Work
- *Knowledge and the Transcendent: An Inquiry into the Mind's Relationship to God* (2009). Its publisher describes it as a defence of the claim that the human mind is open to the world and to God, drawing on Aquinas.[^macdonald-2009]
- *Christian Theology and the Secular University* (2017). Its publisher describes it as arguing that Christian theology belongs in the secular university because it supplies resources the university needs for its main epistemic and educative ends.[^macdonald-2017]
- *God, Evil, and Redeeming Good: A Thomistic Theodicy* (2023). Its publisher describes it as a Thomistic, Christian theodicy, and says that in the author's view the existence of evil creates no intellectual problem that theists must solve to vindicate the rationality of theism.[^macdonald-2023]
- "Schellenberg's Noseeum Assumption about Nonresistant Nonbelief", *European Journal for Philosophy of Religion* 13/3 (2021), 139–156. The paper takes the noseeum assumption as [[Daniel Howard-Snyder]] and Michael Bergmann state it (p. 141) and applies it to Schellenberg's premise. He argues that we do not know enough to judge whether we would detect a non-believer's resistance toward God if it were there, so the noseeum argument for the premise is not a good one. He adds at p. 144 that this does not require claiming that non-resistant non-belief fails to exist.[^macdonald-2021]
