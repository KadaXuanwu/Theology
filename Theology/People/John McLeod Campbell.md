# Description
John McLeod Campbell was a Scottish minister, deposed by the Church of Scotland for teaching that Christ died for all and that assurance is of the essence of faith.[^dnb-campbell][^memorials] His book on the atonement holds that Christ's sufferings had their value from their spiritual nature, not from being penal.[^campbell-1856]

# Work
- *Sermons and Lectures* (Greenock, 1832).[^dnb-campbell]
- *Christ the Bread of Life* (Glasgow, 1851).[^fasti]
- *The Nature of the Atonement and its Relation to Remission of Sins and Eternal Life* (Cambridge: Macmillan, 1856).[^campbell-1856]
- *Thoughts on Revelation* (1862).[^dnb-campbell]

# Stated Position
In *The Nature of the Atonement* he writes that "love cannot be conceived of as doing anything gratuitously, merely to shew its own depth", and asks: "How was so costly an expression of love as the atonement necessary?" (p. 26).[^campbell-1856]
