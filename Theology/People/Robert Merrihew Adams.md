# Description
Robert Merrihew Adams was a philosopher of religion, ethics and Leibniz who taught at the University of Michigan, UCLA and Yale, and later at North Carolina and Rutgers.[^gifford-adams][^adams-obituary] The vault cites his account of the sin of unbelief as rejecting what God has said to a person, and his argument that blame can fit states that are not voluntary.[^adams-1984][^adams-1985]

# Work
- "The Virtue of Faith", *Faith and Philosophy* 1/1 (1984), 3–15.[^adams-1984]
- "Involuntary Sins", *The Philosophical Review* 94/1 (1985), 3–31.[^adams-1985]
- *The Virtue of Faith and Other Essays in Philosophical Theology* (1987).[^gifford-adams]
- *Leibniz: Determinist, Theist, Idealist* (1994).[^mansfield-adams]
- *Finite and Infinite Goods: A Framework for Ethics* (1999).[^mansfield-adams]

# Stated Position
In "The Virtue of Faith" he addresses the essay "to Christians" and counts himself among them: "As Christians we will say it is the testimony of the Holy Spirit" (p. 8). He writes that "I have never been an atheist" (p. 9), and that "it is not my aim here to justify Christian belief" (p. 3).[^adams-1984]
