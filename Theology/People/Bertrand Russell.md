# Description
Bertrand Russell was a British philosopher and logician, best known for his work in mathematical logic and analytic philosophy, including *Principia Mathematica* with Alfred North Whitehead.[^irvine-2024] He was a lecturer and later a fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge, and also taught in the United States.[^irvine-2024]

# Work
- "Pragmatism", *Edinburgh Review* 209/428 (1909), 363–388; reprinted in *Philosophical Essays* (Longmans, Green, 1910), 87–126.[^russell-1909]
- *Principia Mathematica*, with Alfred North Whitehead, 3 vols. (1910–1913).[^irvine-2024]
- *Why I Am Not a Christian* (Watts, 1927), first given as a lecture.[^irvine-2024][^russell-1927]
- *A History of Western Philosophy* (Simon and Schuster, 1945).[^irvine-2024]

# Stated Position
In "Why I Am Not a Christian" he set out to explain "first, why I do not believe in God and in immortality; and, secondly, why I do not think that Christ was the very best and wisest of men, although I grant him a very high degree of moral goodness".[^russell-1927]
