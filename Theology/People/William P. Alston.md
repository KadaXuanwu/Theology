# Description
William P. Alston was a philosopher who taught at the University of Michigan for twenty-two years and later at Syracuse University.[^eps-alston] He argued that a belief formed from experience of God has "precisely the status of individual perceptual beliefs".[^alston-1986]

# Work
- "Perceiving God", *Journal of Philosophy* 83/11 (1986), 655–665.[^alston-1986]
- "Epistemic Circularity", *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 47/1 (1986), 1–30.[^alston-1986-circularity]
- "Religious Diversity and Perceptual Knowledge of God", *Faith and Philosophy* 5/4 (1988), 433–448.[^alston-1988]
- *Perceiving God: The Epistemology of Religious Experience* (1991).[^alston-1991]

# Stated Position
In an autobiographical essay he wrote that "my way back was not by abstract philosophical reasoning but by experience" (p. 28).[^alston-1994-way]
