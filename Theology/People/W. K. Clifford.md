# Description
William Kingdon Clifford was an English mathematician and philosopher, a fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge, and professor of applied mathematics and mechanics at University College London.[^clifford-1879a][^chignell-2018] His essay "The Ethics of Belief" set out the rule that believing anything on insufficient evidence is always wrong, and with James's reply it became "the touchstone for later discussions" of the ethics of belief.[^clifford-1877][^chignell-2018]

# Work
- "The Ethics of Belief", *Contemporary Review* 29 (1877), 289–309.[^clifford-1877]
- "Applications of Grassmann's Extensive Algebra", *American Journal of Mathematics* 1/4 (1878), from p. 350.[^clifford-1878]
- *Lectures and Essays*, ed. Leslie Stephen and Frederick Pollock, 2 vols. (Macmillan, 1879).[^clifford-1879]
- *The Common Sense of the Exact Sciences* (1885), completed by Karl Pearson after his death.[^mactutor-clifford]

# Stated Position
In "The Ethics of Belief" he wrote: "it is wrong always, everywhere, and for any one, to believe anything upon insufficient evidence."[^clifford-1877] In an 1877 symposium on the decline of religious belief he wrote that people who learn the nature of God from the moral sense of man "cannot go on believing the doctrines of popular theology".[^clifford-1879] In the same piece he warned that "sacerdotal Christianity", if it returned to power, "must be expected to produce worse evils than those which it has worked in the past".[^clifford-1879]
