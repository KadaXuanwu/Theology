# Description
John Wesley was an evangelist and the leader of Methodism, a priest and a fellow of Lincoln College, Oxford.[^dnb-wesley] He preached that the grace of God is "free in all, and free for all", against the doctrine of predestination.[^wesley-free-grace]

# Work
- "Free Grace", a sermon preached at Bristol, Sermon 128 in Thomas Jackson's edition.[^wesley-free-grace][^wesley-chronology]
- *The Arminian Magazine*, founded 1778.[^dnb-wesley]
- "On Working Out Our Own Salvation" (1785), Sermon 85 in Jackson's edition.[^wesley-85][^wesley-chronology]

# Stated Position
In "Free Grace" he writes that the doctrine of predestination "represents the most holy God as worse than the devil, as both more false, more cruel, and more unjust", and "I abhor the doctrine of predestination" (§§25, 27).[^wesley-free-grace] In "On Working Out Our Own Salvation" he holds that "no man sins because he has not grace, but because he does not use the grace which he hath" (III.4).[^wesley-85]

It calls him "evangelist and leader of methodism" and gives his birth at Epworth Rectory on 17 June 1703, his election as fellow of Lincoln College on 17 March 1726, his ordination as priest in 1728, his mission to Georgia, his preaching at Bristol and at the Foundery in London, the founding of the *Arminian Magazine* in 1778, and his death at the chapel house in City Road, London, on 2 March 1791.
