# Description
Eusebius was bishop of Caesarea in Palestine, where he had studied with Pamphilus, a presbyter who kept a theological school and a large library there.[^mcgiffert-1910][^eusebius-he67] His *Church History*, issued in several editions, sorts the writings claimed for the New Testament into accepted, disputed and rejected books, and sets heretical forgeries apart from all three.[^barnes-1980][^eusebius-he3]

# Work
- *Chronicle*, in two editions, the second running to Constantine's twentieth anniversary in 325–326.[^barnes-1980]
- *Martyrs of Palestine*, first written in 311. A shorter version survives in manuscripts of the *Church History*, and T. D. Barnes argues it was written to stand in its eighth book.[^barnes-1980]
- *Church History*, in ten books, in a final edition of 324 or 325. Most scholars had dated the first edition no earlier than 311; T. D. Barnes, writing in 1980, puts it about 295.[^mcgiffert-1910][^barnes-1980]
- *Life of Constantine*, in four books, published after the emperor's death in 337.[^mcgiffert-1910]

# Stated Position
On the books of the New Testament, in *Church History* 3.25.1–2: "First then must be put the holy quaternion of the Gospels; following them the Acts of the Apostles. After this must be reckoned the epistles of Paul; next in order the extant former epistle of John, and likewise the epistle of Peter, must be maintained." He places James, Jude, 2 Peter and 2 and 3 John among writings that are disputed but "nevertheless recognized by many" (3.25.3).[^eusebius-he3]
