# Description
Immanuel Kant lectured at the University of Königsberg for his whole career, unsalaried at first and then in the chair of logic and metaphysics, and wrote the three Critiques there.[^sep-kant] The vault cites his distinction between love as an inclination, which cannot be commanded, and practical love, which can.[^kant-groundwork]

# Work
- *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781, second edition 1787).[^sep-kant]
- *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785).[^sep-kant]
- *Critique of Practical Reason* (1788).[^sep-kant]
- *Religion within the Boundaries of Mere Reason* (1793).[^sep-kant-religion]
- *The Metaphysics of Morals* (1797).[^sep-kant]

# Stated Position
In *Religion within the Boundaries of Mere Reason* he holds that rational religion asks nothing of a person beyond "good life-conduct" to "become well-pleasing to God" (Ak 6:170), against a historical faith that judges by doctrine and observance, as the encyclopedia's entry on his philosophy of religion quotes him.[^sep-kant-religion]
