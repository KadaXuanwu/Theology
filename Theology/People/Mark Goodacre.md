# Description
Mark Goodacre is a New Testament scholar and Professor of New Testament and Christian Origins in the Department of Religious Studies at Duke University.[^goodacre-cv] His research interests include the Gospels, the apocryphal New Testament and the historical Jesus, and two of his books are on the [[Synoptic Problem]].[^goodacre-bio][^goodacre-books]

# Work
- *Goulder and the Gospels: An Examination of a New Paradigm* (1996).[^goodacre-books][^goodacre-cv]
- *The Synoptic Problem: A Way Through the Maze* (2001).[^goodacre-books][^goodacre-cv]
- *The Case Against Q: Studies in Markan Priority and the Synoptic Problem* (2002).[^goodacre-books][^goodacre-cv]
- "Scripturalization in Mark's Crucifixion Narrative", in *The Trial and Death of Jesus: Essays on the Passion Narrative in Mark*, ed. Geert van Oyen and Tom Shepherd (Peeters, 2006), 33–47.[^goodacre-articles]
- *Thomas and the Gospels: The Case for Thomas's Familiarity with the Synoptics* (2012).[^goodacre-books][^goodacre-cv]
- *The Fourth Synoptic Gospel: John's Knowledge of Matthew, Mark, and Luke* (2025).[^goodacre-books][^goodacre-cv]
