# Description
Julian of Eclanum was a bishop in southern Italy who wrote against [[Augustine]] on original sin.[^healy-1910][^bonwetsch] He was deposed in 418 after refusing to sign Pope Zosimus's letter against the Pelagians, and most of his writing survives only in Augustine's quotations.[^healy-1910][^bentivegna]

# Work
- *Ad Turbantium*, four books against Augustine on marriage and concupiscence (419).[^bonwetsch]
- *Ad Florum*, eight books against Augustine's reply.[^bonwetsch][^bentivegna]
- Commentaries on Job and on Hosea, Joel and Amos, which survive directly.[^ccsl-88]

# Stated Position
In the passages Augustine quotes from *Ad Turbantium*, Julian holds that "there cannot be sin without the will", and asks Augustine "how will you prove that any person is born guilty?"[^augustine-nuptiis]
