# Description
Hastings Rashdall was an English theologian, moral philosopher and historian of the medieval universities, a fellow and tutor of New College, Oxford, and later Dean of Carlisle.[^bnf-rashdall][^rayner-2005][^stout-1967] His Bampton Lectures on the atonement trace the doctrine's history and side with Abelard's account of it.[^rashdall-1919]

# Work
- *The Universities of Europe in the Middle Ages* (Clarendon Press, 1895).[^rashdall-1895]
- *The Theory of Good and Evil: A Treatise on Moral Philosophy* (Clarendon Press, 1907).[^rashdall-1907]
- *The Idea of Atonement in Christian Theology*, the Bampton Lectures for 1915 (Macmillan, 1919).[^rashdall-1919]

# Stated Position
In the atonement lectures he writes that the doctrine "was in all probability no part of our Master's own teaching" (p. 435).[^rashdall-1919] He adds that a doctrine's historical origin "determines little or nothing as to its validity" (p. 435).[^rashdall-1919]
