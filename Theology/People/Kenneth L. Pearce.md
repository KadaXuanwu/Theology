# Description
Kenneth L. Pearce is a philosopher who works on George Berkeley and early modern philosophy and, in philosophy of religion, on divine power, God's relation to the created world and free will.[^jmu-pearce] He held the Ussher post in Berkeley Studies and headed philosophy at Trinity College Dublin, and is now Professor of Philosophy and Academic Unit Head at James Madison University.[^tcd-2021][^jmu-pearce]

# Work
- *Language and the Structure of Berkeley's World* (Oxford University Press, 2017).[^jmu-pearce]
- "Foundational Grounding and the Argument from Contingency", *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion* 8 (2017), 245–268.[^jmu-pearce]
- *Is There a God? A Debate*, with [[Graham Oppy]] (Routledge, 2022).[^jmu-pearce]

# Stated Position
Of the position he defends in that book he says: "I'm defending a broadly classical view". He adds that by "classical theism" he means "a tradition of approaching this by trying to use ideas and arguments from classical Greek philosophy and the broader history of philosophy to try to make sense of ideas in Abrahamic religious traditions."[^tcd-2021]
