# Description
Rashi (Solomon bar Isaac) was a French commentator on the Hebrew Bible and the [[Talmud]] whose commentaries, in the words of the Jewish Encyclopedia, "carry a weight and authority which have rendered them inseparable from the text".[^je-rashi] He founded a school at Troyes, and his Bible commentary was widely read among Jews and used by Christian scholars.[^je-rashi]

# Work
- Commentary on the Pentateuch, first printed at Reggio in 1475, the first dated Hebrew book printed.[^je-rashi]
- Commentary on the Talmud, whose first complete printing, with the text, is the Venice edition of 1520–22.[^je-rashi]
- Commentary on the rest of the Hebrew Bible, first printed for the whole in *Miḳra'ot Gedolot* (Venice, 1525).[^je-rashi] On [Isaiah 53:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2053:3&version=NIV) it says that the prophet "mentions all Israel as one man", and on [Isaiah 52:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2052:13&version=NIV) that the servant is "Jacob, [i.e.,] the righteous among him".[^sefaria-isa]
- Responsa and liturgical poems (seliḥot).[^je-rashi]

# Stated Position
No published statement of his own position on the truth of Christianity was found.
