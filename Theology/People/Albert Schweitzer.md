# Description
Albert Schweitzer was a physician who founded and ran a hospital at Lambaréné in Gabon from 1913 until his death, and who held doctorates in philosophy and theology from Strasbourg before that.[^britannica-schweitzer] He appears in the vault as the figure in [[Marilyn McCord Adams|Adams]]'s counterexample about whose blow is the more culpable, where the work is done by his public reputation for self-control.[^adams-1975]

# Work
- *Von Reimarus zu Wrede*, translated as *The Quest of the Historical Jesus* (1906).[^britannica-schweitzer]
- *Kulturphilosophie*, translated as *The Philosophy of Civilization* (1923).[^britannica-schweitzer]
- *Die Mystik des Apostels Paulus*, translated as *The Mysticism of Paul the Apostle* (1930).[^britannica-schweitzer]
- *Aus meinem Leben und Denken*, translated as *Out of My Life and Thought* (1931).[^britannica-schweitzer]

# Stated Position
His own principle is ethical rather than doctrinal: "Ethics is nothing other than Reverence for Life. Reverence for Life affords me my fundamental principle of morality, namely, that good consists in maintaining, assisting and enhancing life, and to destroy, to harm or to hinder life is evil."[^schweitzer-ethics]

[^britannica-schweitzer]: ["Albert Schweitzer"](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Albert-Schweitzer), *Encyclopaedia Britannica*. He was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1952 and used the money to add a leprosarium at Lambaréné. He was also a New Testament scholar and a Bach scholar and organist.
[^schweitzer-ethics]: Albert Schweitzer, *Kulturphilosophie* (1923), quoted at ["Reverence for Life"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverence_for_Life), Wikipedia. The principle dates from 1915 and recurs across his later books.
[^adams-1975]: Marilyn McCord Adams, "Hell and the God of Justice", *Religious Studies* 11/4 (1975), 433–447, at 443.
