# Description
John Dominic Crossan is a historical Jesus scholar and professor emeritus in the Department of Religious Studies at DePaul University in Chicago.[^day1-crossan] He was co-chair of the Jesus Seminar.[^westar-crossan][^broadview-2020]

# Work
- *The Cross That Spoke: The Origins of the Passion Narrative* (1988).[^westar-crossan]
- *The Historical Jesus: The Life of a Mediterranean Jewish Peasant* (1991).[^westar-crossan]
- *Jesus: A Revolutionary Biography* (1994).[^westar-crossan]
- *Who Killed Jesus?* (1995).[^westar-crossan]

In a 1993 lecture, published in 1994, he sets two options against each other for the passion narratives, "history memorized" and "prophecy historicized". By the first he means that someone was present, noted the facts and passed them on until they were written down. By the second he means that after Jesus died, people searched the Hebrew Scriptures to see whether God's chosen one could suffer and die. They then shaped the stories from what they found, with [Isaiah 53:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2053:7&version=NIV) as his first example.[^crossan-1994]

# Stated Position
Asked in a 2020 interview how a strictly historical approach affects his faith, he said: "It has strengthened my faith and made it far more viable."[^broadview-2020] In the 1993 lecture he said: "Resurrection is one way, but only one way, of expressing the absence as presence, or presence despite absence, of Jesus as experienced in the world by a believer."[^crossan-1994]
