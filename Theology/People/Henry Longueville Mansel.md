# Description
Henry Longueville Mansel was a metaphysician who held the Waynflete professorship of moral and metaphysical philosophy and then the chair of ecclesiastical history at Oxford, and became dean of St Paul's.[^dnb-mansel] His Bampton Lectures argue that human morality is "not identical with, nor adequate to measure, the Absolute Morality of God".[^mansel-1858]

# Work
- *The Limits of Religious Thought Examined in Eight Lectures*, the Bampton Lectures for 1858.[^dnb-mansel][^mansel-1858]
- *The Philosophy of the Conditioned* (1866), his answer to [[John Stuart Mill]].[^dnb-mansel][^mansel-1866]

# Stated Position
"That there is an Absolute Morality, based upon, or rather identical with, the Eternal Nature of God, is indeed a conviction forced upon us by the same evidence as that on which we believe that God exists at all. But what that Absolute Morality is, we are as unable to fix in any human conception, as we are to define the other attributes of the same Divine Nature" (Lecture VII).[^mansel-1858] He adds that we "can but infer and conjecture from what we know of the moral attributes of man", by an analogy that "can never be so perfect as to preclude all possibility of error" (Lecture VIII).[^mansel-1858]
