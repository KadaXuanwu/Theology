# Description
Edward Deci and Richard Ryan's theory of motivation, which sorts reasons for acting from controlled to autonomous. Acting to meet an outside demand or avoid a punishment is external regulation; acting under inner pressure "to avoid guilt or anxiety" is introjection; both count as controlled. Acting from a value the person "has identified with" and "accepted its regulation as his or her own" is "a more autonomous, or self-determined, form".[^ryan-2000] "Controlled" here is wider than "coerced": it also covers rewards and one's own guilt.
