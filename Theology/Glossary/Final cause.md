# Description
The end or purpose for the sake of which a thing exists or happens, used to explain it by what it is for. Andrea Falcon's entry gives it as the answer to the question "What is its good?", one of Aristotle's four causes, and notes that Aristotle's version does not depend on desires or intentions.[^falcon-2023] Robert Boyle also used it for ends set by the Creator, the first of his four kinds being the "grand and General Ends of the whole World, such as the Exercising and Displaying the Creators immense Power".[^anstey-2025] That is the sense meant where the vault quotes Laplace.[^laplace-1813]
