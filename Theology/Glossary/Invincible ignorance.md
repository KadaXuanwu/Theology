# Description
Ignorance that cannot be dispelled by the reasonable diligence a person could be expected to exercise, and which therefore excuses the resulting act from moral fault. The *New Catholic Encyclopedia* explains that such ignorance "causes the act to be involuntary, at least as far as its objective moral character is concerned", since "what is unknown cannot be the object of volition", and contrasts it with vincible ignorance, which reasonable diligence could have dispelled.[^nce-ignorance] Aquinas defines it as ignorance that "obviously cannot be overcome [even] by effort", and holds that "invincible ignorance is never a sin".[^bullivant-2011] In logic teaching the phrase names something else, a fallacy in which a person defends a position "simply by refusing to acknowledge the force of the arguments supporting the opposing view".[^thompson-fallacy]
