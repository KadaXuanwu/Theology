# Description
In William James's "The Will to Believe", a choice between two hypotheses that are both live for the person choosing: "A living option is one in which both hypotheses are live ones."[^james-1897] A hypothesis is live when it "appeals as a real possibility to him to whom it is proposed".[^james-1897] Liveness, James adds, is not an intrinsic property but a relation "to the individual thinker".[^james-1897] A living option is one of James's three marks of a genuine option, which must also be forced and momentous.[^james-1897]
