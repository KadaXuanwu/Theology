# Description
The view that belief in God can be rational without being based on evidence or argument. Anthony Bolos and Kyle Scott give a central claim of it as "religious belief can be rational without any appeal to evidence or argument".[^bolos-scott-iep] They name Alvin Plantinga, Nicholas Wolterstorff and William Alston as its key figures.[^bolos-scott-iep] The word "Reformed" comes from the Reformed theological tradition of John Calvin and Abraham Kuyper, on which the view draws.[^bolos-scott-iep] In their words it "is best thought of as describing the inspiration behind the position rather than its core claims".[^bolos-scott-iep] Peter Forrest reports that on this view beliefs are [[Warrant|warranted]] without "Enlightenment-approved evidence" provided they are "(a) grounded, and (b) defended against known objections".[^forrest-sep]
