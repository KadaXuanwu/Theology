# Description
In William James's "The Will to Believe", an option that is not trivial. James calls an option trivial "when the opportunity is not unique, when the stake is insignificant, or when the decision is reversible if it later prove unwise".[^james-1897] His example is an invitation to join Nansen's North Pole expedition, which "would probably be your only similar opportunity".[^james-1897] It is one of his three marks of a genuine option, with [[Living option|living]] and [[Forced option|forced]].[^james-1897][^jordan-2022] The *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* reads them as alternatives, momentous if it "may never again present itself, or the decision cannot be easily reversed, or something of importance hangs on the choice".[^jordan-2022] In everyday use "momentous" only means very important; James's sense is set by his definition of trivial.
