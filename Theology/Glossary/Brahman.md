# Description
In Hindu thought, and above all in Advaita Vedanta, the one ultimate reality underlying everything. Sangeetha Menon writes that in classical Advaita Vedanta "Brahman is the fundamental reality underlying all objects and experiences" and "is explained as pure existence, pure consciousness and pure bliss".[^menon-iep] Advaita holds this absolute to be without qualities, "known as nirguņa Brahman, or Brahman 'without qualities'".[^menon-iep] Richard Jones and Jerome Gellman, writing on mysticism, call it "the nonpersonal Brahman".[^jones-gellman-sep] Advaita also allows that it "can be experienced indirectly in the natural world of experience as a personal God, known as saguņa Brahman, or Brahman with qualities".[^menon-iep]
