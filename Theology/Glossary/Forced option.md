# Description
In William James's "The Will to Believe", a choice that cannot be avoided, because the two alternatives leave nothing outside them: "Either accept this truth or go without it" is forced, since "there is no standing place outside of the alternative".[^james-1897] "Choose between going out with your umbrella or without it" is not forced, because you can stay in.[^james-1897] A forced option is one of James's three marks of a genuine option, with [[Living option|living]] and [[Momentous option|momentous]].[^james-1897][^jordan-2022]
