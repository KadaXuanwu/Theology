# Description
The view of God, illustrated by Anselm and Aquinas, that keeps the Greek perfections, among them impassibility, immutability and simplicity, and on which God is the "single-handed origin of the universe, making everything out of nothing".[^diller-2021] On Jeanine Diller's account, classical theists "deny or weaken God's personhood to save the Greek perfections such as impassibility, immutability and simplicity". Theistic personalists "conversely deny or weaken the Greek perfections to save God's personhood".[^diller-2021] The label is also used more loosely. The philosopher Kenneth Pearce says it "is used by different people in different ways", and means by it a tradition that uses "ideas and arguments from classical Greek philosophy and the broader history of philosophy" to make sense of Abrahamic ideas.[^tcd-2021] The vault uses Diller's sense.
