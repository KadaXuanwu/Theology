# Description
The list of books a religious community counts as authoritative scripture. In Nicola Denzey Lewis's words, "whatever list of writings a particular community considers authoritative constitutes a canon".[^denzey-lewis-2022] Michael J. Kruger traces the strict sense, canon as "a fixed, final, and closed list of books", to Albert C. Sundberg.[^kruger-2012] Sundberg held that scripture existed before there was a canon.[^kruger-2012] A wider sense uses the word for any collection that functions as a religious norm, "regardless of whether that collection is open or closed".[^kruger-2012] Kruger says Brevard Childs played a central role in promoting it, and traces its pedigree further back, to Adolf Harnack.[^kruger-2012] The vault means the list.
