# Description
A choice made by a chance device rather than outright, so that each possible act is taken with some probability. Don Ross's entry on game theory describes a player's utility being "optimized through use of a mixed strategy, in which she flips a weighted coin amongst several possible actions".[^ross-sep] A pure strategy, by contrast, is a single course of action.[^ross-sep] In the objection to Pascal's wager the example is a fair coin: heads, you wager for God, and tails, you wager against.[^hajek-sep] It is not Aristotle's [[Mixed act]], an act done from fear of a greater evil.
