# Description
Four passages in Isaiah 40–55 that Bernhard Duhm separated from their context and called "Songs of the Servant of the Lord": 42:1–4, 49:1–6, 50:4–9 and 52:13–53:12.[^avishur-2007] Duhm held that they come from a prophet other than the author of the rest of the chapters and that their servant is "an idealized figure", not Israel.[^avishur-2007] The Jewish Encyclopedia calls them the four "'ebed Yhwh songs".[^hirsch-servant]

Some scholars add 42:5–7 to the first song and 49:7 to the second, and doubt whether the third belongs with the others.[^avishur-2007] The Encyclopaedia Judaica adds that the songs show no differences in language and style from the chapters around them, so that "it has been argued that any distinction between these units and their contexts is somewhat arbitrary".[^avishur-2007]
