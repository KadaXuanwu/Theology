# Description
The name of "two works which have been preserved to posterity as the product of the Palestinian and Babylonian schools during the amoraic period, which extended from the third to the fifth century C.E.", the "Talmud Yerushalmi" (Jerusalem Talmud) and the "Talmud Babli" (Babylonian Talmud).[^bacher-1905] Wilhelm Bacher writes that the Talmud "is practically a mere amplification of the Mishnah by manifold comments and additions".[^bacher-1905] Used alone, the word generally means the Babylonian Talmud.[^bacher-1905] Bacher gives the final editing of the Babylonian Talmud to Ashi (died 427), and says it was completed by Rabina, "whose death in 499 marks, according to an ancient tradition, the end of the amoraic period and the completion of the redaction of the Talmud".[^bacher-1905] He adds that the Babylonian academies "produced the text in the course of 300 years".[^bacher-1905]
