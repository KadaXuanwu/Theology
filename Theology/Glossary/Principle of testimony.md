# Description
Richard Swinburne's claim that what others tell us is to be trusted unless there is good reason not to. Kelly James Clark reports that Swinburne "alleges that it is also reasonable to trust what others tell us unless and until we have good reason to believe otherwise".[^clark-iep] So, Clark writes, "it would be reasonable for someone who did not have a religious experience to trust the veridicality of someone who did claim to have a religious experience".[^clark-iep] Georg Gasser names it the "principle of testimony" and sets it beside the [[Principle of credulity|principle of credulity]] in the first premise of Swinburne's argument.[^gasser-2022]
