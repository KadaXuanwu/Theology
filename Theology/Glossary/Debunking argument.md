# Description
An argument that a belief is unjustified because of how it came about, without claiming that the belief is false. Jason Marsh gives a common form of it: a causal premise, "S's belief that p is explained by X", an epistemic premise, "X is an off-track process", and the conclusion that the belief is unjustified.[^marsh-sep] The form first arose for moral beliefs and has since been applied to religion. There X is a mechanism such as hypersensitive [[Agency detection|agency detection]], to which fuller versions add other by-product and evolutionary causes.[^marsh-sep] The entry reports wide agreement that uncovering a belief's origins "won't (by itself) reveal that they are false", though it may still pose an epistemological problem for the belief.[^marsh-sep]
