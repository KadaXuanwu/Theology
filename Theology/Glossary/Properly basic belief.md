# Description
A belief that is rational to hold without being inferred from other beliefs, evidence or argument. Anthony Bolos and Kyle Scott put the requirement as "the kind of belief that is rational despite not being inferred from any evidence or argument".[^bolos-scott-iep] Alvin Plantinga's examples are "I see a tree", "I am in pain", "I had breakfast this morning" and "God exists", the last known through the [[Sensus divinitatis|sensus divinitatis]].[^bolos-scott-iep] Such a belief is not groundless, since it arises from experience in fitting circumstances, and on Plantinga's account it is "properly basic but open to defeat".[^bolos-scott-iep] Classical foundationalism, which Plantinga rejects, allows only beliefs that are "evident to the senses, incorrigible, or self-evident" to be properly basic.[^bolos-scott-iep]
