# Description
A being such that, if it exists, it "cannot not-exist".[^reichenbach-2026] On the logical reading its non-existence is impossible; on the factual reading, if it exists, it "neither came into existence nor can cease to exist".[^reichenbach-2026] Bruce Reichenbach's sketch of the argument from contingency concludes to "a necessary being (a being such that if it exists, it cannot not-exist)". He quotes J. J. C. Smart for the logical reading, a being "whose non-existence is inconceivable in the sort of way that a triangle's having four sides is inconceivable". Reichenbach argues the argument needs only factual necessity. He notes that Swinburne holds that God, "though factually necessary, is a logically contingent being and so could have not-existed".[^reichenbach-2026]
