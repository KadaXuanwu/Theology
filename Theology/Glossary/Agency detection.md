# Description
The proposed human tendency to read ambiguous cues, such as a movement or a sound, as the work of an intentional agent even when none is present. Jason Marsh reports the proposal of "a hypersensitive agency detection device (HADD) that automatically leads us to interpret certain ambiguous stimuli (such as movements and sounds) in terms of agency, even when no one is present".[^marsh-sep] Because it is a "better-safe-than-sorry" mechanism, "it produces many false positives".[^marsh-sep] Peter Forrest attributes the device to Justin Barrett, for whom it "functions properly if the goal is survival but is hypersensitive if the goal is truth".[^forrest-sep] Marsh notes that whether it is foundational to religious belief is "controversial".[^marsh-sep]
