# Description
Richard Swinburne's principle that how things seem to a person is good reason to believe they are so, unless something counts against it. Mark Webb states it as the principle "according to which one is justified in believing that what seems to one to be present actually is present, unless some appropriate defeater is operative".[^webb-sep] Applied to religious experience, Richard Jones and Jerome Gellman put it as "experiences should be accepted as cognitive until defeated by rational considerations, and the burden of proof shifts to critics".[^jones-gellman-sep]
