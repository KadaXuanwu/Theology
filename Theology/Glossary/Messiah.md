# Description
"The name or title of the ideal king of the Messianic age", from the Hebrew "Ha-Mashiaḥ" and the Aramaic "Meshiḥa", "anointed one".[^jacobs-1904] In the Old Testament the earliest use of the word is "Meshiaḥ Yhwh" ("God's anointed one"), a title of the ruling king, and after the exile the high priest is called "ha-Kohen ha-Mashiaḥ" (the anointed priest).[^jacobs-1904] The same entry notes that Cyrus is called "God's anointed one" in [Isaiah 45:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2045:1&version=NIV).[^jacobs-1904] It states that "The Messiah", with the article, "is, however, not an Old Testament expression, but occurs for the first time in apocalyptic literature".[^jacobs-1904] It describes the expected figure in Jeremiah as "a righteous sprout of David", and in the Psalms of Solomon, which it dates after Pompey's conquest of Jerusalem in 63 BC, as the "son of David".[^jacobs-1904] The entry likens "Mashiaḥ" used without the article as a proper name to Χριστός in the Gospels, and calls the Μεσσιας of the New Testament "a transliteration of the Aramaic form".[^jacobs-1904] The sense defined here is the Jewish one, and the Christian use of the word as a title of Jesus is a separate sense.
