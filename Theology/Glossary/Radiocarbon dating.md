# Description
A method that uses the decay of carbon 14, a radioactive isotope of carbon, to date material that contains carbon.[^hajdas-2021] The measured age is then calibrated against records of known age, such as tree rings, which gives a range of calendar years and not a single year.[^orau-calibration][^reimer-2020] The Oxford Radiocarbon Accelerator Unit explains that a raw measurement is reported in years "before present" (BP), where present means 1950, and that this figure "is directly based on the proportion of radiocarbon found in the sample".[^orau-calibration] The page gives two reasons why this figure is not a calendar age: the proportion of radiocarbon in the atmosphere "has varied by a few percent over time", and it puts the true half-life at 5730 years, not "the original measured value of 5568 years".[^orau-calibration] Calibration looks for tree rings with the same proportion of radiocarbon, but "the measurements on both the tree rings and the samples have a limited precision and so there will be a range of possible calendar years", and "there might be several possible ranges".[^orau-calibration] The results "are often given as an age range", for example "95% sure that the sample comes from between 1375 cal BC and 1129 cal BC" for a measurement of 3000±30 BP.[^orau-calibration]
