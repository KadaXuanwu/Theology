# Description
In Alvin Plantinga's epistemology, the property that makes the difference between knowledge and mere true belief. Kenneth Boyce's entry puts it as "whatever precisely it is which makes the difference between knowledge and mere true belief".[^boyce-iep] On Plantinga's account, to a first approximation, a belief has warrant when it comes from cognitive faculties working properly, in the kind of environment they were designed for, under a design plan aimed at truth and likely to succeed.[^boyce-iep] Elsewhere in philosophy the word can mean the same as justified, as in "warranted assertibility".[^forrest-sep] Plantinga's sense is not that one: a belief can be justified and still lack warrant, as in a Gettier case.[^littlejohn-sep]
