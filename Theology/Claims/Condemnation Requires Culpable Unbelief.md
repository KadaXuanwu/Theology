  - Aquinas counts unbelief a sin only where someone refuses to hear the faith or despises it; those who never heard are damned, if at all, for other sins (ST II-II q.10 a.1).
  - Lumen Gentium 14 condemns only those who refuse knowing the Church was made necessary by Christ, and 16 holds out salvation to those who are ignorant through no fault of their own; Catechism 846 and 847 state one rule.
  - Bullivant reads the council as allowing ignorance without fault to last after hearing, if the proclamation was insufficient or Christians failed scandalously, and calls it a very wide hope.
  - Swinburne limits culpability for unbelief to failure to investigate, and holds that implicit faith suffices for inquirers who reached false conclusions without fault.
  - Rooney states the classical view that serious sin, not a mistaken conclusion, is necessary for damnation.
  - Westminster Confession 10.4 denies salvation to the diligent non-Christian on the ground of non-election, and the Larger Catechism Q. 68 presumes the willful neglect of every non-elect hearer.
  - Restrictive exclusivism is held mainly by strict Calvinists and some evangelicals (D'Costa).
  - Dei Filius says a Catholic can never have a just cause to doubt; Granderath and Vacant read just cause as objective, and Vacant reports that most theologians held leaving the childhood faith a formal mortal sin.
  - The Catholic answer is a dated development, from Florence in 1442 through Pius IX in 1854 and 1863 and the 1949 Holy Office letter to Lumen Gentium in 1964.
  - No instrument shows whether a given unbeliever was at fault, since self-report reaches only the reasons people are aware of (Bradley et al. 2018).

# Description
Christian teaching condemns unbelief only where some fault in the person produced it, not for an honest conclusion as such.

# Origins
Aquinas on unbelief as a sin only through refusal; Vatican II, Lumen Gentium 14 and 16; Bullivant's reading of the council.

# Based On
The Catechism at 846 and 847, and its rules on imputability. Swinburne on culpability for failing to investigate.

# Carries
Answers any argument that Christianity condemns people for an honest conclusion.

# Disputed By
Westminster Confession 10.4 and the Larger Catechism; restrictive exclusivism; Dei Filius on the former Catholic.

# Limits
Conditional: it spares the faultless without saying anyone is faultless, and no study can tell who is.
