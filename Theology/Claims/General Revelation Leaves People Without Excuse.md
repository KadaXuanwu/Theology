# Description
God has made himself knowable through what he created, so no one can plead total ignorance of him. This is general revelation, as distinct from special revelation, which is scripture and Christ. In its classic form it is enough to make a person accountable and not enough to save them, which is why missions still matter. [[John Calvin|Calvin]] went further and argued for an implanted sense of God (sensus divinitatis), a stronger claim than Paul makes.

# Origins
Paul, in [Romans 1:18–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:18-20&version=NIV) and [Romans 2:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202:14-15&version=NIV). Calvin, *Institutes* I.3–5, for the implanted sense of God and its smothering.[^calvin-institutes] [[Alvin Plantinga]] rebuilds Calvin's sense of God as a belief forming faculty in *Warranted Christian Belief*.[^plantinga-wcb]

Needs sources. A systematic treatment of general revelation with named holders and dates, and the confessional statements that fix the distinction between general and special revelation.

# Based On
- [Romans 1:19–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:19-20&version=NIV) what may be known about God is plain, understood from what has been made, so people are without excuse.
- [Romans 2:14–15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202:14-15&version=NIV) people without the Law sometimes do what it requires, showing its work written on their hearts.
- [Psalm 19:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2019:1&version=NIV) the heavens declare God's glory.
- [Acts 14:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2014:17&version=NIV) God left a witness in rain and harvests, said to a pagan crowd.
- [Acts 17:26–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2017:26-27&version=NIV) God set out where people would live so they would reach for him and perhaps find him.
- Calvin argues for a natural sense of God in everyone from birth, which is why he says there is no honest unbelief.[^calvin-institutes]
- [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] is the empirical support usually cited for this today.
- [[Kelemen 2004]] the specific finding that carries most of the weight: the purpose instinct is early and not taught.

# Carries
- It removes the plea of ignorance. Someone who never heard the gospel is still answerable for what creation showed, so an objection built on people who never had the chance has to argue that creation shows less than Paul says it does.
- It names the world itself as the evidence God gives, which answers the complaint that he gives none. It does not say that evidence is of the kind given to Thomas or to Paul on the Damascus road, and the instinct it points to is real whether or not it lands on a creator.
- Plantinga's version reaches further than Paul's text. If the sense of God is a working faculty, belief in God can be rational without being argued for. That is an argument in its own right and is not carried by Romans 1.

# Disputed By
- [[Peoples, Duda & Marlowe 2016]] the population level test. Animism in all 33 forager societies, a creator in 39%. The specific content Romans 1:20 names is the part that does not show up.
- [[Some Unbelief Isn't Resistance]] the people who looked and came out unconvinced, whom "without excuse" has to cover.
- Needs sources. The exegetical case that Romans 1:20 describes the pagan world as a group rather than every individual, from named commentators on Romans.

# Limits
- Paul describes working it out from the world, not an inner feeling. The feeling version is Calvin's addition and should not be quoted as Paul's.
- [Acts 17:27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2017:27&version=NIV) says "perhaps" reach out and find him. That is weaker than the reading usually put on Romans 1.
- The suppression clause in [Romans 1:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%201:18&version=NIV) is what carries the weight when someone reports no sense of God, and [[The Suppression Thesis Rests on Possibility]] is where that move gets examined, with [[No Outcome Counts Against It]] holding the general form. Its best placed defenders now reject the universal version of it.
- Calvin's stronger version has the harder job. If there is an implanted sense of God in everyone from the womb, honest lifelong unbelief should be rare, and it isn't.
- It sits awkwardly next to [[Obedience Comes Before Evidence]]. If God's nature is already plain from creation to everyone, it is not clear why a prior act of obedience should be needed to see it. Worth deciding which of the two carries the weight.

[^calvin-institutes]: John Calvin, *Institutes of the Christian Religion* I.3–5, the implanted knowledge of God, its smothering, and the knowledge conspicuous in creation.
[^plantinga-wcb]: Alvin Plantinga, *Warranted Christian Belief* (Oxford University Press, 2000).
