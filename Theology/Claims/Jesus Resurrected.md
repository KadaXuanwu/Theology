# Description
Three days after his crucifixion, Jesus was raised to life.

# Origins
The earliest Christian preaching. The formula in [1 Corinthians 15:3–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2015:3-8&version=NIV) is introduced with handing-on language ("what I received I passed on to you"), which marks it as material Paul inherited rather than composed, and Paul is writing in the mid 50s CE.

# Based On
- [1 Corinthians 15:3–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2015:3-8&version=NIV) lists named witnesses, including a group of over five hundred, most of whom Paul says are still alive. The passage is widely treated as a pre-Pauline confession, which puts the claim early rather than legendary in origin. On the scholarly debate over what the formula actually asserts, see [[James Ware]], [The Resurrection of Jesus in the Pre-Pauline Formula of 1 Cor 15.3–5](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/abs/resurrection-of-jesus-in-the-prepauline-formula-of-1-cor-1535/B98A7CB0EDE64897CAFEE83263BE6289), *New Testament Studies* 60 (2014), 475–498.
- The empty tomb accounts name women as the first witnesses ([Mark 16:1–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016:1-8&version=NIV)), whose testimony carried little weight in that setting. An invented story would not be built that way.
- [Acts 9:3–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%209:3-6&version=NIV) and [Galatians 1:13–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians%201:13-16&version=NIV) Paul turns from persecutor to preacher, which needs explaining.
- [[Jesus Existed]] is a precondition, not a support. It has to be settled first.

# Limits
- Do not use [[The Bible Only Speaks Truth]] as evidence here while also listing this note as evidence for it. The two currently lean on each other. The point of the evidence above is that it stands without assuming inerrancy: an early creed, an awkward detail nobody would invent, and a hostile witness who changed sides.
- [[Tacitus]] and [[Josephus]] attest that Jesus lived and was executed. Neither attests a resurrection. They belong under [[Jesus Existed]] and should not be quoted here.
- "Five hundred witnesses" is Paul's assertion, not five hundred independent statements. No one else records them.
- The gospel resurrection accounts differ on who arrived, when, and what was said. That variation is used both ways and this node should eventually say which reading it takes.
