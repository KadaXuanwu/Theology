# Description
The obstacle between God and a sinner is God's nature, not his willingness. Sin is described as something his presence cannot hold, so the separation follows from what he is rather than from a decision to punish. The barrier is the state of a person who has not dealt with sin standing next to something that burns it off, not a penalty he chose to impose and could waive on request.

That is why the offer is repentance and covering rather than a pardon issued without either. The problem being solved is proximity, not paperwork. On this account God is not withholding forgiveness when he does not simply forgive. He is describing a condition.

# Origins
The doctrine is assembled from the holiness texts below rather than stated in any one place. Needs sources. A systematic theology on divine holiness and on why sin cannot stand in God's presence, with the tradition's reading of [Isaiah 59:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2059:2&version=NIV) and [Hebrews 12:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2012:29&version=NIV), would give the claim named holders and dates.

# Based On
- [Isaiah 59:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2059:2&version=NIV) your iniquities have separated you from your God. The separation is stated as the effect of sin, not as a sentence handed down.
- [Habakkuk 1:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Habakkuk%201:13&version=NIV) eyes too pure to look on evil. But see Limits, this verse does not say what it is usually quoted as saying.
- [Psalm 5:4–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%205:4-5&version=NIV) with you, evil people are not welcome; the arrogant cannot stand in your presence.
- [Leviticus 11:44–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Leviticus%2011:44-45&version=NIV) be holy, because I am holy. Holiness as the standard rather than a rule among rules.
- [Hebrews 12:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2012:29&version=NIV) our God is a consuming fire. The nature-not-choice image in one line.
- [Revelation 21:27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%2021:27&version=NIV) nothing impure will ever enter it.

# Carries
- It turns the charge of arbitrariness into a claim about God's nature. If the separation follows from what God is, the requirement that sin be dealt with is not a rule he picked and could drop, even though it is not negotiable either.
- It says what is judged: a person's standing before holiness, not a verdict on a question they got wrong.
- It is the reason the separation exists at all. [[Hell Is Self-Chosen Separation]] applies the same idea to the outcome rather than the cause.
- It explains the need for a remedy, not the remedy. Why blood in particular should remove the barrier is a further question the claim does not answer, and [[The Covenant Changed with Christ]] ends the animal system without answering it either.

# Disputed By
- Needs sources. The counter position is that the separation is a judicial act God performs, not a state that follows from his nature, so that forgiveness without a remedy is open to him. Named theologians holding the penal reading belong here, with the passages under Limits where God is present among sinners.

# Limits
- [Habakkuk 1:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Habakkuk%201:13&version=NIV) is the prophet's complaint, and the second half of the same verse asks why God tolerates the treacherous. The verse's own point is that God does tolerate evil, at least for a time. Quoting the first clause on its own reverses what the passage says. Use it only with the whole verse visible.
- The nature reading has to explain the Old Testament, where God repeatedly is present among people who are sinning, and the Gospels, where Jesus eats with people the standard would exclude. "Cannot" is doing more work than the texts always support.
- If the separation is a fact about God rather than a choice, then it is not something he can be praised for either. The move that removes the charge of arbitrariness also removes the mercy from the arrangement.
- If God set both the standard and the human capacity to meet it, calling the mismatch a fact about his nature does not settle who is responsible for it.
- Most of the verses above are poetry or law rather than doctrinal statement. The doctrine is built from them; it is not stated outright in any single one.
