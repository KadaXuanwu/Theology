# Description
In scripture the order runs obedience first, confirmation after. Peter has fished all night and caught nothing. Jesus tells him to put out again. Peter is a career fisherman, says so plainly, and lets the nets down anyway on the strength of who is asking. The catch follows the obedience, not the other way round.

On this reading God is not indifferent to evidence, he sequences it. Confirmation is real but sits on the far side of the step, so someone who waits for proof before moving never arrives at the place where proof is given. [John 7:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%207:17&version=NIV) states the rule outright: do the will first, then you find out where the teaching came from.

# Origins
Read off the passages below rather than from a named theologian. Needs sources. The traditional root is [[Augustine]]'s "believe so that you may understand", not opened for this node, and a treatment of faith preceding understanding would give the claim its history.

# Based On
- [Luke 5:4–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%205:4-5&version=NIV) Jesus says "put out into deep water and let down the nets for a catch." Peter answers "Master, we've worked hard all night and haven't caught anything. But because you say so, I will let down the nets." That "because you say so" is the whole claim in one line. The core case.
- [John 7:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%207:17&version=NIV) anyone who chooses to do God's will finds out whether the teaching is from God. The ordering stated as a principle rather than a story.
- [Hebrews 11:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2011:8&version=NIV) Abraham sets out without knowing the destination.
- [Joshua 3:13–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%203:13-16&version=NIV) the river stops only once the priests' feet are already in it, and the text notes it was at flood stage.
- [2 Kings 5:10–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%205:10-14&version=NIV) Naaman is told to wash in a river he considers beneath him, objects, obeys, and is healed. Same shape as Peter: an expert told to do something that sounds beneath his own judgment.
- [Malachi 3:10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Malachi%203:10&version=NIV) test me in this. The one place God invites the test explicitly, and it still requires acting first.
- [Hebrews 11:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2011:6&version=NIV) without faith it is impossible to please God, so the ordering is presented as a requirement, not a preference.
- [John 20:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2020:29&version=NIV) blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe. Belief before sight is treated as the better state.
- [Psalm 34:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2034:8&version=NIV) taste and see that the Lord is good, and [Jeremiah 29:13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2029:13&version=NIV) you will find me when you seek me with all your heart. The same ordering stated as an invitation: try it, then see.

# Carries
- It makes the placing of evidence after commitment deliberate rather than a failure to provide it. An objection that God gave Thomas the wounds, appeared to Paul and sent fire for Elijah while withholding that kind of evidence from everyone else has to meet the reply that theirs also followed a step toward God. That holds for Peter and is hard to argue for Paul on the Damascus road.
- The same structure exists outside theology. [[William James]] argued in *The Will to Believe* (1896) that where an option is living, forced and momentous and cannot be settled on the evidence, waiting for proof is itself a choice, and that faith in a fact can help bring the fact about. That the move is not special pleading invented for religion is what that parallel shows, and it is an argument in its own right rather than part of this claim.

# Disputed By
- [[Experiential Confirmation Doesn't Distinguish Religions]] grants the sequence and attacks what it delivers. Other religions promise the same confirmation on the same terms, so obedience first can produce conviction without being able to say which conviction is the right one.
- [[Humans Are Primed to See Agents and Purpose]] supplies the natural mechanism. Once someone has committed and is looking, confirmation is what they will tend to find whether or not anything is there.

# Limits
- Peter was not working from zero. In Luke's own ordering Jesus had already healed Peter's mother-in-law ([Luke 4:38–39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%204:38-39&version=NIV)) and drawn a crowd big enough to press him to the water's edge ([Luke 5:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%205:1&version=NIV)). Peter obeyed on prior evidence, not on none. That makes him a poor model for someone starting cold, which is the case the claim most needs to cover.
- The cost is not comparable. Peter risked one net-drop and an hour. Someone asked to reorder their life on the same logic is being asked for far more on the same amount of evidence, and the story does not carry that weight on its own.
- Nobody records the failures. Scripture keeps the cases where obedience was followed by confirmation. People who stepped out and got nothing do not get a chapter. Whatever the true rate is, the sample here cannot show it.
- The step has no stated content. What exactly is being tried, and what would count as it not working, are both open. Without an answer to the second, the method cannot fail.
- The claim resists testing. If confirmation arrives, the rule held. If it does not, the obedience or the willingness was insufficient. No outcome counts against it, which is the same structural problem flagged in [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]] over the suppression clause. [[No Outcome Counts Against It]] holds the general form, including what it does and does not license.
- A first step taken under an eternal penalty is not obviously a free step, which is the pressure [[Hell Is Self-Chosen Separation]] also has to hold against.
- It sits awkwardly next to [[General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse]]. If God's nature is already plain from creation to everyone, it is not clear why a prior act of obedience should be needed to see it. Worth deciding which of the two carries the weight.
- Do not use John 21:6, the "right side of the boat" passage, for this claim. In [John 21:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2021:4&version=NIV) the disciples do not know who is speaking, so they are taking a tip from an unrecognised man on the shore and only work out who it is after the catch. That is not trust in the Lord. Luke 5 is the passage that carries the point.
