# Description
A historical figure known as Jesus of Nazareth lived in the early first century CE, whether understood as a prophet, the incarnate Son of God, or another figure of religious significance within the Christian tradition.

# Origins
The Christian movement itself, from the 30s CE onwards. As a separate historical question, rather than a theological one, it dates from nineteenth century critical scholarship, which is also where the denial of it comes from.

# Based On
- [[Tacitus]], *Annals* 15.44 (written c. 116 CE), reports that "Christus" was executed under Pontius Pilate during the reign of Tiberius. A hostile Roman source with no interest in helping the movement.
- [[Josephus]], *Antiquities* 20.200 (c. 93–94 CE), refers to James as "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ". Generally treated as authentic, unlike the longer passage below.
- Josephus, *Antiquities* 18.63–64, the Testimonium Flavianum. Use with care: the text as it stands contains Christian additions, and scholars are split between a partly authentic core and full interpolation. See [Early Christian Writings](https://earlychristianwritings.com/testimonium.html) for the range of positions.
- Paul's letters, written in the 50s CE, refer to meeting Jesus' brother James and the disciple Peter in person ([Galatians 1:18–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Galatians%201:18-19&version=NIV)). That is a named claim about named living people, made to an audience able to check it.
- [[The Bible Only Speaks Truth]] is the internal case, but it is the weakest support here rather than the strongest, because it assumes what an outsider is asking about.

# Carries
- [[Jesus Resurrected]] requires this first. It cannot be argued in the other direction.

# Disputed By
- Nothing currently in this vault. The mythicist position exists but is a small minority in the field.

# Limits
- Tacitus and Josephus both write sixty to eighty years after the events and may be reporting what Christians said about themselves rather than independent record.
- Establishing that a man existed and was crucified is a low bar. It does nothing for any theological claim on its own, and should not be presented as if it does.
