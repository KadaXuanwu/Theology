# Description
The Bible presents the old covenant as a stage that Christ ends and replaces, not as God changing his mind. Under the new terms, judgment is God's alone and is held back until the end, while people are told to love enemies rather than remove them. This is why the conquest commands are not treated as a standing rule by any later biblical writer.

# Origins
Hebrews is the earliest statement of the reading, quoting Jeremiah's promise and calling the first covenant obsolete. Needs sources. A named treatment of covenant theology, and the traditions that differ on how clean the break is, would give the claim holders and dates beyond the text itself.

# Based On
- [Jeremiah 31:31–34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Jeremiah%2031:31-34&version=NIV) a new covenant is promised while the old one still stands, so the change is announced from inside the Old Testament.
- [Hebrews 8:6–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%208:6-13&version=NIV) quotes that promise and calls the first covenant obsolete.
- [Matthew 5:38–39,43–44](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%205:38-39,43-44&version=NIV) the standard is reset to non-retaliation and love of enemies.
- [Matthew 13:29–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2013:29-30&version=NIV) weeds and wheat grow together until the harvest, so separation is deferred.
- [Romans 12:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%2012:19&version=NIV) revenge is handed back to God, taken out of human hands.
- [John 18:36](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2018:36&version=NIV) the kingdom is not one his followers fight for, breaking the link between faith and territory.
- [Luke 9:54–55](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%209:54-55&version=NIV) two disciples propose calling down fire in Old Testament style and Jesus rebukes them.

# Carries
- It answers why the conquest commands are not a standing rule, and only that. The harsh episodes are placed in a stage that has ended, so an objection that God is brutal has to be made about that stage rather than about the present terms. Whether the stage itself was wrong stays open, see Limits.
- It ends the animal sacrifice system without explaining why blood was ever the mechanism. [[God Can't Accept Sin]] gives the need for a remedy; neither claim gives the reason for that remedy.
- [[God Delays Judgment to Allow Repentance]] makes the parallel move within a single episode rather than across time.

# Disputed By
- Needs sources. The counter is that a good God's standards do not change, so a revised covenant either was not needed or shows the earlier one was wrong. The immutability texts under Limits carry the point; a named theologian pressing it belongs here.

# Limits
- The Bible also insists God does not change ([Malachi 3:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Malachi%203:6&version=NIV), [Numbers 23:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%2023:19&version=NIV), [Hebrews 13:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews%2013:8&version=NIV)). So the claim has to be that the covenant changed while the character behind it didn't, which is harder to hold than it sounds.
- [Matthew 5:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%205:17&version=NIV) has Jesus saying he came to fulfil the law, not abolish it, which cuts against a clean break.
- Violence is postponed, not dropped. [Revelation 19:11–21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%2019:11-21&version=NIV) describes a violent final judgment, so this answers "why not now" rather than "why ever".
- It does not clear the original command. If the standard is better now, the honest question stays open whether it was wrong then.
- In [Luke 9:54–55](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%209:54-55&version=NIV) only the rebuke is in the earliest manuscripts. The extra line about not knowing what spirit they are of is a later addition, so don't quote it.
