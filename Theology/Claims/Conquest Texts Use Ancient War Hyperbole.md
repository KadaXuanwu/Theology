# Description
"Leave nothing alive" and "no survivors" were standard victory wording across the ancient Near East, used by kings whose enemies clearly survived. Read that way, the conquest texts claim a decisive win rather than report a body count.

# Origins
K. Lawson Younger Jr., *Ancient Conquest Accounts* (Sheffield 1990), a comparative study of Joshua against Hittite, Egyptian and Assyrian war reports. Taken up by Kenneth Kitchen, *On the Reliability of the Old Testament* (Eerdmans 2003), and by Paul Copan and Matthew Flannagan, *Did God Really Command Genocide?* (Baker 2014).

# Based On
- [[Merneptah Stele]] Egypt, c. 1208 BCE. "Israel is laid waste, its seed is no more." Israel is still there afterwards.
- [[Mesha Stele]] Moab, c. 840 BCE. "Israel has perished forever", about 120 years before the northern kingdom actually fell.
- The Bible does the same thing internally. [Joshua 10:40](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2010:40&version=NIV) says nobody was left, [Joshua 13:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Joshua%2013:1&version=NIV) says much land is untaken, [Judges 1:27–33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%201:27-33&version=NIV) names Canaanites still living there. Amalek is destroyed in [1 Samuel 15:7–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2015:7-8&version=NIV) and raiding again in [1 Samuel 30:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2030:1&version=NIV).

# Carries
- The conquest texts can be wrong in detail without being dishonest, because the genre allows overstatement.
- Missing destruction layers are a smaller problem than a literal reading makes them. What the spade fails to find is closer to what the wording actually promised.
- Anything resting on a flat literal reading of these texts has to answer the genre question first, whether it reads them as history or as an atrocity.

# Disputed By
- Wesley Morriston has pressed the command/report distinction against Copan and Flannagan. See Limits.

# Limits
- It works on victory reports, not on commands. [Deuteronomy 20:16–18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2020:16-18&version=NIV) and [1 Samuel 15:2–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2015:2-3&version=NIV) tell someone what to do, and an order is not softened by the fact that the later brag was overstated. This is the strongest objection to the claim.
- It cuts both ways on the archaeology. If the wording is hyperbole, the missing destruction layers stop being a problem, but the historical conquest shrinks to something much smaller than the text describes.
- Showing the convention existed in Egypt and Moab does not prove any given biblical passage is using it. That still has to be argued case by case.
