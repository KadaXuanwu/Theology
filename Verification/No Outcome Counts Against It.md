### V2 — Popper, and how far he meant it to reach
**Status:** open
**Node:** `Claims/No Outcome Counts Against It`
**Source:** Karl Popper, *Logik der Forschung* (1934), translated as *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959)
**What the node says:** Popper is the usual starting point for the demand that a claim rule something out. The node says this framing needs sourcing before it stays.
**What is open:** nothing of Popper was read. Two things are wanted, not one. First, the falsifiability criterion in his own words. Second, and more important, whether he meant it as a test of meaning or only as a line between science and non science, since the node's whole argument depends on which. His stated targets were Marxism and psychoanalysis, not theology, and he is often quoted as though he had settled a question he was careful not to settle.
**What would settle it:** the book. A quotable statement of the criterion, and any passage where he says what it does not reach.

**What is open:** everything. This is the node's weakest section and the reason it is a draft. Laudan matters most: if the demarcation project really is dead in current philosophy of science, the Popper framing in `Origins` has to go and the node has to rest on the plain evidential point instead, which is the stronger version anyway. The Duhem and Quine point needs a source that distinguishes a principled auxiliary assumption from an ad hoc one, because without that distinction the node's whole objection collapses into an objection to something every science does.
**What would settle it:** Laudan first. He is widely anthologised and the paper is short.
