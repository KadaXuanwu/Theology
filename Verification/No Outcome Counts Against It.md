### V2. Popper, and how far he meant it to reach
**Status:** partial
**Kind:** content
**Node:** `Claims/No Outcome Counts Against It`
**Source:** Karl Popper, *Logik der Forschung* (1934), translated as *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1959)
**What the node says:** Popper is the usual starting point for the demand that a claim rule something out. The node says this framing needs sourcing before it stays.
**What is open:** first, what Popper argues, and the page of each passage in the cited 1959 printing. Only snippets of his words were seen, so the argument is unread. Second, the node's sentence that his own target was Marxism and psychoanalysis rather than theology has no footnote and is not carried by this book. It belongs to his lecture "Science: Conjectures and Refutations", chapter 1 of *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963), which was seen only in snippets.
**What would settle it:** the book. A quotable statement of the criterion, and any passage where he says what it does not reach.
**Needs:** Popper 1959, The Logic of Scientific Discovery
**Tried:** 2026-11-01, clear-queue: Internet Archive full text search of the Routledge Classics upload and of the 1959 Basic Books and Hutchinson printings, snippets only; the finder read the upload through, which the access rules bar, so it counts as snippets; the book text has no hit for Marx, Freud or psychoanalysis; reprint snippets of the 1963 lecture name Marx, Freud and Adler; Hansson in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, read in full; *Conjectures and Refutations*, all copies restricted, not opened.
**Verdict:** Seen only as full text search snippets of a user upload of the Routledge Classics text and of the 1959 Basic Books printing, with two phrases also checked in the Hutchinson printing. The criterion stands in section 6, "Falsifiability as a Criterion of Demarcation": a scientific system must be possible "to be refuted by experience". Popper offers falsifiability as a criterion of demarcation "but not of meaning", and calls the idea that he proposed it as a criterion of meaning a myth. Section 20, "Methodological Rules", accepts an auxiliary hypothesis only if its introduction does not diminish the degree of falsifiability, which bears on the principled versus ad hoc question in the node. The argument and the pages were not seen. Bears on the node, no change made: the node says how far he meant the criterion to reach is disputed, yet in the 1959 text he states it himself. And "rather than theology" may need a qualifier. Hansson, "Science and Pseudo-Science", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, revised 28 July 2025, sections 4.2 and 4.3, read in full, reports that Popper (1962, 39) put religious statements on the far side of his line. That is one secondary report, not a ruling.

**Verdict:** The citation is confirmed from the record: Larry Laudan, "The Demise of the Demarcation Problem", in *Physics, Philosophy and Psychoanalysis*, ed. R. S. Cohen and L. Laudan (Boston Studies in the Philosophy of Science, 1983), pp. 111–127, DOI 10.1007/978-94-009-7055-7_6 (Crossref). Hansson's bibliography names Reidel, and running heads in the snippets show pp. 113, 115 and 117 inside the chapter. At snippet level Laudan says falsifiability counts as scientific every crank claim that makes ascertainably false assertions, that the focus should be on the "empirical and conceptual credentials" of claims, and that terms like "pseudo-science" should be dropped (Pigliucci cites the last as p. 125). Pigliucci's chapter in the 2013 *Philosophy of Pseudoscience* is titled "The Demarcation Problem: A (Belated) Response to Laudan", so this is the paper cited for the demise. Hansson, "Science and Pseudo-Science", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, revised 28 July 2025, read in full: section 4.3 reports Popper's criterion criticised both for excluding legitimate science and for admitting some pseudosciences, and that most pseudosciences "have been thoroughly falsified"; section 5 reports that many authors replaced the single criterion with lists of criteria; section 8 finds "almost complete disagreement" on the general criteria but "virtual unanimity" on which doctrines are pseudoscience. So the demise is contested, not settled. Kyle Stanford, "Underdetermination of Scientific Theory", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, revised 4 April 2023, sections 1 and 2.1, read in full, bears out that Duhem's point applies to theory testing in every field, and that the rule separating principled from ad hoc revisions is the difficulty: Quine granted that some rescues "strike us as ad hoc" and answered with pragmatic virtues such as simplicity and conservatism. For a rule on auxiliaries, Popper 1959, section 20, at snippet level (V2 in this file), and van Elk 2025, p. 140 (V3 in this file), name the same line. Laudan was not read in full, so nothing here confirms what he argues.
