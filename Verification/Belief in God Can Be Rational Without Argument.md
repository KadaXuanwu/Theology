Crux: The argument stands or falls on what happens when a reflective believer meets an objection she cannot answer that does not assume God is absent. On Plantinga's account defeat is fixed by what a properly working mind, with the part of its design plan that sustains the belief aimed only at truth, would give up, so whether evil defeats the belief turns on whether Christian belief is true (Warranted Christian Belief pp. 362–363, 491–492); Quinn asks for a better reason against each defeater, and Plantinga's 1986 reply lets the belief's own warrant outweigh it. Which account of defeat is right is a question for epistemology that more sources will not settle.
Strong counter: Philip Quinn and Joshua Thurow. For many intellectually sophisticated adult theists, evil and naturalistic accounts of how belief in God forms are substantial reasons against it, and belief that issues only from the process cognitive science describes is debunked for someone with an independent reason to doubt God; belief resting partly on argument is not.
