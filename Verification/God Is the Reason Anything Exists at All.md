### V2. Saunders, known only through reviewers
**Status:** corrected
**Kind:** content
**Node:** `Arguments For/God Is the Reason Anything Exists at All`
**Source:** Nicholas Saunders, *Divine Action and Modern Science* (Cambridge University Press, 2002)
**What the node says:** reviewers report that Saunders finds the attempts to square special divine action with quantum theory and chaos theory still far from a satisfactory account. The node uses this to limit its own claim to general divine action.
**What is open:** nothing
**What would settle it:** the book, or a named review. The specific thing wanted is whether Saunders' verdict is as negative as the node implies, and whether he leaves any route open.
**Tried:** 2026-11-01, clear-queue: Crossref (eight named reviews); Cambridge Core page (blurb, chapter list); Cambridge front matter PDF, Preface pp. ix–xviii, read in full; Tracy's review at *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews*, read in full; Yong's review essay in *Zygon*, read in full; the reviews by Peacocke, Epperson, Schoen, Stannard and McFarland not open, not tried beyond the record; Smedes at Taylor and Francis, Cloudflare check, dropped.
**Verdict:** The node is wrong on three points. It was checked against the Preface of the cited printing (the publisher's front matter PDF, pp. ix–xviii, read in full) and two reviews read in full: Thomas Tracy, *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews*, 9 October 2003, and Amos Yong, *Zygon* 43/1 (March 2008), 191–200. First, the wording "still far from a satisfactory account" is the publisher's cover blurb, which speaks of divine action and modern science in general. Neither Tracy nor Yong uses it. Second, Saunders' own verdict is harsher: the quantum and chaos approaches to special divine action do not survive scrutiny (Preface, pp. xvi–xvii), and both reviews report the same from chapters 6 and 7 (Yong, p. 192, quoting pp. 170 and 172; Tracy quotes p. 172). Tracy disagrees with him on the quantum case. Third, he leaves a route. Both reviewers report that he rates Peacocke's whole and part model highest, quoting p. 213, and the Preface (p. xvii) names Nancy Cartwright's view of laws as a possible direction for research. The node's gloss of special divine action as miracles and answered prayer is not his definition as Tracy reports it (p. 21), and the Preface (p. xiv) calls miracle as law violation theologically undesirable. Chapters 1 to 9 were not opened. Pages 21, 170, 172 and 213 are known only through the two reviews, so the change cites p. 213 as their report. The existing [^saunders-2002] footnote is right as it stands. The gloss of special divine action in the first sentence of the change can be dropped if only the verdict sentence is to change. Change for the node, made 2026-11-01: replace "[[Nicholas Saunders]] separates general divine action, creating and keeping things in being, from special divine action, miracles and answered prayer. Reviewers report he finds the attempts to square the second with quantum theory and chaos theory still far from a satisfactory account.[^saunders-2002]" with "[[Nicholas Saunders]] separates general divine action, creating and keeping things in being, from special divine action, God bringing about physical effects that would not have happened had he not acted. In his Preface (p. xvii) he concludes that neither the quantum nor the chaos approach to special divine action "survives detailed scientific and theological scrutiny", which leaves "very little detailed contemporary support" for it.[^saunders-2002] Two reviewers report that he still rates Arthur Peacocke's whole and part model the best account on offer (p. 213), a model his Preface calls a bold metaphysical assertion.[^tracy-2003][^yong-2008]" and add the footnotes "[^tracy-2003]: Thomas Tracy, [review of *Divine Action and Modern Science*](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/divine-action-and-modern-science/), *Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews*, 9 October 2003." and "[^yong-2008]: Amos Yong, "Divining 'Divine Action' in Theology-and-Science: A Review Essay", *Zygon* 43/1 (2008), 191–200."
