### V1. Hitzeman and Wastell, abstract only
**Status:** partial
**Kind:** content
**Node:** `Claims/The Suppression Thesis Rests on Possibility`
**Source:** Cortney Hitzeman and Colin Wastell, *Journal of Cognition and Culture* 17 (2017), 27–50
**What the node says:** they report that the priming effect in atheists tracked earlier exposure to religious behaviour rather than atheism itself. The node calls it the sharpest test it has, on 88 students.
**What is open:** the body of the article. How the exposure measure (religious credibility enhancing displays) was built and scored. How lifetime and convert atheists were defined and how many there were in each cell. The effect sizes. Whether the authors claim the design separates exposure from implicit theism, or only that exposure must be controlled.
**What would settle it:** the article. How exposure was measured, and whether the authors themselves think it separates the two explanations.
**Needs:** Hitzeman 2017, Are Atheists Implicit Theists
**Tried:** 2026-11-01, clear-queue: Crossref (record and abstract, no licence); OpenAlex, closed, no repository copy; Semantic Scholar, closed; Brill page, 403, and its text-mining PDF, 503; Macquarie research portal, abstract only, no file; Figshare, ResearchGate, web search and quoted phrases, no free copy; PhilArchive, 403; relbib, browser check, dropped; the open QUB manuscript of Turpin, Andersen and Lanman (2019), read in full, cites the study in one sentence.
**Verdict:** Confirmed from the abstract only, of the published article (Crossref record, DOI 10.1163/15685373-12342190; the same abstract is on the Macquarie research portal): Cortney Hitzeman and Colin Wastell, "Are Atheists Implicit Theists?", *Journal of Cognition and Culture* 17/1–2 (2017), 27–50. The abstract says 88 university students were randomly assigned to a religious or a control prime, the outcome was pro-social behaviour in a dictator game, and exposure was a reported measure of religious credibility enhancing displays. Convert atheists in the prime condition were significantly more pro-social than lifetime atheists, and higher exposure scores went with higher pro-sociality in the religious condition than in the control. The authors conclude that past personal differences in exposure "should be considered" in tests of implicit theism. That reads as a weaker claim than separating the two explanations, but it is the abstract only. The node's sentence and its 88 match the abstract. Its "rather than atheism itself" goes slightly past it, since the abstract compares convert with lifetime atheists and reports no test of atheism as such. "The sharpest test here" is the node's own judgment and not a matter for the source.
