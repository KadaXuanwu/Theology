### V2. Koch's dates from catalogue records
**Status:** confirmed
**Kind:** page
**Node:** `Evidence/The Royal Touch`
**Source:** Robert Koch, announcement to the Berlin Physiological Society, 24 March 1882, published in *Berliner klinische Wochenschrift* that year; "Die Aetiologie der Tuberkulose", *Mittheilungen aus dem Kaiserlichen Gesundheitsamte* 2 (1884), 1–88
**What the node says:** the bacterial cause of tuberculosis was identified in 1882, with the 1884 paper following.
**What is open:** nothing
**What would settle it:** the Robert Koch Institute's digitised copy, or any history of medicine that carries the date from the primary.
**Tried:** 2026-11-01, clear-queue: Internet Archive full-text search on the title, only later citing works; Internet Archive title search, two scans of the original printings found; OCR text of the 1882 volume and the 1884 volume, Koch's article read in full in the first, the title page, contents and opening of the second; the Robert Koch Institute copy and Crossref not needed.
**Verdict:** Confirmed against scans of both original printings, read as OCR text. The 1882 paper is in *Berliner klinische Wochenschrift* 19, no. 15, Monday 10 April 1882 (Internet Archive item BerlinerKlinischeWochenschrift188219), pp. 221–230. Its heading says it follows a lecture to the Berlin Physiological Society on 24 March of that year, and the previous issue's news column reports the talk of 24 March. The 1884 paper begins on p. 1 of *Mittheilungen aus dem Kaiserlichen Gesundheitsamte*, volume 2 (Berlin: August Hirschwald, 1884; Wellcome Library scan, item s3728id1397087), with the plate key on p. 88, so 1–88 stands. Its opening sentence dates the first report to 24 March 1882 and cites no. 15 of the 1882 volume. Date, venue and pagination all stand. Two changes for the node, both optional: the 1882 paper has no citation of its own and could take "[^koch-1882]: Robert Koch, "Die Aetiologie der Tuberculose", *Berliner klinische Wochenschrift* 19, no. 15 (10 April 1882), 221–230."; and because the 1882 paper has the same title as the 1884 one, spelled "Tuberculose", "followed by "Die Aetiologie der Tuberkulose" in 1884" reads as if the titles differ, which "followed by the full report in 1884" would avoid.
