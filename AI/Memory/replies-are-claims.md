`Arguments For` holds positive cases only: arguments that start from premises a person outside the faith can grant or fight, and end at Christianity or at something Christianity needs, including the rational standing of belief. A node whose premises are Christian teaching and whose conclusion is that an objection fails is a claim. It goes in `Claims`, and the objection it answers links down to it.

**The test:** write the node as premises and a conclusion, delete every premise that is itself a Christian teaching, and look at what is left. If the argument still runs, it is an argument. If it collapses, it was a reply, and a reply is a claim.

**Why:** on 2026-09-15 seven of the eight nodes in `Arguments For` were replies. Their own Countered By sections said so ("is what this argument answers"). The vault already filed one reply as a claim (Conquest Texts Use Ancient War Hyperbole) beside another filed as an argument (God Delays Judgment to Allow Repentance), both answering God Is Brutal and Not Merciful. Objections come out argument shaped because they conclude something about Christianity. Replies come out claim shaped because they assert a doctrine or a reading that removes a premise of the objection. Set by the user on 2026-09-15.

**How to apply:**

- Six nodes moved to `Claims` that day: General Revelation Leaves People Without Excuse, God Can't Accept Sin, God Delays Judgment to Allow Repentance, Hell Is Self-Chosen Separation (was Hell as Self-Chosen Separation), Obedience Comes Before Evidence, The Covenant Changed with Christ. Personal Relationship with God was rewritten as the argument Religious Experience Is Evidence for God.
- Argument content dropped from a moved node becomes a new `Arguments For` stub with the dropped material as its `points`, then goes through `draft-node`. Two came out of the move: Belief in God Can Be Rational Without Argument (Plantinga) and Faith Can Be Rational Before the Evidence Is In (James).
- The librarian and the drafter should refuse to file a reply in `Arguments For`. The rule sits in `.agents/skills/source-node/references/templates.md` under Argument.
- A claim title is a proposition, not a phrase: Hell Is Self-Chosen Separation, not Hell as Self-Chosen Separation. See [[link-direction]] for what a moved claim may still link.
